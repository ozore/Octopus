### C-2 · A lifecycle email tells a paying customer she is saving money when the shipped pricing function says she is not

**File:** `outreach/lifecycle-emails.md` §4.5 `allowance_warning`, §4.6 `auto_upgrade_fired`

**Exact problem.** §4.5 sends: *"The overage stops at `{cap_amount}`. That is exactly the gap to
`{next_plan}`… so at the cap we move you to `{next_plan}` and stop charging overage — **because past that
point `{next_plan}` costs you less than staying here**."*

Run it against the shipped catalogue (`app/src/platform/billing/catalog.ts`: `solo includedFilings: 8`,
`crew: 40`, `multi: null`; `pricing.ts`: overage $2.50, `capCents = price(next) − price(current)`):

| | Solo | Crew |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $99 | $249 |
| Included filings | 8 | 40 |
| Overage cap | $150 (= gap to Crew) | $350 (= gap to Multi) |
| Filings at which the cap binds | 8 + 60 = **68** | 40 + 140 = **180** |
| Total at the cap | **$249** | **$599** |

At 68 filings the Solo customer has hit the cap: overage has stopped, she pays **$249**, and further filings
are free. The auto-upgrade then moves her to Crew — **$249, but with only 40 included filings and $2.50
metering above them.** At 69 filings she pays $249 + 29 × $2.50 = **$321.50** on Crew against **$249** capped
on Solo. The upgrade makes her strictly worse off, and gets worse with every filing.

§4.6 compounds it: *"On `{old_plan}` that would have been `{would_have_paid}`."* Because the cap is *defined*
as the gap to the next tier, `would_have_paid` at the moment of upgrade equals `next_price` **exactly**. The
counterfactual can only ever render a wash — unless it is computed from an uncapped overage the cap forbids,
in which case it renders a saving that was never available.

**Why it matters.** §4.6 quotes the test it has to pass and then fails it: *"the upgrade must be defensible as
cheaper for her, or it is a trap"* (`USER_JOURNEY.md` §11.4). This is a false statement about a customer's own
bill, in a transactional message, from a product whose entire positioning is that the number on your form
carries its source. It is also the one message class in the whole plan with money attached and no human to
correct it. Note the direction of the error: the ladder's own code comment reasons correctly for **Crew →
Multi** (Multi is unlimited, so the upgrade is at worst neutral) and the copy generalised that reasoning to a
step where it does not hold, because **Crew is still metered**.

**Concrete fix.** One of two, and phase 3 owns the copy either way:
(a) delete the causal clause and render the true comparison from `assessUsage()` — if `next_plan` still meters,
say so and let her choose; or
(b) escalate to the ladder owner: an auto-upgrade may only fire into a tier whose included allowance is at
least the filings already reached, otherwise the cap is the better plan and the upgrade must not be automatic.
Until one lands, §4.5's causal clause and §4.6's counterfactual line must not ship.

have. Requiring the constrained tool to out-run the unconstrained one is a test of input availability, not of
whether provenance draws. A ratio below 1.0 is the expected outcome even if provenance is exactly the draw —
so the plan is pre-registered to kill its own central hypothesis on a measurement that cannot speak to it.
Ries' point is that thresholds must be written first, not that any threshold written first is valid.

**Fix:** keep the ratio as a descriptive counter with no verdict attached, and move the kill to a
population-matched comparison already defined in `dashboard.md` §2.1 — **diff-session → account creation versus
generator-session → account creation** (`t1.tool_to_account`, split by tool). That compares conversion within
each tool's own denominator, which is what "is provenance the draw" actually asks.

### M-5 · The standing disclosure's headline sentence is true of a post and false of an email

**Files:** `outreach/community-playbook.md` §3.1, §3.2 vs. `outreach/lifecycle-emails.md` §1, §2 C1, §7

The disclosure, to be used *"unmodified wherever any of it applies"*, states in bold:
**"Nothing you write in reply is delivered to anyone."** `lifecycle-emails.md` §2 C1 states the opposite of the
email path: *"Replies land at a published address and increment G5's counter"*, and §7 attributes minutes *"by
wall-clock to first reply"* — i.e. replies are delivered, counted, **and answered**. The playbook hedges two
sentences later ("One address does reach a mailbox"), but the bolded absolute is the sentence a reader keeps,
and §3.2's one-line form carries the absolute without the hedge: *"No reply here reaches a person."*
**Fix:** scope it — *"Nothing you write in reply **to this post** reaches anyone"* — and in the one-line form,
*"Replies here reach nobody; the billing address is the only address that reaches a mailbox."*

## Verdict

**REJECT for shipping; ACCEPT as the strongest draft in the run so far.** This is not a contradiction: the
strategy is sound and unusually honest, and the specific things that would reach a customer are wrong.

What is genuinely good, and should not be diluted in the rewrite: the struck-claims register survived 35
probes over 13 files with zero hits, including guarded ones; every venue rule quoted is real and I verified
three of them verbatim; the 16 CFR 465 mapping is correct where I expected it to be wrong; the refusals are
argued from mechanism rather than from taste; the trade-show and ABC rows are left visible as the itemised
price of A1 rather than scored away; `community-playbook.md` §5 reaches the right answer on the help-forum
question and reaches it on the ethical ground rather than hiding behind the venue rules — *"we cannot label our
way out of accepting terms we will not meet"* is the best sentence in the phase; and nothing has been sent,
posted, drafted or credentialed anywhere.

**Ship-blocking:** C-1, C-2, H-2, H-3, H-4. C-2 is money, in a message, to a customer, with no human to
correct it. H-2 is live in the shipped app. H-4 is the only path by which this company can mail a stranger.

**The pattern under the findings.** Every serious defect is the same error in a different costume: **the
documents write specifications in the indicative mood.** `claims-lint` "fails the build"; `crm.dashboard.render`
"generates this file"; the auto-upgrade "costs you less"; the geometry is "right"; the listing "is free". None
of those is a lie, and all of them are unbuilt, unmeasured or unread. `CORRECTIONS.md` §0.1 installed a rule
against printing an unfetched claim; this phase needs its twin — **no sentence describes machinery in the
present tense until the machinery exists**, because a company that cannot tell a specification from a
description will eventually fail to tell a mechanism from an outcome, which is the exact failure the register
was opened to end.

**A limit of this review, stated rather than hidden.** I verified eight external assertions against primary
sources (HN guidelines, Show HN, Product Hunt commenting guidelines, 16 CFR 465 via the eCFR API, Google Ads
budget limits, Stripe pricing, LCPcertified pricing, IndexNow documentation) and re-probed one venue
(capterra.com/vendors/). That is a sample, not a census: roughly seventy external URLs are cited across the
thirteen files and sixty-odd remain unchecked by me. Two of the nine I did check did not carry what was hung
on them (H-1's free-listing claim, M-6's IndexNow roster) and one was checked precisely because it looked
wrong and turned out right (the CFR mapping). A 2-of-9 miss rate over an unbiased sample is the number to
carry forward — it does not condemn the corpus, and it does mean the unchecked sixty should not be treated as
verified merely because they are cited.
