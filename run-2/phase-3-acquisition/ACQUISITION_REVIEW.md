### C-2 · A lifecycle email tells a paying customer she is saving money when the shipped pricing function says she is not

**File:** `outreach/lifecycle-emails.md` §4.5 `allowance_warning`, §4.6 `auto_upgrade_fired`

**Exact problem.** §4.5 sends: *"The overage stops at `{cap_amount}`. That is exactly the gap to
`{next_plan}`… so at the cap we move you to `{next_plan}` and stop charging overage — **because past that
point `{next_plan}` costs you less than staying here**."*

Run it against the shipped catalogue (`app/src/platform/billing/catalog.ts`: `solo includedFilings: 8`,
`crew: 40`, `multi: null`; `pricing.ts`: overage $2.50, `capCents = price(next) − price(current)`):

| | Solo | Crew |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $99 | $249 |
| Included filings | 8 | 40 |
| Overage cap | $150 (= gap to Crew) | $350 (= gap to Multi) |
| Filings at which the cap binds | 8 + 60 = **68** | 40 + 140 = **180** |
| Total at the cap | **$249** | **$599** |

At 68 filings the Solo customer has hit the cap: overage has stopped, she pays **$249**, and further filings
are free. The auto-upgrade then moves her to Crew — **$249, but with only 40 included filings and $2.50
metering above them.** At 69 filings she pays $249 + 29 × $2.50 = **$321.50** on Crew against **$249** capped
on Solo. The upgrade makes her strictly worse off, and gets worse with every filing.

§4.6 compounds it: *"On `{old_plan}` that would have been `{would_have_paid}`."* Because the cap is *defined*
as the gap to the next tier, `would_have_paid` at the moment of upgrade equals `next_price` **exactly**. The
counterfactual can only ever render a wash — unless it is computed from an uncapped overage the cap forbids,
in which case it renders a saving that was never available.

**Why it matters.** §4.6 quotes the test it has to pass and then fails it: *"the upgrade must be defensible as
cheaper for her, or it is a trap"* (`USER_JOURNEY.md` §11.4). This is a false statement about a customer's own
bill, in a transactional message, from a product whose entire positioning is that the number on your form
carries its source. It is also the one message class in the whole plan with money attached and no human to
correct it. Note the direction of the error: the ladder's own code comment reasons correctly for **Crew →
Multi** (Multi is unlimited, so the upgrade is at worst neutral) and the copy generalised that reasoning to a
step where it does not hold, because **Crew is still metered**.

**Concrete fix.** One of two, and phase 3 owns the copy either way:
(a) delete the causal clause and render the true comparison from `assessUsage()` — if `next_plan` still meters,
say so and let her choose; or
(b) escalate to the ladder owner: an auto-upgrade may only fire into a tier whose included allowance is at
least the filings already reached, otherwise the cap is the better plan and the upgrade must not be automatic.
Until one lands, §4.5's causal clause and §4.6's counterfactual line must not ship.

so the plan is pre-registered to kill its own central hypothesis on a measurement that cannot speak to it.
Ries' point is that thresholds must be written first, not that any threshold written first is valid.

**Fix:** keep the ratio as a descriptive counter with no verdict attached, and move the kill to a
population-matched comparison already defined in `dashboard.md` §2.1 — **diff-session → account creation versus
generator-session → account creation** (`t1.tool_to_account`, split by tool). That compares conversion within
each tool's own denominator, which is what "is provenance the draw" actually asks.

### M-5 · The standing disclosure's headline sentence is true of a post and false of an email
