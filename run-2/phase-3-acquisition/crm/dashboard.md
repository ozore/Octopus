## 1. The four questions this dashboard exists to answer

1. **Is provenance the draw?** (T1 — the load-bearing assumption of the whole brand.)
2. **Can we hold a search surface nobody else holds?** (T2 — the revision history, not the rate.)
3. **Does anyone pay?** (T3 — the only instrument that returns a money answer in weeks.)
4. **Does the artifact loop have any yield at all?** (Instrumented, not planned against.)

Everything else on this page is either an input to one of those four or a gate that stops us saying
something we have not measured.

## 2. Metric definitions

Rules that apply to every row — specified for the renderer, and binding on any human who reads a number off this page in the meantime: a metric is printed with its **denominator** and its **as-of date** or
it is not printed; a missing day is recorded as missing and never interpolated; and a ratio whose
denominator is below its stated floor renders `—`, not zero.

**Why the kill moved off `t1.diff_ratio`.** The pre-registered line was *"KILL if `t1.diff_runs ÷
t1.gen_runs` < 1.0 at week 8"*, described in four files as the direct test of whether provenance is
the draw. **The two tools do not share a population.** The generator needs nothing — type a crew, or
skip it and type your own rates. The diff checker needs a determination number **and** an award date
the visitor must already hold. Requiring the constrained tool to out-run the unconstrained one
measures **input availability**, not draw: a ratio below 1.0 is the expected outcome even if
provenance is exactly the draw, so the plan was pre-registered to kill its own central hypothesis on
a measurement that cannot speak to it. Ries' point is that a threshold must be written first, not
that any threshold written first is valid. The ratio stays as a counter because it is genuinely
interesting; it no longer carries a verdict. The verdict moves to the population-matched comparison
above, which is what *"is provenance the draw"* actually asks. Recorded in `CRM.md` §10.2 and
`channels.csv` CH-11b.

> **ASSUMPTION F4 — retention, unmeasured.** The affordable-CAC ceilings this test's cap was compared
> against (**Solo $1,038 · Crew $2,603 · Multi $6,263**) are **11-month payback ceilings**, and an
> 11-month payback ceiling on a monthly subscription is a **retention claim**: it assumes the account
> survives eleven months. F1 covers conversion, F2 seasonality, F3 market size. **Nothing covers
> churn**, which is the input those ceilings depend on most. If a Solo account churns at month six, an
> $1,038 CAC is a loss of roughly **$472**. So the ceilings are bracketed rather than deleted — they
> remain ceilings *under an unmeasured survival assumption*, and they may not be borrowed to make
> another number look conservative. That borrowing is exactly what happened here: T3 sells a **$49
> one-time SKU** whose payback is instantaneous and carries no retention risk at all, and its cap was
> made to look modest by measuring it in subscription LTVs it does not earn. The cap is now stated in
> the units the test actually buys. The named blocker on T3 remains the one already recorded — no
> conversion rate has been measured — and F4 is now printed beside it rather than assumed through it.

**No row on this page may be restated as an upgrade saving.** Read against the shipped catalogue, the
auto-upgrade is **not cheaper** at the Solo→Crew step and this dashboard must not be used to imply it
is. A Solo account reaches the cap at **8 + 60 = 68 filings**, paying **$99 + $150 = $249**, after
which further filings that period cost nothing. The upgrade then moves it to Crew: **$249, but with 40
included filings and $2.50 metering above them.** At 69 filings that is **$249 + 29 × $2.50 =
$321.50** on Crew against **$249** capped on Solo, and the gap widens with every filing. The
counterfactual is a wash by construction, too: because the cap is *defined* as `price(next) −
price(current)`, the amount owed at the moment of upgrade equals the next tier's price exactly. The
honest framing, and the only one permitted anywhere downstream of this file: **the overage is capped
at the next tier's price, and the upgrade is what the cap buys.** The step that reasons correctly is
**Crew→Multi**, because Multi is unlimited and the upgrade is therefore at worst neutral; Crew is
still metered, which is why the same reasoning does not carry down a rung. Source of truth:
`app/src/platform/billing/catalog.ts` and `pricing.ts`, read 2026-08-13.

## 3. GATED — measured internally, may not be printed

**What is gated is the sentence, not the measurement** — that is the design, and it is worth separating
from what is true today.

- **Specified.** Each metric is to exist as a counter from day one; the renderer emits the mechanism
  sentence while the flag is false and the measured sentence, with its numbers and dates injected,
  when it is true; a human editing copy **will not be able to** promote any of them, because the flag
  will live in a signed `claims.json` the renderer verifies.
- **Today.** `claims.json` does not exist, no signing key has been issued, and neither `CL-1` nor
  `CL-2` is implemented — so **a human editing copy can promote any of these sentences right now**,
  and nothing but review would catch it. The gate is procedural, not structural, and the difference is
  the one `CORRECTIONS.md` §5 exists to keep visible.
- **The exception, stated because it is the only one.** The G2 row's mechanism sentence is checkable
  today: eCPR schema validation is **built** at `app/src/artifacts/ecpr/validate.ts` against the
  published `CPR.xsd` in the same directory, kept fresh by the `ingest.dir.xsd` job that **is** in the
  registry. That sentence describes machinery that exists. The other five do not, and the difference
  is now printed rather than averaged away.

| Metric | Gate | Unlocks when | Printable **today** | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Golden-suite exact-match rate | **G1** / F-1 | ≥500-line suite over ≥25 determinations in ≥8 states, 100% exact match, **30 consecutive green days** | "All money arithmetic is deterministic code under property tests. No model touches it. A divergence blocks both corpus promotion and the build." | **GATED** |
| Acceptance count | **G2** / F-2 | ≥50 WH-347s **and** ≥25 CA eCPR files confirmed accepted, XSD hash green throughout | "Generated to the DOL form geometry; the XML validates against the published schema. **Generated, not acceptance-tested.**" | **GATED** |
| Corpus completeness | **G3** / F-3 | 60 days of zero unexplained reconciliation delta above 0.5% | "N active determinations mirrored as of *(timestamp)*. Reconciliation delta: x%. Last successful crawl: *(timestamp)*." — the live status page | **GATED** (mechanism sentence is live) |
| `csv_to_artifact_seconds` median | **G4** / F-4 | ≥100 real filings | "Ratepin reads a payroll CSV and writes the WH-347 and the eCPR XML. There is no queue and no turnaround window." | **GATED** |
| `human_minutes` per customer per month | **G5** / F-4 | 90 days below 2 min/customer/month at ≥50 paying accounts. The counter increments on **every** inbound message to the billing address, with minutes by wall-clock to first reply — the party that benefits does not define the denominator | "There is no support queue. No human reviews any output, at any tier. Refunds are a button." | **GATED** |
| Staleness auto-credit | **G6** / F-4 | The chaos test passes at both scales, upstream killed in staging | *(silence — the mechanism may be built and may fire; it may not be marketed)* | **GATED** |

**Two dashboard-specific gate rules.** No acquisition metric on this page may be restated as a
customer-facing outcome, in any form — a conversion rate is not a claim about the product, and a
channel result is not evidence of correctness. That rule binds a human writer today and needs no
machinery. The second rule does need machinery and does not have it: `claims.json` arriving unsigned,
stale or unverifiable **is to render** every row of this section as GATED, fail-closed, per A5 — and
since the file does not exist at all, the fail-closed state is the only state that has ever obtained.
Which is the correct state, by accident rather than by design, and accident is not a control.

## 4. Metrics we refuse to compute, and why

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| Pageviews, sessions or impressions as a success metric | They measure supply of pages, not demand for the product. T2's success metric is indexation and downstream tool starts |
| MQLs, SQLs, leads, pipeline, contact counts | There are no person records here (`CRM.md` §1). Inventing a lead stage would be a lie about the mechanism |
| A viral coefficient for the artifact loop | Weinberg & Mares require a measurable *k* with a cycle time. We have neither, and a *k* computed from a footer that is often printed or faxed would be unfalsifiable and flattering at once |
| Time-saved or money-saved figures of any kind | F-4. Even a correctly-read external burden estimate would say nothing about Ratepin, because nobody has measured Ratepin |
| Any characterisation or count of the open-shop 5–75-employee segment | `research/01-demand-pmf.md` found no source that sizes it. `BRAND.md` C-B5 forbids printing a segment size no source supports |
| Enforcement-frequency series as a demand indicator | DBRA concluded compliance actions fell from 1,711 in FY2013 to 641 in FY2025 (WHD fiscal-year data, read 2026-08-13). The buying trigger we actually observe is the GC withholding the progress draw, which happens weekly regardless |
| NPS or any survey | It is a message to a person about a product whose entire premise is that no message is required, and no decision on this page turns on it |

## 5. How a number is **to** reach a public surface — the specified path, and what exists of it

One path, and it does not run through this file. **None of the five steps is implemented.** They are
written below as the specification they are, each with the artefact whose existence makes it real —
because a precondition you cannot point at a file for is not a precondition, and a Day-0 gate written
as five assertions can only ever be passed by assertion.

| # | Specified step | The artefact that makes it checkable | Exists? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A measurement job writes the value and its flag into `claims.json` and **signs** it | a `claims.json` at a declared path, plus a public verification key committed to the repo and a job kind in `app/src/worker/jobs.ts` that writes it | **No.** `find run-2 -name claims.json` → nothing; no key; no job |
| 2 | The renderer verifies the signature and `generated_at` freshness before rendering any measured sentence; an unverifiable file renders the mechanism sentence in every slot | a **route test** asserting that a tampered or stale `claims.json` renders the mechanism sentence and not the measured one | **No** |
| 3 | `CL-1` fails the build if a measured-claim template string appears outside the renderer | a **CI job id** that runs `claims-lint`, and a red build on a seeded violation | **No.** `claims-lint` exists only as prose in `CORRECTIONS.md` and `GTM_PLAYBOOK.md` |
| 4 | `CL-2` fails the build for any number on a Scope A surface that does not resolve to `BRAND.md` §5.5 or `claims.json` with an as-of date | `corrections.probes.json` **extracted from the register and parsing**, plus the same CI job id | **No** |
| 5 | A regression flips the flag on the next measurement run and the live sentence narrows within one job interval — P-C, executed by the scheduler, not by anyone noticing | a scheduled job kind plus a test that narrows a sentence when a flag flips | **No** |

Two consequences, stated rather than left to be discovered:

- **The gate that depends on this cannot currently be evaluated.** Any Day-0 precondition phrased as
  *"`claims-lint` returns zero blocking matches"* or *"`claims.json` is signed and fresh"* is not a
  test today; it is a sentence. Replacing each with the artefact in column three is what makes the
  gate evaluable, and the four "No"s above are the honest current score.
- **Step 4 has a conflict waiting for it.** `CL-2` as written would fail this very file on the day it
  is built: `1,711 → 641`, `5,188`, `9,424`, `3.35`, `14,195`, `122,936`, `$46.98` are all correct,
  dated and sourced, and none of them is in `BRAND.md` §5.5's admitted list. That is the register
  owner's decision — split Scope A, or extend §5.5 with a *measured from our own corpus, with method
  and date* class — and it is raised here rather than quietly worked around.

**This dashboard is a read model** — or rather, it is the specification of one. Copying a figure from
it onto a page is meant to be a red build; today it would be a quiet mistake, which is precisely why
the rule is printed at the top of the page as well as here.
