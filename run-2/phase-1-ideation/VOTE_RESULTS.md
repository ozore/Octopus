## The ballot

| Candidate | One-liner |
|---|---|
| **Wage Line** | Pins every federally funded job to the exact wage-determination revision in force, then generates the week's WH-347 and state e-CPR XML from a payroll CSV — so the rate on the certified payroll is the rate the auditor will look up. |
| **Sunset Graph** | Joins every vendor's dated deprecation and breaking-change record to the exact call sites in your repos, and ships the countdown plus a test-gated migration PR before the sunset. |
| **Ship Record** | Turns your per-release SBOM and your vendor list into the dated evidence artifacts procurement and regulators actually ask for — attribution NOTICE, licence-change and subprocessor alerts, and the CRA 24-hour exploited-vulnerability clock. |
| **Portfolio Guard** | Read-only into every client account an agency runs — ad accounts, app listings, WordPress sites, sending domains — mapping each platform's dated published rule change onto the specific entities in each client it will break. |
| **Rate Card** | Recomputes the bills your vendors send against the rate cards those same vendors publish — parcel, card processing, delivery-platform payouts — on a flat fee, so you keep 100% of anything you recover. |
| **Landed** | Classifies your catalog to 10-digit HTS with the CBP rulings behind each code, prices the full stacked duty, and — the part nobody covers — models the retroactive AD/CVD liability that lands eighteen months after a zero cash deposit. |
| **Redlist** | Watches the two enforcement systems that stop consumer goods at the door — California Prop 65 60-day notices and FDA import alert red lists — against your own catalog and supplier list, and shows the wave 30–90 days before it reaches your SKU. |
| **Allowed Amount** | Joins the reimbursement benchmarks payers and CMS are legally required to publish — MRF negotiated rates, weekly coverage and NCCI edits, weekly NADAC — to a provider's own NPI and code mix, and alerts the month one of them moves. |

## Rejected by the autonomy gate before voting

| Idea | Gate failed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tierline | A4 | The core asset is a hand-authored 50-state EPCRA Tier II requirements matrix — submission platform, accepted file format, state-specific fields, fees, deadlines, state ID format rules (Florida wants pounds not range codes; Utah wants UT001234; Colorado has its own State Field tab) — and the idea's own text concedes EPA's state-requirements page confirms no consolidated database or standardised format exists. Content-hash crawling 50+ SERC pages detects THAT a page changed; it cannot re-derive the new fee, field or format rule, which is an interpretive extraction from prose. The declared fail-closed state proves the point: the product flips the state to 'verify before filing', suppresses the auto-generated cover letter, and offers a refund — i.e. it hands the judgement back to the customer on the very artifact they paid for. That is a knowledge base maintained by a person, not one that refreshes itself from machine-readable sources. The threshold arithmetic (TPQ/EHS lists via eCFR, CAS matching) genuinely is self-maintaining, but it is the free tier, not the paid deliverable. |
| Trustline | A4 | Same defect as Tierline. The declared moat is a 50-state trust/escrow rules matrix (reconciliation frequency, retention period, permitted broker funds such as Arizona's $5,000 ceiling, interest handling, required worksheet elements, signature and dating rules, separate-ledger rules) scraped from state real estate commission pages, plus a mined disciplinary-order corpus — 'this matrix does not exist anywhere as a machine-readable resource; it is the whole moat.' The stated refresh includes 'quarterly full re-verification of the rules matrix', which with no machine-readable source is human work on a recurring schedule, and the differentiator is precisely the layer that cannot self-maintain. The deterministic three-way reconciliation engine underneath does pass the gate, but with citations suppressed on stale rows it collapses to a reconciliation feature that AppFolio, Buildium and Rent Manager already bundle — so what survives autonomously is not the product. |
| NexusPilot | A4 | Self-admitted in the candidate's own risk section: 'The weakest A4 in this set: the threshold table is extracted from human-readable state pages, and while the diff-and-flag mechanism makes staleness detectable, the extraction itself still has to be right and there is no authoritative machine-readable feed for all 46 jurisdictions.' Detecting that a state DOR page changed is not the same as re-deriving the new threshold, measurement period (calendar year versus trailing twelve months differ by state) and marketplace-facilitator treatment — and the product's whole claim is a dated legal assertion ('your Illinois sales crossed $100,000 on 2026-07-14'). The failure mode is a wrong date on a registration obligation. Aggravating rather than causal: Shopify ships a free built-in nexus tracker, so the substitute is already in the buyer's admin. |
| Tariffgrid | A4 | The primary corpus, OpenEI's URDB, is a free API but is itself 'collected and quality-controlled continuously by Illinois State University' — the freshness of the knowledge base is a third party's human curation programme, not a machine-readable publication cadence we can monitor. The idea's own mitigation makes the dependency explicit: 'URDB coverage and accuracy vary by utility - mitigated by PUC-sourced verification for the top ~100 IOUs', i.e. a standing human verification effort across a hundred utilities, plus a monthly crawl of PUC tariff-filing dockets per state to extract eligibility clauses ('you may not switch back for 12 months', kW/kWh thresholds) that live in prose inside thousands of per-utility tariff PDFs. Because the paid output is a ranked recommendation to change rate schedules, a stale or mis-extracted eligibility clause produces a switch the customer cannot make — and the honest degrade (mark the tariff 'not modelable' and exclude it) shrinks the deliverable rather than preserving it. |

## Judge rationales

### Lens: Autonomy integrity — can this actually run with zero human minutes on the unhappy paths (ambiguous input, stale corpus, angry customer, refund/chargeback, legal exposure), judged against 12-Factor App and Karpathy on LLM systems

Ranking: Sunset Graph > Rate Card > Ship Record > Wage Line > Portfolio Guard > Landed > Allowed Amount > Redlist

Judged on whether autonomy survives the unhappy path, not on how many fail-closed sentences a pitch contains.

Sunset Graph leads because it exports verification: the customer's own CI adjudicates every codemod, so Karpathy's generation-verification loop closes without an operator, and the corpus is entirely versioned machine artifacts (SDL, OpenAPI, endoflife.date — 83KB JSON, verified live). A bad output is a closed PR, not a fine. Rate Card is the purest 12-Factor build — no LLM in the money path, a golden-canary invoice gating deploys (dev/prod parity), Stripe auto-credit on Partial runs — docked because card-statement geometry is a standing parsing tax and a null $149 teardown is a refund conversation. Ship Record designs the money unhappy path end to end (UNWATCHED banner plus auto-credit), though subprocessor pages behind bot walls quietly shrink coverage. Wage Line's arithmetic is the most deterministic here and its FAR 22.404-6 refusal the pool's most honest boundary, but failing closed against an undocumented SAM endpoint on a Friday statutory deadline manufactures precisely the angry customer for whom it declares no support path. Portfolio Guard's clause→observable-fact join is permanent human editorial work, and Apple/Google enforcement is discretionary, not a function of a published page. Landed's sources really are APIs (HTS reststop returned JSON), yet its headline liability number hinges on a future Commerce decision. Allowed Amount carries terabyte per-payer ingest against CRS-documented bad data — engineering minutes forever. Redlist last: both corpora are HTML-only (verified — no API; FDA 302s), and its crown jewel is LLM extraction of ppm limits from legal PDFs.

### Lens: PMF evidence: Mom Test (real money/behaviour beats stated interest), Ellis 40% rule, Andreessen market pull — ranked by strength of proof that money already changes hands for a worse solution to the same job.

Ranking: Wage Line > Rate Card > Ship Record > Portfolio Guard > Redlist > Allowed Amount > Landed > Sunset Graph

Mom Test evidence means a named vendor cashing a named check for a worse version of the same job. Wage Line wins outright: contractors pay LCPtracker/eBacon $175–$1,200/mo plus $995–$4,995 setup — nobody pays four-figure onboarding out of curiosity — and the substitute is ~$19,500/yr of clerical labour already funded. Andreessen pull is structural: the GC will not release payment without a defensible WH-347. Rate Card is second on cash velocity: LateShipment already takes 25–35% of recoveries, the purest revealed preference here. Discount it, though — contingency converts because nothing leaves the buyer's pocket, and Swipesum/FeeQuery giving statement analysis away free prices the diagnostic at zero; the beachhead is deliberately below where Sifted or Shipware quote, so pull is proven one tier up, not in-segment. Ship Record third: FOSSA at ~$207 and sbomify at $159 are money moving for attribution today, but the CRA half is a September 2026 deadline, and anticipated compliance is stated interest until the first fine lands. Portfolio Guard's Opteo/Optmyzr/WP Remote comparables prove agencies buy software, not that anyone buys policy monitoring — today's incumbent is a free Google email. Redlist's $10k–$90k settlements flow to plaintiffs' counsel, and it concedes purchase is trigger-driven; Ellis's 40% needs habitual use. Allowed Amount and Landed both confess non-purchase in their own copy — pharmacies "never executed the appeal," importers "reserve nothing" — and Landed skips the crowded paid end for the unpaid one. Sunset Graph rests on HN comments against free Dependabot: pain stated, never purchased.

### Lens: speed-to-revenue-selfserve — Ries lean MVP with the concierge shortcut banned: how fast can a fully automated v1 be built and charge a real credit card, judged on ingestion friction, pre-revenue corpus burden, determinism of the output, external human gates, and whether the buyer purchases without a call.

Ranking: Rate Card > Ship Record > Wage Line > Redlist > Portfolio Guard > Sunset Graph > Landed > Allowed Amount

Ries's MVP test here is build-time to a charged card, so I discount every Helmer moat that must be assembled before v1 emits anything. Rate Card wins: ingestion is a file the customer already owns (UPS Billing Data export) — no OAuth, no API approval, no scraping exposure — the recompute is arithmetic with no LLM in the money path, and the pure error findings (address correction $25.50, DIM, residential misclass) compute without contract rates, so the $149 teardown ships before the effective-dated rulebook is finished. Dunford's flat-fee-not-contingency line removes a buying objection rather than adding a concept. Ship Record is second: SPDX license-list-data and KEV/OSV are free and structured, the CRA 11 September clock is four weeks out, and FOSSA/sbomify bracket a proven self-serve price. Wage Line's WH-347 math and PDF are bounded, but eCPR XSD conformance is untestable without a DIR account and the $49 rate card is free public data reformatted. Redlist's $199 category report needs only the AG notice mirror, but both corpora are HTML scrapes with no API and settlement-PDF ppm extraction is a research project. Portfolio Guard is gated on Google Ads developer-token approval — a human external dependency that breaks the automated premise — atop a hand-authored clause-to-fact join. Sunset Graph needs four language surface-diff toolchains and sandboxed test-gated codemods before Moore's beachhead pays. Landed's fast half, classification, is commoditized by Zonos/3CE; its wedge, the AD/CVD rate graph, is the slow half. Allowed Amount is last: terabyte MRF ingest plus healthcare's slowest buyers, even at Ramanujam-friendly $149.

### Lens: Willingness to pay and monetization clarity — Ramanujam (WTP-first, value metric, packaging, price ceiling), Hormozi (value equation, risk reversal), Poyar/OpenView (value-metric alignment, expansion/NDR, self-serve price bands). Ranked by price ceiling defensibility for a no-human product.

Ranking: Wage Line > Landed > Allowed Amount > Portfolio Guard > Ship Record > Rate Card > Redlist > Sunset Graph

Ramanujam's WTP-first test: is the budget already forced, and does a reference price exist? Wage Line leads — WH-347 filing is legally mandatory weekly, DBA penalties run $28,619/violation plus FCA trebling, and it emits the filed artifact (PDF plus XSD-validated eCPR XML), which is what carries Hormozi's perceived-likelihood term; the per-report metric is market-validated. Skeptical hit: I verified CertifiedPayrollPro already sells self-serve at $49–$249/mo plus $1–5/report with no setup fee, so "zero setup fee is the wedge" is occupied — the premium must come from the WD revision-history corner, not from price. Landed second: I found no self-serve AD/CVD comparable (Descartes-class is quote-only), so $899 against six-figure retroactive liquidation sets its own anchor, with the >20%-unresolved auto-refund as genuine Hormozi risk reversal. Caveat — no reference price also means unproven budget. Allowed Amount and Portfolio Guard own the best Poyar value metrics (NPIs aggregated via RCM resellers; accounts × managed spend), yet both self-cap: $149–$399 chosen explicitly to dodge physician sign-off, and I confirmed Opteo at $129/$249/$499 for 10/25/75 accounts — the market has already priced that exact metric low. Ship Record splits three jobs across no single metric against FOSSA's $207 and free SBOM tooling, then hits an urgency trough between Sept 2026 and Dec 2027. Rate Card has the clearest ROI and worst ceiling: LateShipment charges 25–35% contingency — $0 upfront — so flat $149 fights a zero-risk alternative while forfeiting all upside. Redlist confesses trigger-driven churn. Sunset Graph substitutes $700/engineer-day labour against free Dependabot for the pool's poorest buyers.

### Lens: Moat and retention: compounding, time-locked or usage-fed data advantage (Helmer 7 Powers, Thiel proprietary tech) combined with habitual, high-frequency use and automatic expansion (Vohra Superhuman PMF, Poyar frequency/NRR). One-off transactional jobs rank low.

Ranking: Wage Line > Allowed Amount > Portfolio Guard > Sunset Graph > Ship Record > Landed > Redlist > Rate Card

Wage Line wins both axes. A legally compelled weekly WH-347 is the highest-frequency habit here (Poyar), and the payroll-title→SOC→WD-class crosswalk is the pool's only asset compounding from customer corrections rather than crawling — real Thiel proprietary tech atop a WD revision history SAM.gov won't bulk-export (Helmer cornered resource). Vohra's 40% test passes trivially when Friday's filing gates payment. Allowed Amount: payers overwrite MRFs and CMS ships snapshots, not changelogs, so the longitudinal series is genuinely unreconstructable; terabyte ingest is a scale economy; Turquoise's cannibalization fear is real counter-positioning. But alerting is ambient, not obligatory, and RCM resale, not habit, supplies NRR. Portfolio Guard has the best NRR mechanic — accounts and spend metered from the API — and a buyer living in those accounts daily; yet policy pages are cheap to crawl, only diff history is locked, and Opteo/Optmyzr sit adjacent. Sunset Graph is the most workflow-embedded (push webhooks, PRs) and surface-diffing is genuine proprietary tech, but npm/PyPI artifacts are immutable, so a later entrant re-derives that half retroactively; "cannot backfill" is half-true, and Dependabot is free. Ship Record's per-release SBOMs and LICENSE history are rebuildable from git, and CRA demand is deadline-shaped — latent after September. Landed's AD/CVD graph is strong assembly, but its recurring event is an annual review anniversary, and it concedes classification, the sticky part. Redlist names its own defect — trigger-driven purchase, hostile to renewal — despite the best cornered resource. Rate Card last: a $149 one-shot whose value self-terminates once the leak is fixed; flat-fee positioning is copyable in a week.

### Lens: Distribution without a salesperson — strength of compounding self-serve channels (Weinberg & Mares Bullseye, Hormozi Core Four minus human selling): programmatic SEO, app stores/marketplaces, communities, integrations, free tools

Ranking: Sunset Graph > Redlist > Ship Record > Wage Line > Landed > Portfolio Guard > Rate Card > Allowed Amount

Bullseye (Weinberg & Mares) rewards breadth then concentration; Hormozi's Core Four minus human selling leaves cold content, free tools, marketplaces, integrations. Sunset Graph alone hits four: GitHub Marketplace, the GitHub App as channel-and-integration, HN/Shopify-Partner communities that adopt dev tools without a call, and a free single-repo scan whose output — a draft PR — every engineer in the repo sees, the pool's only in-org viral loop. Discount its SEO; endoflife.date already owns those queries, free. Redlist has the deepest programmatic asset: tens of thousands of notice, defendant, plaintiff-firm and import-alert pages over a corpus with no API or export, matched to the panic query — your own company name, the day the letter lands. Second only because it is single-channel and itself admits trigger-driven purchase. Ship Record stacks three link-earning free tools, but the CRA hook decays past 11 Sept 2026 and the subprocessor-feed reader isn't reliably the buyer. Wage Line has the best free-tool-to-product intent match (WH-347 generator) plus a county×craft long tail — but davisbaconrates.com sits in its own evidence list, there's no marketplace or integration, and it leans on SAM.gov cold lists into a demo-and-quote buyer. Landed's Shopify listing and HTS pages feed classification, the commodity it concedes isn't the wedge, while AD/CVD importers arrive via broker BD. Portfolio Guard's digest compounds across only a few hundred policy pages. Rate Card owns one calculator on SERPs saturated by contingency lead-gen. Allowed Amount last: AMA licensing bars publishing the CPT descriptors its SEO needs, no store or integration exists, and its named route is reselling through billing companies — partnership selling by a human.
