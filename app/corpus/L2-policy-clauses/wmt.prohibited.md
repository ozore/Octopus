## clause: wmt.prohibited#legal-compliance
heading: Products must comply with applicable law
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [WMT.OPS.PROHIBITED]
status: active
excerpt: "All products that are listed for sale on Walmart.com must comply with any applicable local, state, and federal laws and regulations."

Every product listed for sale on Walmart.com must comply with any applicable local, state and federal laws and regulations.

The obligation is stated at the level of the individual listing, which is what makes a catalogue-wide audit — rather than a fix to the flagged item — the credible corrective action for this code.

## clause: wmt.prohibited#safety-documentation
heading: Product safety documentation
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [WMT.OPS.PROHIBITED]
status: active
excerpt: "You must provide product safety documentation for children's and other regulated consumer products"

Sellers must provide product safety documentation for children's and other regulated consumer products, so that listed items comply with applicable safety regulations and with Walmart's own product safety standards.

Two standards are named, not one: the applicable regulation and Walmart's standard. Documentation that satisfies the regulator may still be short of what Walmart asks for, and an appeal that assumes the two are the same has not addressed the second.

## clause: wmt.prohibited#seller-responsibility
heading: Seller responsibility for policy awareness
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [WMT.OPS.PROHIBITED]
status: active
excerpt: "All Walmart Marketplace sellers are responsible for being aware of and compliant with all policies, rules and guidelines."

Sellers are responsible for being aware of, and compliant with, all policies, rules and guidelines.

This sentence recurs across Walmart's policy set, and it forecloses the most common instinctive root cause — that the seller did not know. Under this clause not knowing is the violation, so the root-cause section has to explain the process gap that allowed the listing, not the knowledge gap.

## clause: wmt.prohibited#inventory-custody
heading: Prohibited products in Walmart's custody
obligation_type: standard
reason_codes: [WMT.OPS.PROHIBITED]
status: active
excerpt: "If we don't receive a response from you within five days, Walmart or Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) may return, destroy, dispose"

Where prohibited items are in Walmart's custody, Walmart retains authority to manage that inventory — when mandated by law enforcement or regulatory agencies, or as it deems necessary at its sole discretion.

The seller-facing deadline is the operative fact: if Walmart receives no response within five days, it or Walmart Fulfillment Services may return, destroy, dispose of or otherwise manage the prohibited products as it finds suitable, at its sole discretion, with any expenses charged to the seller.

Five days is short enough that this clause changes the order of work: responding about the inventory comes before drafting the appeal, not after it.

## clause: wmt.prohibited#consequences
heading: Consequences of a prohibited-products violation
obligation_type: standard
reason_codes: [WMT.OPS.PROHIBITED]
status: active
excerpt: "Walmart will remove any product listing that may be incompatible with its brand or that violates law, this policy"

Walmart states it will remove any product listing that may be incompatible with its brand, or that violates the law, this policy, or the Walmart Marketplace Seller Retailer Policies, and that corrective actions include suspending or terminating the Marketplace account.

"May be incompatible with its brand" is a discretionary standard sitting alongside the legal one, and it is not appealable on legal grounds. Where a notice rests on it, the argument is about fit, not compliance.
