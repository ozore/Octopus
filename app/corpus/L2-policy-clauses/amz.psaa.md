## clause: amz.psaa#scope-and-consequences
heading: Scope of the policy and consequences of non-compliance
obligation_type: standard
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SECTION3]
status: active
excerpt: "can result in cancellation of listings, suspension from use of Amazon tools and reports, or the removal of selling privileges"

This policy sets out the activities Amazon treats as prohibited for sellers of both products and services, and it states its own enforcement range up front: a breach can cost you your listings, your access to Amazon's seller tools and reports, or your selling privileges entirely.

The policy is explicitly additive. It says it is in addition to, and does not limit, a seller's other obligations under the seller agreement. That matters for an appeal: answering this policy does not answer the agreement, and a notice that cites a deactivation ground outside this document is not addressed by pointing at this document.

## clause: amz.psaa#divert-transactions
heading: Attempts to divert transactions or buyers from Amazon channels
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.DIVERSION]
status: active
excerpt: "any attempt to circumvent the established Amazon sales process or to divert Amazon users to another website or sales process is prohibited"

Amazon prohibits any attempt to move a transaction or a buyer off Amazon's own sales process. The prohibition is written broadly: it covers advertisements, marketing messages and special offers, and any "call to action" that leads, prompts or encourages an Amazon user to leave the Amazon website.

Two concrete mechanisms are named. The first is email used to divert customers away from the Amazon sales process. The second is the inclusion of hyperlinks, URLs or web addresses inside seller-generated order-confirmation messages or inside product and listing description fields, where they are intended to divert customers away from that process.

Most enforcement under this clause is unintentional: a packaging insert carrying a website, a warranty-registration card, or a support-email footer with a company URL. Nothing in the clause requires an intent to divert for the conduct to be prohibited — the named prohibition attaches to the artefact, not to the seller's motive.

## clause: amz.psaa#improper-business-name
heading: Unauthorized and improper business names
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.BIZ_NAME]
status: active
excerpt: "must be a name that: accurately identifies you; is not misleading; and that you have the right to use"

Your business name is the string identifying your business entity on Amazon, and the policy imposes three cumulative tests on it: it must accurately identify you, it must not be misleading, and you must have the right to use it — which the policy spells out as not including, or otherwise infringing, anyone else's trademark or other intellectual property right.

There is also a mechanical rule that is easy to trip without noticing: a business name may not contain an email suffix such as .com, .net or .biz. A name that passes the three tests can still fail on this alone.

## clause: amz.psaa#inappropriate-email
heading: Inappropriate email communications
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.DIVERSION]
status: active
excerpt: "emails related to marketing communications of any kind are prohibited unless carried out in compliance with applicable privacy law"

Unsolicited email to Amazon customers is prohibited except where it is necessary for order fulfilment and related customer service. Marketing communications of any kind are prohibited unless they are carried out in compliance with applicable privacy law, including obtaining the customer's consent.

The practical shape of this clause is that the exception is narrow and the burden of establishing it sits with the seller. "The customer bought from us, so we emailed them" is not the exception; order fulfilment and customer service are.

## clause: amz.psaa#direct-email-addresses
heading: Direct email addresses
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.DIVERSION]
status: active
excerpt: "Sellers are prohibited from providing or soliciting direct, non-Amazon-generated email addresses on the Amazon website or in correspondence"

Buyer–Seller Messaging assigns Amazon-generated addresses to both parties. Sellers are prohibited from providing or soliciting direct, non-Amazon-generated email addresses, either on the Amazon website or in correspondence sent through the Buyer–Seller Messaging Service.

Both directions are prohibited. Giving out your own address and asking the buyer for theirs are each independently caught, which is why a signature block is a common and entirely accidental route into this clause.

## clause: amz.psaa#customer-phone-numbers
heading: Appropriate treatment of customer phone numbers
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.DIVERSION]
status: active
excerpt: "Never contact a customer using their phone number, unless you obtain consent and comply with applicable privacy law"

Amazon gives professional self-fulfilling sellers access to customer phone numbers so they can meet carrier label requirements, and then restricts what may be done with them. Permitted uses are narrow: print them on labels to satisfy carrier requirements, dispose of retained numbers once orders are processed, and control who inside your organisation can see them.

The prohibitions are stated as absolutes: never contact a customer by phone unless you have obtained consent and comply with applicable privacy law, never share customer phone information with an external party, and never pass along customer information of any kind outside Buyer–Seller Messaging on the same condition.

## clause: amz.psaa#infringement-notices-as-agent
heading: Filing infringement notices as an agent or brand protection agency
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SELLER_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "Any person filing notices as an agent that benefit their own status as a seller may have their selling account terminated"

Amazon accepts intellectual-property infringement notices from authorised brand-protection agents. What it does not permit is an individual with an active selling account filing those notices as a brand's agent where the filing could benefit their own selling account — the example given is the removal of competing listings.

The stated consequence is termination of the selling account, and it attaches to the person filing, not to the brand on whose behalf they filed.

## clause: amz.psaa#misuse-case-management
heading: Misuse of Amazon Case Management or threatening Amazon employees
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SELLER_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "It's prohibited to abuse this functionality."

Sellers may use Selling Partner Support for questions and problems, and may rate the quality of the support they receive. Abusing that functionality is prohibited, and the policy states an expectation of professional communication and fair ratings toward Amazon's customers and employees.

This clause is short and is often the unstated background to a deactivation that a seller experiences as arbitrary: a support thread that escalated in tone is, under this policy, conduct — not merely a frustrating exchange.

## clause: amz.psaa#misuse-selling-service
heading: Misuse of the Amazon selling service
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SELLER_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "If you misuse or make excessive or unreasonable use of the Amazon selling service, Amazon might, in its sole discretion, restrict or block your access"

The policy frames excessive use as a fairness problem between sellers: repeatedly uploading excessive amounts of data, or otherwise using the service in an excessive or unreasonable way, creates a disproportionate load and impairs other sellers' access.

The stated remedy is targeted rather than terminal — Amazon may restrict or block access to the product feeds or the specific function being misused, in its sole discretion, until the misuse stops.

## clause: amz.psaa#ratings-feedback-reviews
heading: Misuse of ratings, feedback, or reviews
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.REVIEW_MANIP]
status: active
excerpt: "Any attempt to manipulate ratings, feedback, or reviews is prohibited."

The blanket rule is that any attempt to manipulate ratings, feedback or reviews is prohibited. The policy then makes the general rule concrete on both surfaces.

On ratings and feedback: you may not post abusive or inappropriate feedback, include personal information about a transaction partner, or post ratings or feedback to your own account. You may request feedback from a buyer, but you may not pay for it or offer any incentive for providing or removing it, and posting ratings or feedback by spoofing or for commercial purposes is prohibited outright.

On reviews: you may not write reviews for products or services you have a financial interest in — including those you or your competitors sell — you may not provide compensation for a review, including free or discounted products, you may not solicit only positive reviews or offer compensation for them, and you may not ask a buyer to modify or remove a review.

## clause: amz.psaa#sales-rank
heading: Misuse of sales rank
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.RANK_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "Any attempt to manipulate sales rank is prohibited."

Best-seller rank exists so buyers can judge a product's popularity, so any attempt to manipulate it is prohibited. Three specific behaviours are named: soliciting or knowingly accepting non-authentic or fraudulent orders (including ordering your own products), compensating buyers for purchasing your products or giving them claim codes in order to inflate rank, and making claims about a product's best-seller rank in the detail-page content, including the title and description.

That last one catches sellers who never manipulated anything: a "#1 Best Seller" phrase written into a title is a violation of this clause on its own terms.

## clause: amz.psaa#inaccurate-product-matching
heading: Matching product offerings inaccurately
obligation_type: requirement
reason_codes: [AMZ.AUTH.CONDITION]
status: active
excerpt: "the product being offered must be listed on a product detail page that accurately describes the product in all respects"

When listing against an existing detail page, the product offered must be listed on a page that accurately describes it in all respects. The policy enumerates the attributes it has in mind — manufacturer, edition, binding, version, format and player compatibility — and states the list is not exhaustive.

It also draws a hard line on identifiers: a seller may not match their item to a detail page carrying a different ISBN, UPC, EAN or other external identifier. This is the clause that most often sits underneath a complaint a seller experiences as being about condition or authenticity, because a mismatched page makes an accurate item look inaccurate to the buyer.

## clause: amz.psaa#duplicate-detail-pages
heading: Creating duplicate product detail pages and separate listings
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SEARCH_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "Creating a product detail page for a product already in the Amazon catalog is prohibited."

Creating a detail page for a product already in the Amazon catalogue is prohibited. Separately, sellers may not create separate listings for identical copies of the same item: multiple copies belong in the quantity field, and a separate listing is only appropriate where the same item is genuinely offered in a different condition.

The policy gives its own reason — duplicate listings are confusing for buyers and frustrating for other sellers — which is worth mirroring in an appeal, because it identifies the harm Amazon believes it is preventing.

## clause: amz.psaa#search-and-browse
heading: Misuse of Search and Browse
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.SEARCH_ABUSE]
status: active
excerpt: "Any attempt to manipulate the Search and Browse experience is therefore prohibited."

The stated premise is buyer expectation: customers using Amazon's search engine and browse structure expect relevant and accurate results, so any attempt to manipulate that experience is prohibited.

Three non-exhaustive examples are given: artificially simulating customer traffic, whether through bots or by paying for clicks on organic search results; providing misleading or irrelevant catalogue information across title, bullet points, description, variations and keywords; and adding product identifiers such as brand names, product names or ASINs to hidden keyword attributes — explicitly including your own.

## clause: amz.psaa#a-to-z-misuse
heading: Misuse of the Amazon A-to-z Guarantee
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.PERF.ODR]
status: active
excerpt: "Sellers who have an excessive number or monetary amount of A-to-z Guarantee claims are subject to termination."

Misuse of the A-to-z Guarantee claims process is prohibited, and — separately from misuse — sellers carrying an excessive number or monetary amount of claims are stated to be subject to termination. Amazon also reserves the right to seek reimbursement from the seller where it has reimbursed a buyer under the Guarantee.

This is the clause that connects a metrics deactivation to a policy document. A-to-z claims are one of the three defect types feeding the Order Defect Rate, so a seller reading an ODR notice is usually looking at the downstream effect of this clause.

## clause: amz.psaa#post-transaction-price
heading: Post-transaction price manipulation and excessive shipping fees
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.COC.FRAUD]
status: active
excerpt: "Any attempt to increase the sale price of an item after a transaction has been completed is prohibited."

Increasing the sale price of an item after the transaction has completed is prohibited, and setting excessive order-fulfilment and shipping costs is prohibited alongside it.

The second half has no numeric threshold in the policy text. "Excessive" is left to Amazon's judgment, which means an appeal on this clause argues about the reasonableness of a specific fee against a specific cost base, not against a published limit.

## clause: amz.psaa#listing-prohibited-products
heading: Listing prohibited products
obligation_type: prohibition
reason_codes: [AMZ.SAFETY.RESTRICTED]
status: active
excerpt: "Parts or products from whale, dolphin, porpoise, shark, elephant (including elephant ivory) or from any other regulated endangered plant or animal are prohibited."

This policy carries one named category of prohibited product: parts or products from whale, dolphin, porpoise, shark or elephant (including elephant ivory), and from any other regulated endangered plant or animal.

It is not the restricted-products list. A seller deactivated for a restricted listing is almost always looking at the separate Restricted Products policy pages, and this clause should not be cited as though it enumerated them.
