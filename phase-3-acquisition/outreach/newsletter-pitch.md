## 0. The problem this file has to solve honestly

A sponsorship pitch is a claim contest, and **we have nothing to claim.** Every other advertiser in this category arrives at a media buyer holding a success rate: 85%, 87%, "10,000+ sellers." We are forbidden from doing that (`N10`, `R11`, `BRAND.md §4.1`), we have no customers to count, and per `BRAND.md §0` the prohibition is the position rather than a handicap to work around.

So these pitches are built on the only three assets we actually hold on day one (`BRAND.md §3.3`, proof levers 1, 2 and 4, in that order):

1. **A free tool whose output the audience can check.** The Decoder shows the governing policy clause verbatim, with its source, before anyone pays or logs in. Per `demand-seo.md §0.3`, no competitor page sampled — including the four competing free classifiers — quotes policy with a checkable source. That is a demonstrable difference, not an adjective.
2. **Honest triage stated in public.** We decline to draft counterfeit, IP and fraud cases and say so before anyone is charged. To an editor, a sponsor who volunteers what their product won't do is a different kind of sponsor.
3. **Transparent pricing.** $149, $399, $49/mo, published. No demo gate, no "contact us."

**What we therefore ask for is smaller than a normal sponsor asks for**, and the pitches say so plainly: a slot, a rate card, an editorial collaboration, or a trial placement — not a campaign, not an exclusive, not a testimonial we cannot source.

**One honest constraint the founder should carry into any rate negotiation** (`03-gtm-pricing.md §6.4`): contribution LTV ≈ **$355**, maximum sustainable CAC at 3:1 ≈ **$118**. A placement priced above roughly $118 × expected conversions is not a marketing decision, it is a loss taken deliberately. **Ask for the rate card before proposing anything.**

## 1. Rules for every message in this file

1. **The CAN-SPAM block in `partner-sequences.md §1` is mandatory and pasted unchanged** — sender identity, physical-address placeholder, functioning opt-out, non-deceptive subject. Media inboxes get more cold email than anyone; the opt-out is not decoration.
2. **Cadence is the same three-touch ceiling** (`CRM.md §4.3`): Touch 1 day 0, Touch 2 no sooner than day +14, Touch 3 no sooner than day +28, then the row ages to `dormant`. No fourth touch.
3. **Write to the outlet, not to the person.** `CRM.md §6.2` keeps personal names out of the CRM; the drafts inherit it. Reference the *show*, the *newsletter*, the *survey* — the published business identity — not an individual's biography. **No individual is named in any file in this phase**, including hosts and founders whose names appear in the underlying research; where a show is commonly styled with a host's name, this repo records it by its neutral form (e.g. "Better Advertising (Junglr podcast)"). If the founder chooses to address a real person by name in the message they personally send, that is their call at send time — it is not written down here.
4. **Every claim traces to `IDEA_DOSSIER.md` or `BRAND.md`** (`CRM.md §6.3` check 6). Permitted: the category frame, tier names and prices, the free Decoder, the citation mechanic, honest triage, no-account-access, no-automated-submission. **Prohibited: any success rate, reinstatement rate, customer count, "trusted by," or outcome statistic — ours or a competitor's.**
5. **Disclosure of paid placement is offered by us, not extracted from them.** Where a placement is paid, our copy should say so and we should say we expect it to be labelled. In the US, endorsement and testimonial advertising is governed by the FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 255), which turn on disclosure of material connections. *(Founder/counsel to confirm the specific obligations before the first paid placement; this file states the posture, which is: label it.)*
6. **Never propose copy that puts words in a host's mouth.** A host-read ad script is written so that every sentence is true of the product and none of it is a claim about results. Template E is written to that constraint.
7. **No comparative or disparaging copy about another vendor**, in a pitch or in a script (`BRAND.md §4.6`). If asked to differentiate, use the approved Step 7 sentences verbatim.

## 3. Template A — Editorial collaboration (data/analysis newsletters)

**For:** Marketplace Pulse and any outlet whose product is analysis rather than promotion.
**The ask:** a conversation about a piece, not a slot.
**Why no ad buy:** `partners.md` row 33 records that a data-driven guest piece fits their editorial format better than a straight ad buy. Pitching a banner to an analysis publication signals we haven't read it.

**Subject options:**
- `Editorial idea: what suspension notices actually cite`
- `Collaboration idea — reading the enforcement text directly`

```
Hello,

I'm [FOUNDER NAME], founder of Clausewright — a suspension defense copilot
for Amazon and Walmart sellers. This is an editorial idea, not an ad
enquiry.

[PERSONALISATION — a specific recent piece or recurring data series the
outlet published, named accurately, with one sentence about why it's the
reason I'm writing rather than a compliment.]

The idea: almost every published guide to Amazon seller suspensions
paraphrases policy rather than quoting it. I've checked a fair number of
them and the pattern is consistent — the policy is described in the
writer's own words, with no source a reader can open. Meanwhile the
enforcement texts themselves are public for most categories, and reading
what they actually say against what the ecosystem says they say is a
piece I'd read.

What I can contribute: a per-reason-code breakdown of which enforcement
texts are genuinely public and quotable, which are behind the seller login
and therefore can only be approximated, and where the gap between the two
is widest. I'd flag our own limits in the piece too — there are categories
where the governing text isn't publicly reachable and we say so rather than
paraphrasing around it.

What I'm not offering: a success rate or a reinstatement statistic. Nobody
in this category can audit those numbers, including us, and we don't
publish one. If the piece needs a headline number, this isn't the piece.

Would a conversation be worth 15 minutes? If the answer is that you don't
run contributed work, that's useful to know and I'll leave it there.

[CAN-SPAM BLOCK — see partner-sequences.md §1]
```

**Touch 2 (day +14 min).** Offer the artifact rather than repeating the ask:
> *"Following up once. Whether or not a piece makes sense, here's the underlying material: [DECODER URL] classifies a pasted notice and shows the governing clause verbatim with its source, free and with no account. If it's useful as a reference for something you're already writing, use it and no credit needed."*

**Touch 3 (day +28 min).** Close, offer a redirect, stop.

## 4. Template B — Newsletter sponsorship enquiry

**For:** Seller Bites, Billion Dollar Seller Network, Ad Badger Newsletter.
**The ask:** rate card and audience shape. **Do not propose a price or a package in Touch 1** — asking for their rate card first is both more useful and more respectful of a publisher who has one.

**Subject options:**
- `Sponsorship enquiry — rate card?`
- `Advertising enquiry for [NEWSLETTER NAME]`

```
Hello,

I'm [FOUNDER NAME], founder of Clausewright — a suspension defense copilot
for Amazon and Walmart sellers. Short sponsorship enquiry.

[PERSONALISATION — the newsletter's cadence and focus as it describes
itself, plus the specific reason its readership overlaps ours: sellers who
run their own account and would handle a deactivation themselves.]

What we'd be advertising: a free notice decoder. A seller pastes their
Amazon or Walmart deactivation notice and gets the reason code, the exact
policy clause they were charged under quoted with its source, and a
critique of what a Plan of Action would still be missing — no account, no
card. Paid tiers start at $149 for a complete submission-ready draft and

Two things worth stating up front, because they change what a sponsorship
can say: we don't publish a success rate and won't, and we decline to
draft counterfeit, IP and fraud cases at all rather than attempting them.
Any copy we run has to be consistent with both.

Could you send your rate card and a sense of list size, open rate and what
sponsor formats you offer? I'd rather start from what you actually sell
than propose something that doesn't exist.

[CAN-SPAM BLOCK]
```

**Touch 2 (day +14 min):**
> *"Following up once on the rate card. In the meantime the tool itself is free and open if you or your readers ever want it: [DECODER URL]. If sponsorships are sold out or not currently offered, just say so and I'll close the loop."*

## 6. Template D — Guest / co-produced episode

**For:** My Amazon Guy (YouTube), Better Advertising (Junglr podcast), or any show where the editorial bar makes an ad read poorly.
**The ask:** an episode about the seller's problem, in which we are a source rather than a sponsor.
**Why this is often the better ask:** per `BRAND.md §3.3`, the strongest proof we hold is a demonstration the audience can check — not a claim. A screen-share of a real notice being decoded, clause visible and sourced, is that demonstration. A 30-second read is not.

**Subject options:**
- `Episode idea: reading a deactivation notice line by line`
- `Guest idea — what the enforcement text actually says`

```
Hello,

I'm [FOUNDER NAME], founder of Clausewright — a suspension defense copilot
for Amazon and Walmart sellers. Episode idea rather than a sponsorship
enquiry.

[PERSONALISATION — a specific episode or series the show has published on
account health, suspensions, or policy, named accurately.]

The episode: take a real deactivation notice, on screen, and read it the
way someone who has to answer it should — identify which enforcement family
it belongs to, pull up the governing policy text and read the clause
verbatim from the source, and then say plainly what a response has to
contain and what it must not do. Then do the same for a category we
*refuse* to touch, and explain why a tool shouldn't draft an IP or
counterfeit case at all.

Why I think it earns its slot: nearly everything published on this topic
paraphrases the policy instead of quoting it, so listeners can't check any
of it. Doing it live with the source open is unusual, and it's useful to
someone who never buys anything from anyone.

What I'd bring: the notice-by-notice walkthrough, the honest list of what
we decline, and the fact that we don't publish a success rate — which I'd
rather explain on air than be asked about.

Interested? Happy to do it without any commercial arrangement, and happy
for the free tool to go unmentioned if you'd rather.

[CAN-SPAM BLOCK]
```

**Touch 2 (day +14 min):** offer to record a short unlisted sample rather than repeat the pitch.
**Touch 3 (day +28 min):** close, redirect, stop.

## 7. Template E — Host-read script (only after a placement is agreed)

Written so **every sentence is a statement about the product, not about results.** 30-second and 60-second versions. `BRAND.md §2.4` register R-1 for the opening, R-2 for the mechanic.

**30 seconds:**
> *"This episode is sponsored by Clausewright — a suspension defense copilot for Amazon and Walmart sellers. If your account goes dark, you can paste the notice into their free decoder and it will tell you which policy you were charged under and show you the exact clause, with the source, so you can read it yourself. No account, no card. If you want the full plan of action written, that's $149, or $399 if you want a person to review it the same day. They never ask for your Seller Central login, and they don't submit anything for you — you do that yourself. That's [LIVE DOMAIN — clausewright.com is acquisition-pending per BRAND.md §5.7; use the actual launch domain]."*

**60 seconds — adds the honest-triage beat, which is the part worth the extra thirty seconds:**
> *"…One more thing, because it's unusual: there are case types they won't draft at all — counterfeit, intellectual property complaints, fraud allegations. They tell you that before you pay anything, and they say who to talk to instead. They also don't publish a success rate, which they'll tell you is because nobody in this category can audit those numbers, including them. What you can check for yourself, free, before you spend a cent, is whether they've correctly identified what you're charged with and whether the clause on screen is really the clause. That's the whole pitch. [LIVE DOMAIN]."*

**Prohibited in any script** (`BRAND.md §4`): any percentage or rate; "guaranteed reinstatement"; "trusted by thousands"; "AI-powered" as the differentiator; "our AI agent"/"autopilot"/"we submit for you"; "legal advice"/"counsel"/"we'll fight Amazon for you"; any countdown, discount, or expiring offer; any comparison to a named competitor.
**Required if the placement is paid:** the placement is labelled as advertising in the format the outlet normally uses. We ask for this; we do not accept an unlabelled paid read.

## 9. Pre-send checklist

Beyond the six checks in `CRM.md §6.3`, which all apply:

1. **Outlet-fit check.** Am I pitching an ad buy to an outlet that publishes analysis, or a guest slot to an outlet that only sells slots? Wrong template is the most common way this fails.
2. **One sequence per company.** Verify the company is not already at `contacted` under a `partner-sequences.md` sequence.
3. **Personalisation check.** Does the reference name real, current, published work? Would the sentence survive a find-and-replace of the outlet's name? If yes, rewrite it.
4. **No-person check.** Am I referencing a show, a newsletter, or a company — not an individual's biography? (`CRM.md §6.2`.)
5. **Claim sweep.** Zero rates, zero counts, zero outcome statistics, zero "trusted by," ours or anyone's. (N10/R11.)
6. **Price check.** `$149.` `$399.` `$49/mo.` No fake anchoring, no "was/now," no discount (`BRAND.md §4.6`).
7. **Tier names.** Decoder, Rescue, Rescue + Human, Shield, Shield Pro (`BRAND.md §5.3`). Never "free trial," "Pro," "Premium."
8. **Autonomy/legal vocabulary sweep** (`BRAND.md §4.4`, §4.5) — *copilot* not *agent*; *policy clause* not *legal clause*; *you review and submit*.
9. **Disclosure posture.** If money changes hands, does the message state that we expect the placement to be labelled? (§1 rule 5.)
10. **Rate discipline.** Before agreeing any figure, check it against ~$118 max sustainable CAC × expected conversions (`03-gtm-pricing.md §6.4`). Above that line is a deliberate loss, and it should be taken deliberately or not at all.

## 10. Frameworks applied

- **Alex Hormozi**, *$100M Leads* (2023) — the lead magnet leads every pitch: what we ask an outlet to put in front of its audience is the free Decoder, not the paid tier, because a complete free solution to a narrow problem is what a publisher can defend to their readers. Paid ads sit fourth in the Core Four for this business (`03-gtm-pricing.md §4.4`), which is why these asks are bounded trials rather than campaigns.
- **Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares**, *Traction* (2015) — "Targeting Blogs" (18/25) and Business Development (18/25) are middle-ring and reserve channels, not the engine (D8). These pitches are cheap, time-boxed tests with a stop rule, per the Bullseye method.
- **April Dunford**, *Obviously Awesome* (2019) — the category frame appears in full once in every first message (`BRAND.md §5.2`); Step 7's non-disparaging sentences are the only permitted comparative language if an editor asks how we differ.
- **Aaron Ross**, *Predictable Revenue* (2011) — a fixed three-touch cadence with a defined stop; a reply of "send more" is `replied`, not progress (`CRM.md §2.2`).
- **Rob Fitzpatrick**, *The Mom Test* (2013) — Template B and C ask for the rate card and the media kit *before* proposing anything, which is the commercial form of asking about their world rather than pitching ours.
- **FTC Endorsement Guides**, 16 C.F.R. Part 255 — disclosure of material connections in endorsement and testimonial advertising; the reason §1 rule 5 and §7 require paid placements to be labelled. *Founder/counsel to confirm specifics before the first paid placement.*
- **CAN-SPAM Act**, 15 U.S.C. §7704 — implemented via the mandatory block in `partner-sequences.md §1`.
