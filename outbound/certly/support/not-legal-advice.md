Hello,

Thank you for the question, and we want to answer it accurately, which
means saying where our answer stops.

Certly compares what a certificate says to the requirements you entered. It
can tell you that a certificate shows a $1,000,000 each-occurrence limit
against a requirement of $2,000,000, that an additional insured box is
ticked with no endorsement page attached, or that a policy period ended
last month. It cannot tell you whether $2,000,000 is the right number for
your lease or subcontract, whether a particular endorsement would respond
to a particular claim, or whether a vendor is insured today. Only the
insurer can confirm coverage, and only your own broker or counsel can tell
you whether your requirements are the right ones.

The requirement templates in the product are starting points built from
published sources and real contract exhibits, each dated and linked; your
own agreement always governs, and they should be edited before you rely on
them.

The standing disclaimer that appears on every result, report and export,
quoted from the product so the wording is exact:

Certly reads documents. It does not verify coverage. A certificate of
insurance is issued as a matter of information only and confers no rights on
the certificate holder. Certly extracts what a document says and compares it
to the requirements you entered. It does not confirm that a policy is in
force, that an endorsement exists, or that coverage would respond to a
claim. Only the insurer can confirm coverage, and only your own counsel or
broker can tell you whether your requirements are the right ones.

If it helps, we are glad to show exactly what a certificate says and where
we read it from, so you can put a precise question to your broker.
