Hormozi · the binding constraint

Spend everything on belief

Dream Outcome 9. Time Delay 9. Effort 8. Perceived Likelihood: 3. Optimising a 10-minute draft down to 4 minutes optimises a term already at 9. Every dollar of offer budget goes to proof.

The peak-end rule

Sell the panic, subscribe at relief

Willingness to pay is event-shaped — it spikes at suspension and collapses at reinstatement. So 30 days of Shield ships free with every appeal, card on file. The retention decision lands 30 days later, at the moment of relief.

Guarantee stack

Lead with time

"Your draft is in your inbox in 10 minutes or it's free." Cheap, fully in our control, and nobody else offers a time guarantee. Honest triage screens unwinnable cases out before payment — which is what makes a strong refund guarantee survivable.

How it is built — 01

A workflow, not an agent.

Four fixed stages with predefined code paths. No vector database, no fine-tuning, no marketplace API in v1 — the whole corpus sits in a cached prompt at a tenth of the input cost, so marginal cost per draft is cents against a $149 price.

Paste notice→ Classify→ Retrieve→ Draft→ Critique→ Preview · free→ Pay→ Full POA + PDF
Low confidence? An UNCLASSIFIED result never guesses. It converts to the $399 human tier — the single failure mode that would burn a seller's one appeal attempt becomes the differentiated revenue line instead.
Outcome loop Consent at payment, then day 3 / 10 / 21 follow-ups feed redacted (notice → draft → outcome) triples back into the corpus. This is the only component whose value compounds. If the build slips, everything else gets cut first.
Routing + prompt chaining

20–30 reason codes

Section 3, inauthentic, IP complaint, safety, restricted product, ODR, late shipment, linked account, dropship, review manipulation, verification — plus Walmart equivalents. Each emits a confidence score.

Evaluator–optimizer

The critique is shown free

"No supplier invoices referenced. No measurable preventive control. Apologetic tone." Visible pre-paywall — because a differentiator hidden behind payment tests the promise, not the product.

Code-level invariant

No citation, no render

Any policy reference lacking a backing citation object is stripped before render, enforced by a test in CI. A hallucinated clause would destroy the exact trust we sell, so it is not left to a prompt instruction.

The knowledge base — 4 layers

L1–L3 build in a day. L4 is the asset.

Reason-code taxonomy, our own structured policy summaries keyed to clause IDs, and structural appeal patterns are hand-authored from public sources. The consented outcome corpus starts at zero — and is the only thing that compounds.

The bright line

We never touch the login

Zero credential handling, zero automated submission, zero access behind Seller Central. Public robots-allowed forums only, re-checked before every crawl. It removes an entire risk class for free — and a competitor already markets its absence.

How it is built — 02

Money before product. Then five days.

Day 0 · blocking, human decision

Five pre-flight gates

Kill the name — reinstate.io has traded since 2019 and ReinstateIQ is live; building first means rebuilding. Then counsel review of the disclaimer set, the forum participation rules, the agent-policy primary source, and accounts. None takes more than a day. All are cheap now and expensive later.

Days 1–3 · concierge

Take money before the product exists

One landing page with the anchor table, two Stripe links, and a manual delivery runbook: intake → retrieval → draft → risk pass → PDF within 60 minutes. The triage-refusal list is written down before the first customer, so it is applied consistently rather than negotiated under revenue pressure.

Days 2–14 · the daily loop

8–15 genuinely useful replies a day

Identify the reason code from their pasted notice, quote the exact clause, name the one thing most sellers get wrong for that code. Every reply must stand alone as useful if the link is never clicked. Every customer gets a 15-minute call — customer discovery disguised as onboarding.

Days 1–5 · the build

Corpus → pipeline → paywall → evals → ship

Day 1 corpus and skeleton. Day 2 the four stages, each tested independently, citation invariant enforced. Day 3 frontend, Stripe, PDF, and the outcome loop. Day 4 evals against 40 hand-labelled notices plus an adversarial pass. Day 5 deploy, one live transaction, and the first rush orders fulfilled by hand — every human edit written down. The corrections are the roadmap.

Days 5–35 · innovation accounting

Two numbers, pre-committed rules

Primary metric: preview → paid, measured only on sessions that reached a successful classification. Persevere ≥8%. Iterate at 3–8%. Pivot below 3% — there is no cheaper price to retreat to that isn't already the incumbent's ground. Drafts generated, page views and waitlist size are designated vanity metrics and are not reported.
