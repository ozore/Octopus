The Experts in Nationwide Contractor Licensing

We help you get your license quickly nationwide.

At API Processing, we offer several pathways to help contractors obtain out-of-state licenses, depending on the states involved and the type of work you do. Here’s a breakdown of the options and how we can help with each one.

1. Reciprocity

Many states have reciprocity agreements, allowing you to use your existing contractor license from one state to apply for a license in another without needing to retake exams. These agreements vary by trade and state.

Benefits of Reciprocity

  • Faster Licensing Process. Reciprocity agreements save you from having to take a new exam in every state, reducing delays in getting your license.
  • Reduced Paperwork. The process is often simpler, requiring fewer documents and less hassle compared to applying from scratch.
  • Immediate Expansion. Reciprocity allows you to enter new markets quickly and start working on projects in other states.

API Processing researches which states offer reciprocity for your license, manages your application, and ensures you meet all the necessary requirements to get licensed as efficiently as possible.

2. NASCLA Accreditation

For contractors looking to operate in multiple states, the NASCLA (National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies) Accredited Exam is one of the most effective solutions. The NASCLA exam is accepted by many states, allowing you to take one exam and apply for licenses in several states at once.

Benefits of NASCLA Accreditation

  • Cost and Time Savings. Fewer exams mean less time and money spent on exam fees and preparation.
  • Seamless Multi-State Licensing. Once you pass the NASCLA exam, you can easily apply for licenses in states that recognize the accreditation.
  • One Exam, Multiple States. Instead of retaking exams in every state, passing the NASCLA exam allows you to apply for a license in multiple states without additional tests.

States Accepting NASCLA

Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, North Carolina, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia, U.S. Virgin Islands.

Two Types of NASCLA Exams

For more information about NASCLA exams, please visit NASCLA.org.

NASCLA Exam for Commercial General Building Contractors

This exam is designed for contractors specializing in commercial building projects. Passing this exam allows you to apply for a general contractor’s license in multiple states without having to retake exams in each one.

States that Accept the NASCLA Exam for Commercial Building Contractors:

Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia, U.S. Virgin Islands.

Benefits:

Once you pass this exam, you can submit your results to any of the accepting states to streamline your application process, saving time and effort.

NASCLA Exam for Electrical Contractors

The NASCLA Accredited Electrical Exam is designed specifically for electrical contractors who want to perform electrical work in multiple states. This exam ensures that you meet the licensing standards for electrical work in participating states.

States that Administer or Accept the NASCLA Exam for Electrical Contractors:

Alabama, Arizona, D.C., Florida, Idaho, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, New Mexico, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia

Benefits:

Similar to the general contractor exam, passing the NASCLA Electrical Exam means you won’t need to retake separate state exams. This allows you to expand your electrical contracting services across multiple states with less hassle.

3. Endorsement and License Transfers

In states where reciprocity isn’t available, some states offer license by endorsement. This allows you to transfer your license based on your qualifications, work experience, and other factors without needing to retake the trade exam, though there may be additional requirements like passing a business law exam.

Benefits of Endorsement

  • Recognition of Your Experience. States that offer license by endorsement allow you to use your existing qualifications and experience to bypass the full licensing process.
  • Faster and More Flexible. While some additional requirements may apply, this option can still save time compared to starting the licensing process from scratch.
  • Cost-Effective. Fewer exams and less paperwork mean lower overall costs when expanding your business.

API Processing assists in gathering and submitting the necessary documentation to ensure your license transfer goes smoothly, while also handling any additional state-specific requirements like business law exams or experience verification.

4. State-Specific Contractor Exams

If reciprocity or NASCLA accreditation is not available, you may need to take a state-specific contractor exam. These exams typically test your trade knowledge, business acumen, and understanding of state laws and regulations.

Benefits of State Specific Contractor Exams

  • Complete State Compliance. Passing a state-specific exam ensures you meet all the state’s requirements for operating legally.
  • Full Licensing Scope. Once licensed, you can work on any type of project within that state’s jurisdiction, giving you more flexibility.
  • Expanded Expertise. Preparing for state-specific exams can enhance your knowledge of local laws and best practices, making you a more competitive contractor.

API Processing manages everything from registering for state-specific exams to ensuring you meet the eligibility criteria. Once you pass the exam, we handle the licensing application and documentation, so you’re ready to start working.

5. Temporary and Project-Specific Licensing

For contractors who only need to work on a short-term project in another state, some states offer temporary or project-specific licenses. These allow you to work without obtaining a full, long-term license.

Benefits of Temporary Licensing

  • Quick Approval. Temporary licenses allow you to start working faster than a full license application.
  • Lower Costs. Temporary licenses are often cheaper than long-term licenses, making them a cost-effective solution for short-term projects.
  • No Long-Term Commitment. These licenses are tied to a specific project, allowing you to work in the state without committing to a permanent license.

API Processing handles the paperwork for temporary or project-specific licensing, ensuring compliance with state requirements and getting you authorized to work quickly.

What is Reciprocity?

Reciprocity in contractor licensing is a valuable opportunity for contractors who are looking to expand their business into new states. It refers to agreements between states that allow a contractor who holds a valid license in one state to obtain a similar license in another state without having to go through the full licensing process again. This means that if you're already licensed and in good standing in one state, you may be able to fast-track your licensing in another state, avoiding the need to retake exams or meet all the standard requirements from scratch.

However, reciprocity agreements vary significantly from state to state. Not all states offer reciprocity, and those that do may have specific conditions or limitations. For example, you may need to pass a state-specific exam, meet certain experience requirements, or provide detailed documentation of your qualifications.

At API Processing, we specialize in helping contractors navigate the complexities of reciprocity agreements. Our expert team will assess your current licensing status and guide you through the process of obtaining additional licenses in other states, ensuring that you meet all the necessary requirements and can expand your business as smoothly as possible.

NASCLA - Explained in Simple Terms

NASCLA, or the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies, is a pivotal organization in the construction industry that unites state licensing agencies, regulatory bodies, and other key entities involved in contractor licensing across the United States.

What Does NASCLA Do?

  1. NASCLA Accredited Exam: NASCLA is best known for its development of the NASCLA Accredited Examination for Commercial General Building Contractors. This exam is designed to simplify the licensing process for contractors who want to work in multiple states. By passing this single, standardized exam, contractors can satisfy the examination requirements in several participating states, making it easier and quicker to obtain licenses in those states.
  2. State Collaboration: NASCLA fosters collaboration among state licensing agencies, promoting the adoption of best practices and the development of uniform licensing standards. This helps to reduce confusion and streamline the regulatory environment for contractors working across state lines.
  3. Advocacy and Education: NASCLA advocates for effective regulatory policies and provides educational resources to help contractors stay informed about licensing requirements and industry standards. Their efforts contribute to the professionalization of the contracting industry and help protect both contractors and consumers.

Why is NASCLA Important?

  1. Streamlined Licensing: For contractors looking to expand their operations into multiple states, NASCLA is crucial. The NASCLA Accredited Exam allows contractors to avoid the time-consuming and costly process of taking multiple state-specific exams. Instead, with one exam, they can qualify for licensure in several states, accelerating their ability to grow their business.
  2. Uniform Standards: NASCLA’s push for standardized licensing practices across states helps create a more predictable and navigable landscape for contractors. This uniformity is especially important for contractors who work in multiple states or who plan to expand their services regionally or nationally.
  3. Industry Leadership: NASCLA’s leadership in contractor licensing ensures that the industry moves toward higher standards of professionalism, safety, and quality. By supporting NASCLA initiatives, contractors can contribute to and benefit from a well-regulated, fair, and competitive industry.

At API Processing, we recognize the importance of NASCLA and can help you navigate its resources, including preparing for and leveraging the NASCLA Accredited Exam to expand your contracting business across state lines with ease.

FAQ - Nationwide Contractor Licensing

What is out-of-state contractor licensing?Expand

Out-of-state licensing refers to obtaining a contractor license in a state other than Florida, or getting licensed in Florida if you’re already licensed elsewhere. This often involves: Reciprocity agreements, where one state recognizes certain credentials from another Endorsement applications, which let you skip certain exam requirements New applications, where you'll need to meet that state’s licensing requirements from scratch At API Processing, we help contractors evaluate eligibility, gather the right documentation, and navigate the specific rules of each state to streamline the multi-state licensing process.

What is the difference between a Certified and a Registered Contractor License?Expand

A Certified Contractor License allows you to work anywhere in Florida, and it is issued by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). A Registered Contractor License only allows you to work in specific local jurisdictions where you’ve obtained a competency card from the local licensing authority.

If you're looking to expand your business or operate freely across the state, go for the Certified License.

What are the basic requirements to get a Certified Contractor License?Expand

Requirements vary by state, but generally to qualify for a Certified Contractor License, you generally need:

  • At least 4 years of experience (or an approved combination of education and experience)

  • A passing score on the state certification exam

  • Background check and credit report

  • Liability and workers’ compensation insurance

  • Proof of financial responsibility

We make sure you’re fully prepared and meet each requirement before your application is submitted.
