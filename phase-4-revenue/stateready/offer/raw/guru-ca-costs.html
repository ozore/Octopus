California Contractors License Costs: Fees, Renewals & Hidden Expenses

  • December 17, 2025

California Contractors License Costs: Fees, Renewals & Hidden Expenses

Last updated: August 31st, 2026

Understanding California Contractors License Costs

The process of obtaining a contractor’s license in California involves several costs, and understanding them before you apply can help you avoid surprises.

The Contractors State License Board (CSLB) charges fees for your original application and initial license issuance. If examinations are required, those fees are now paid separately and directly to PSI, CSLB’s examination vendor.

There are also ongoing expenses to consider after you’re licensed, including renewals, your contractor bond, insurance requirements, and other business-compliance costs.

Knowing the difference between CSLB fees, PSI examination fees, and other business expenses makes it much easier to create a realistic licensing budget.

For an overview of the licensing process itself, visit our guide to California contractor licensing.

Breaking Down Initial Application and Examination Fees

The current Application for Original Contractor License fee is $450 for one classification.

This is a non-refundable CSLB application fee, so it’s important to make sure your application is complete and your experience documentation supports the classification you’re requesting before you submit it.

California Contractor License Application Fee

Current CSLB application cost:

  • Original license application: $450

The application fee is the same whether you’re applying as a sole owner or a non-sole-owner entity.

However, the initial license issuance fee differs depending on the business entity.

Initial License Fee

Once CSLB requests your initial license fee, the current amounts are:

  • Sole Owner: $200
  • Non-Sole Owner: $350

Non-sole-owner entities include structures such as corporations, partnerships, LLCs, and joint ventures.

That means the basic CSLB application and initial licensing fees for one classification currently total:

  • Sole Owner: $650
  • Non-Sole Owner: $800

These totals do not include examination fees, fingerprinting, bonds, insurance, exam preparation, or other business expenses.

What Do the CSLB Exams Cost?

This part of the licensing process has changed.

CSLB contracts with PSI Exams to administer its licensing examinations. After CSLB accepts your application and refers you for testing, you’ll receive a Notice to Schedule.

You then schedule your required examination or examinations and pay the applicable exam fees directly to PSI.

Rather than relying on an old static dollar amount published in a blog post, check the current fee shown through the PSI California Contractors exam portal when you’re ready to schedule.

CSLB also explains the current process in its Examination FAQ.

If you don’t pass an examination and need another attempt, you’ll also schedule and pay the applicable re-examination fee directly through PSI.

Preparing thoroughly before test day can help you avoid unnecessary retesting costs. Our guide, Don’t Sweat the CSLB Exam: Practice Your Way to Success, explains how practice exams can fit into your preparation.

Navigating License Issuance and Renewal Fees

Getting the license issued isn’t the end of your licensing expenses.

An active California contractor license generally must be renewed every two years.

The renewal fee depends on whether your license belongs to a sole owner or a non-sole-owner entity.

Current Timely Active Renewal Fees

If CSLB receives an acceptable renewal on or before the license expiration date, the current timely active renewal fees are:

  • Sole Owner: $450
  • Non-Sole Owner: $700

These are not interchangeable.

The previous version of this article described the renewal fee as simply $700, but that applies to a non-sole-owner active license, not every contractor license.

What Happens if You Renew Late?

Missing the renewal deadline can make the process more expensive.

Current delinquent active renewal fees are:

  • Sole Owner: $675
  • Non-Sole Owner: $1,050

That makes timely renewal one of the easiest ways to avoid an unnecessary licensing expense.

You can review CSLB’s current contractor license fees whenever you’re budgeting for an application or renewal.

Some classifications can also have additional statutory charges. For example, active C-10 Electrical license renewals currently include an additional fee used for electrician certification enforcement.

Staying informed about new laws affecting California contractors can help you catch changes that may affect licensing costs or compliance requirements.

Understanding the $25,000 California Contractor Bond

One important expense is the California Contractor’s Bond.

An active contractor license generally requires a $25,000 Contractor’s Bond to be on file with CSLB.

But there’s an important distinction:

The $25,000 bond is not a guarantee that you will complete a customer’s construction project.

According to CSLB, the contractor bond is filed for the benefit of:

  • Consumers who may be damaged by defective construction or other violations of contractor license law
  • Employees who have not been paid wages that are due to them

The bond is also not the same thing as general liability insurance.

Do You Pay $25,000 for the Bond?

Usually, no.

The $25,000 figure is the bond amount, not necessarily the amount you pay out of pocket to purchase the bond.

Contractors typically purchase the bond through a licensed surety company and pay a premium. Your actual premium can vary based on the surety company’s underwriting criteria and other factors.

That makes it difficult to give every contractor one universal bond price.

You can review the CSLB Contractor Bond requirements for the current rules.

Depending on your entity and qualifying individual, additional bond requirements may also apply.

Identifying and Managing Hidden Contractor License Expenses

The CSLB application and license fees are only part of your total cost.

Several additional expenses can arise depending on your application, classification, business structure, and employees.

Fingerprinting and Live Scan

Many license applicants must complete fingerprinting.

CSLB currently lists separate Department of Justice and FBI processing fees, while the Live Scan location can charge its own rolling fee.

Because those rolling fees vary by provider, don’t assume every applicant will pay exactly the same amount.

Exam Preparation

You can prepare using free CSLB study guides, self-study materials, or a structured contractor-license exam preparation program.

The cost depends entirely on the type of preparation you choose.

If you’re taking a classification-specific exam, using current materials is especially important. For example, HVAC applicants can review our guide to passing the California C-20 license exam.

Insurance

Your insurance costs depend on factors such as:

  • Classification
  • Employees
  • Payroll
  • Business structure
  • Type of work performed
  • Claims history
  • Contract requirements

Workers’ compensation rules also vary depending on the license classification and circumstances, so don’t assume every no-employee contractor has identical requirements.

Business Entity Expenses

If you’re operating as a corporation or LLC, there can be separate business-formation and compliance costs outside CSLB.

These can include:

  • Secretary of State filings
  • Statement of Information filings
  • California taxes and fees
  • Business banking
  • Insurance
  • Accounting or bookkeeping
  • An Agent for Service of Process or professional agent service, if you choose to use one

These aren’t technically contractor license fees, but they’re real costs of operating the business entity holding the license.

If you’re considering professional agent services, see our guide on choosing an Agent for Service of Process.

LLC-Specific Contractor Costs

Contractor LLCs have additional CSLB requirements that don’t apply to a standard sole-owner license.

For example, California contractor LLCs have additional bonding and liability-insurance requirements.

So if you’re comparing the cost of a sole proprietorship, corporation, and LLC, don’t look only at the $200 versus $350 initial license fee. Consider the ongoing entity-specific requirements as well.

How Much Does a California Contractor License Really Cost?

There isn’t one single number that applies to every applicant.

Your total cost can include:

  • $450 CSLB application fee
  • $200 sole-owner or $350 non-sole-owner initial license fee
  • PSI examination fees, when examinations are required
  • Fingerprinting and Live Scan costs
  • Contractor bond premium
  • Workers’ compensation insurance, when required
  • Other insurance
  • Exam preparation
  • Business entity costs
  • Local licenses and permits
  • Renewal fees

The best way to budget is to separate mandatory CSLB fees from expenses that vary based on your business and application.

Conclusion: Effective Cost Management Strategies for Contractors

Managing California contractor licensing costs comes down to knowing which fees are fixed and which expenses vary.

The major CSLB numbers to remember are:

  • Original Application: $450
  • Initial License – Sole Owner: $200
  • Initial License – Non-Sole Owner: $350
  • Timely Active Renewal – Sole Owner: $450
  • Timely Active Renewal – Non-Sole Owner: $700
  • Contractor Bond Amount: $25,000

Examination fees are separate and are paid directly to PSI when you schedule, so check the current PSI amount rather than relying on an old exam-fee figure from a blog.

And remember: the $25,000 contractor bond is not a project-completion guarantee. It serves specific consumer and employee protections established under California contractor license law.

Understanding these costs before applying can help you budget properly, avoid late-renewal penalties, and keep your license in good standing.

FAQs

What are the initial costs for a contractor’s license in California?

The current CSLB application fee for one classification is $450.

After CSLB requests the initial license fee, you’ll currently pay:

  • $200 for a sole-owner license
  • $350 for a non-sole-owner license

Examination fees, when required, are separate and paid directly to PSI.

How much does it cost to renew a California contractor license?

For a timely active renewal, the current fees are:

  • $450 for a sole-owner license
  • $700 for a non-sole-owner license

If you renew late, higher delinquent fees apply.

How often do I need to renew my contractor’s license?

An active California contractor license generally must be renewed every two years.

Make sure your renewal is submitted on time to avoid delinquent fees and a possible interruption in your license status.

How much do the CSLB exams cost?

Applicants now schedule required examinations and pay the associated fees directly to PSI after CSLB approves and refers the application for testing.

Because exam fees are handled through PSI, check your current scheduling information rather than relying on an old static dollar amount.

What is the $25,000 contractor bond?

California generally requires an active contractor license to maintain a $25,000 Contractor’s Bond.

The bond benefits consumers who may be damaged by defective construction or other license-law violations and employees who haven’t received wages due to them.

It is not a guarantee that a contractor will complete a particular job.

Do I have to pay $25,000 to get the contractor bond?

Typically, no.

$25,000 is the required bond amount. Contractors generally purchase the bond from a surety company and pay a premium based on that company’s underwriting.

Are there hidden California contractor license costs?

There can be.

Depending on your circumstances, additional costs may include:

  • PSI examination fees
  • Fingerprinting
  • Bond premiums
  • Workers’ compensation insurance
  • General liability insurance
  • Exam preparation
  • Business entity filings
  • Agent for Service of Process services
  • Local business licenses
  • Late-renewal penalties

Budget for the entire licensing and business setup process rather than only the $450 application fee.
