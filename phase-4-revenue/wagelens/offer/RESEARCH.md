# WageLens — Offer & Landing Research

**Subject:** WageLens — type your project's state, county and construction type; get the exact
Davis-Bacon rate and fringe per classification; map your workers; enter weekly hours; download a
filled WH-347 and Statement of Compliance every week; get alerted when the determination changes.
**Assignment:** what actually converts for this buyer, evidenced, so that `OFFER.md` and
`LANDING_SPEC.md` are built on fetched sources rather than instinct.
**Author:** Offer & Landing agent (WageLens), wave 1. **Date:** 2026-09-03.
**Method:** Hormozi *$100M Offers* (value equation, Grand Slam construction, guarantee taxonomy,
bonuses, honest scarcity, naming); Suby *Sell Like Crazy* (Godfather offer, HVCO, the eight phases);
Wiebe / Copyhackers (message hierarchy, voice-of-customer, copy-length doctrine); CXL and Unbounce
on B2B landing structure and length; Nielsen Norman Group on scanning; Poyar / Growth Unhinged and
TrustRadius on trial design and pricing transparency; and the incumbents' own live pages.
**Rule obeyed:** every load-bearing claim below carries a URL that was fetched on 2026-09-03 and the
sentence it came from. Where a number could only be obtained second-hand it is marked
**[second-hand]** and is barred from customer-facing copy.

## 0. Executive summary — the six decisions this research forces

**D1 — The competitive story in `shortlist.json` is half wrong and must be restated.** The shortlist
positions WageLens against "incumbents' quote-gated $175–2,000/mo." Two corrections, both from
first-party pages fetched today. (a) **LCPtracker publishes prices for its contractor product**:
LCPcertified is "$12 Per Report", "Up to 5 Active Projects: $145/Month", up to
"Unlimited Active Projects: $7,400/Year"
([lcptracker.com/solutions/lcpcertified](https://lcptracker.com/solutions/lcpcertified/)). (b) A
transparent, cheaper, feature-adjacent competitor exists that the shortlist never names:
**CertifiedPayrollPro at "$49/month" + "$5 per certified payroll report" / "$99/month" + "$3" /
"$249/month" + "$1", with a "14-day free trial with 3 free reports included. No credit card required"**
([certifiedpayrollpro.com/pricing](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/pricing)). *Transparent pricing
is therefore not, by itself, our differentiator.* It is table stakes against the one competitor most
likely to be in the buyer's other tab.

**D2 — The wedge is not the lookup. It is the revision.** *(Restated after the verification pass —
§3.6. The first draft of this document got this wrong and the correction is the most valuable single
output of the research.)* A free county-level Davis-Bacon lookup is a **commodity**: at least two exist
publicly today, and one of them belongs to our nearest competitor —
[certifiedpayrollpro.com/wage-lookup](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/wage-lookup), "Search
Davis-Bacon prevailing wage determinations from SAM.gov instantly", "**100% Free, No Account
Required**", plus "prevailing wage rate change alerts". What **no** incumbent page fetched today
describes is **the revision**: which modification of the determination your contract actually locked,
and the ability to reproduce that rate three years later. 29 CFR 1.6 fixes the applicable determination
at solicitation or award, so today's published rate is frequently *not* the buyer's rate; and 29 CFR
5.5(a)(3)(ii)(G) requires him to keep the records for three years after the prime contract completes.
**Everyone sells the form. Two sell the lookup. Nobody sells the receipt.** WageLens should sell the
receipt — the rate, its determination, its revision, and the ability to prove all three long after the
job is closed.

**D3 — The strongest number in the whole offer is DOL's own.** The WH-347 page carries the statutory
burden statement: **"We estimate that it will take an average of 55 minutes to complete this collection
of information"** ([dol.gov/agencies/whd/forms/wh347](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/forms/wh347)).
It is the government's own estimate, per form, per week, per project. Points North independently
markets the same order of magnitude — "Per individual project, it is expected that your team will
spend almost an hour just on managing certified payroll reports"
([points-north.com](https://www.points-north.com/certified-payroll-reporting)). **A contractor on
four active federal jobs is being told by the issuing agency that this costs him roughly 3.7 hours
every week, forever.** That is the Time Delay term of Hormozi's denominator, sourced, and it needs no
embellishment.

**D4 — Urgency is real and dated; do not manufacture any.** 29 CFR 1.6 supplies three genuine clocks:
general determinations "remain valid until revised, superseded, or canceled" and carry no expiration;
"a revised wage determination issued at least 10 calendar days before the opening of bids is effective
with respect to the solicitation and contract"; a contract unawarded 90 days after bid opening picks up
the revision; and open-ended contracts take "the most recent revision(s) of any applicable wage
determination(s) on each anniversary date of the contract's award"
([eCFR 29 CFR 1.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/2026-09-01/title-29?part=1&section=1.6)).
Plus the weekly clock: certified payrolls are due **"weekly, for each week in which any DBA- or Related
Acts-covered work is performed"**
([eCFR 29 CFR 5.5(a)(3)(ii)(A)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/2026-09-01/title-29?part=5&section=5.5)).
Hormozi's warning against fabricated urgency is unnecessary here — the calendar does the work.

**D5 — Publish the price, and say so as a claim.** TrustRadius's 2023 B2B Buying Disconnect (n=1,604
technology buyers, surveyed March 2023) finds **"Software pricing not being available on your website
continues to be the number one reason (54%) buyers would be less likely to purchase from you,"**
**"72% of buyers indicated access to transparent pricing made them more likely to purchase a product,"**
and **38% say "having to contact sales would also make them less likely to purchase"**
([solutions.trustradius.com](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/2023-b2b-disconnect/)).
Six of the nine incumbent pages fetched today gate price behind a form. Publishing is worth more than
the margin it costs.

**D6 — The page must be felt in one scan, and it must be written for a foreman, not a CFO.**
79% of NN/g's test users "always scanned any new page they came across; only 16 percent read
word-by-word", and web copy at half the word count of conventional writing plus scannable layout plus
objective language measured **124% better usability** combined
([nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)). Unbounce's Conversion
Benchmark Report finds **"Landing pages written at a 5th to 7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%,
56% higher than pages written at an 8th to 9th grade level"** and "more than 2x higher than
professional-level writing which converts at 5.3%", with word count correlating **−18.6%** against
conversion ([unbounce.com](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/)). The brief's "under 450
words above the pricing block" is not an aesthetic preference; it is the measured direction of travel.

## 2. The buyer, and the moment the money moves

Established elsewhere in the repo and used as given: the ICP is **small and mid-size specialty
subcontractors (electrical, plumbing/HVAC, concrete, masonry, painting, roofing, drywall, flooring,
steel, site work) on federally funded or state prevailing-wage construction — especially non-union firms
new to government work with no compliance department**, plus small GCs who must collect and audit their
subs' payrolls (`phase-3-acquisition/prospects/wagelens/README.md`, 10,749 organisations collected
2026-09-03). The sharpest rows in that file are firms with *many small awards*, not few large ones —
"33 federal subawards, $1.6M since 2024 … the worst possible ratio of paperwork to revenue, which is
the buying trigger."

Three things about this buyer decide the offer:

1. **The purchase is triggered by a calendar, not by a crisis.** Unlike the phase-1 Reinstate case
   (panic buying, willingness-to-pay spikes then collapses), Davis-Bacon willingness-to-pay is
   *recurring and scheduled*: a certified payroll is due **"weekly, for each week in which any DBA- or
   Related Acts-covered work is performed"** (29 CFR 5.5(a)(3)(ii)(A)). **This is the single most
   favourable structural fact about the business** — the pain re-presents itself every seven days, so
   a subscription is the natural container rather than a retrofit, and a trial that spans one real
   Friday is worth more than fourteen idle days.
2. **The buyer is not the person who reads marketing copy.** He is an owner-operator, an office
   manager, or the owner's spouse. Unbounce's reading-level finding (5th–7th grade converts at 11.1%
   vs 5.3% at professional level) is not a stylistic note for this audience; it is the specification.
3. **He is afraid of being wrong, and he has no one to ask.** The DOL's own conformance process exists
   because the classification list is routinely incomplete: a conformance is required "only when the
   type of work needed for a project is **not** performed by a labor classification **already** listed
   on the applicable wage determination", and where a classification is missing, workers must still
   "be paid the prevailing wage for the labor classification of work actually performed"
   ([dol.gov conformance FAQ](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/construction/faq/conformance)).
   That is a judgement call the buyer is legally required to make and is not equipped to make.

**A caution on §2 as a whole.** PERSONA.md did not exist when this was written (see §7, gap G4). The
above is inferred from the prospect file, the regulations and vendor-mediated sources. It is a
hypothesis with good provenance, not measured buyer research.

### 3.5 The one competitor that changes the plan

**CertifiedPayrollPro** matches WageLens's positioning almost exactly ("Generate federal WH-347 and
state certified payroll reports in minutes, not hours"), publishes its prices, undercuts the proposed
$79–99 tier at $49, offers "14-day free trial with 3 free reports included. No credit card required",
charges "$0 setup", and includes state forms for CA, IL and NY plus a subcontractor portal
([certifiedpayrollpro.com](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/) and
[/pricing](https://www.certifiedpayrollpro.com/pricing)).

Three honest observations:

1. ~~**Their published feature list contains no wage-determination lookup and no determination-change
   alert.**~~ **WRONG — corrected in §3.6.** The pricing page's feature list does not name one, but the
   product has a free public lookup and state-level change alerts on separate pages. Reading a feature
   list is not reading a product. The surviving gap is narrower and better: **the revision** (§3.6).
2. **They charge per report on top of the subscription** ($5 / $3 / $1). A sub filing four projects ×
   52 weeks = 208 reports/yr pays $49×12 + 208×$5 = **$1,628/yr** on Starter, or $99×12 + 208×$3 =
   **$1,812/yr** on Pro. A flat WageLens price of $99/mo is **$1,188/yr with no per-report meter** —
   *cheaper in the real usage pattern of our sharpest prospects despite a higher sticker*. This is the
   pricing argument, and it is arithmetic rather than assertion.
3. **They are the reason not to lead with "transparent pricing."** Against them, transparency is
   parity. Against the other six, it is an advantage. Say it once, in the pricing block, and spend the
   headline on the rate.

## 4. What converts for this buyer — the evidence

### 4.1 Scanning: the page is looked at, not read

- **"79 percent of our test users always scanned any new page they came across; only 16 percent read
  word-by-word."** Web writing should use "half the word count (or less) than conventional writing".
  Measured against a promotional control: concise text **+58%** usability, scannable layout **+47%**,
  objective language **+27%**, all three combined **+124%**. And: "promotional language imposes a
  cognitive burden." ([nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/))
- **F-shaped scanning**, from eyetracking of **232 users** across thousands of pages: two horizontal
  sweeps then a vertical scan down the left. "**The first two paragraphs must state the most important
  information**", and headers, paragraphs and bullets should begin with "information-carrying words".
  A follow-up study eleven years later confirmed the findings held.
  ([nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/))

**Consequence for LANDING_SPEC.md:** a hard word budget, left-aligned information-carrying openers on
every heading and bullet, no promotional adjectives, and the whole argument survivable by someone who
reads only the first line of each section.

### 4.2 Length and reading level

- Unbounce's Conversion Benchmark Report: **"Landing pages written at a 5th to 7th grade reading level
  convert at 11.1%, 56% higher than pages written at an 8th to 9th grade level"** and "more than 2x
  higher than professional-level writing which converts at 5.3%". Correlations against conversion:
  difficult words (3+ syllables) **−24.3%**, reading time **−19.4%**, word count **−18.6%**; and
  "the negative correlation between difficult words and conversion rates is 62% stronger than it was
  in 2020". Median conversion across nine industries **6.6%**, industry medians **3.8%–12.3%**.
  ([unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/))
- Copyhackers' corrective, which prevents over-reading the above: **"Copy length is a red herring.
  It's a distraction from what's actually important: forming a persuasive argument that gets your ideal
  visitors to take the next step."** Length is set by five factors — visitor awareness stage, market
  sophistication, intent and context, risk/price/complexity, and number of personas — and for fast
  decision-makers the guidance is scannable hero copy, "value-based crossheads (not 'Features')",
  third-party proof early. ([copyhackers.com](https://copyhackers.com/2020/09/saas-websites-is-shorter-copy-really-better/))
- CXL's landing page anatomy: headline → subheadline → hero visual → benefit copy → social proof → CTA;
  "a quick explanation of your offer *above the fold*"; **"There should be only _one_ possible action
  for the visitor to take"**; duplicate the CTA at the bottom if the page scrolls; place proof "near
  your CTAs". ([cxl.com](https://cxl.com/blog/how-to-build-a-high-converting-landing-page/))

**Reading the three together.** Our buyer is *problem-aware and solution-unaware*: he knows Friday
hurts and does not know a lookup-plus-form product exists. Copyhackers' factor 1 says that needs
education, which argues *longer*; Unbounce says every extra word costs, which argues *shorter*. The
resolution the brief already reaches for is the right one: **carry the education in pictures, not
paragraphs.** Diagrams and a live widget are not counted by a word-count correlation and are not read
at 8th-grade level — they are seen. Hence the ≤450-word budget above the pricing block *plus* three to
five worked visuals.

### 4.3 Pricing transparency

TrustRadius, 2023 B2B Buying Disconnect, **n=1,604 technology buyers, surveyed March 2023**:
**"Software pricing not being available on your website continues to be the number one reason (54%)
buyers would be less likely to purchase from you"**; **"72% of buyers indicated access to transparent
pricing made them more likely to purchase a product"**; **"100% of buyers want to self-serve all or
part of the buying journey"**; and **38%** say "having to contact sales would also make them less
likely to purchase". ([solutions.trustradius.com](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/2023-b2b-disconnect/))

Against a field where six of nine vendors demand a form before a number, this is the cheapest available
conversion gain: publish, and let the page say plainly that there is no demo call.

### 4.4 Trial design

Kyle Poyar / Growth Unhinged, **n=200 B2B software products**:
- "The median free-to-paid conversion rate across all products is **8%**"
- **"Free trials that require a credit card see 30% free-to-paid conversion – more than 5x ones that
  don't require one"**
- "**57%** of products have a free trial as their primary landing point for new customers, more than
  twice the rate of freemium (**26%**)"
- "The most common trial length is **14 days** (**62%** of products)"
- "Among free trial products, **20%** require users to provide a credit card upfront"
([growthunhinged.com](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/how-to-improve-free-to-paid-conversion))

**The WageLens-specific reading.** The 30%-with-card figure is the strongest single lever in the
dataset, but it collides with two local facts: (a) our nearest competitor advertises "No credit card
required" as a feature, so a card wall is a visible disadvantage in a side-by-side; (b) **our value
event is not time-based, it is event-based — it is the first completed Friday.** A 14-day trial and a
"first WH-347 free" trial are not the same instrument: the second guarantees the buyer experiences the
outcome before deciding, and it is unforgeable proof that we know what the product is for. `OFFER.md`
resolves this as a card-on-file trial framed by the event rather than the clock.

### 4.5 Pricing-page structure — the weakest evidence here, flagged

The commonly repeated claims — 60–70% of buyers pick the middle of three tiers; decoy structures lift
willingness-to-pay 13–26% "according to Profitwell's analysis of over 5,000 SaaS companies"; a CXL
study showing decoys lifting higher-tier conversion "by up to 30%"; Price Intelligently on size-based
segmentation giving "20% higher average revenue per user" — are reachable today only through
aggregator articles that name the source but not the study
([getmonetizely.com](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/the-decoy-effect-how-strategic-pricing-tiers-can-maximize-revenue)).
The primary sources are gone: `priceintelligently.com/blog/pricing-page-design` 404s (Paddle retired
the blog) and OpenView's pricing-transparency study is offline with the firm.
**Treated as folklore with a plausible mechanism, not as evidence.** Three tiers are adopted because
they are legible on a phone and because our segments genuinely differ (solo sub / multi-project sub /
GC roll-up), **not** because of a cited lift.

### 4.6 The two offer frameworks

- **Hormozi's value equation**, stated as
  **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**,
  with the four terms defined as "How will this improve my life?", "How likely is this to happen?",
  "How long will this take?", "How much work will this take?" — and the operative note:
  **"Most products focus on the top of the equation, but the bottom is where the magic is."**
  ([creatoreconomy.so](https://creatoreconomy.so/p/the-value-equation-for-irresistible-products) —
  **secondary**; acquisition.com's own pages 404 or carry no equation text, §7 gap G3.)
- **Suby's eight phases**: 1. Understand and Identify Your Dream Buyer · 2. Create the Perfect Bait
  for Your Dream Buyer · 3. Capture Leads and Get Contact Details · **4. The Godfather Strategy** ·
  5. Drive Traffic · 6. The Magic Lantern Technique · 7. Convert Sales · 8. Automate and Multiply
  ([aestudio.au](https://aestudio.au/sabri-suby-is-daddy-our-sell-like-crazy-review/)). The Godfather
  offer is "an offer so compelling that it becomes almost impossible for the target audience to refuse",
  built from "enhanced value propositions, bonuses, scarcity, and urgency tactics" (ibid.), and more
  operationally: "presenting offers so compelling that prospects can't refuse. Focus on benefits,
  create credible and valuable offers, include attractive bonuses, and provide strong guarantees"; the
  **High-Value Content Offer** is content that "exchange[s] valuable content for simple contact details
  like names and emails" and "should be simple, relevant, and address pressing issues"
  ([jessenyokabi.substack.com](https://jessenyokabi.substack.com/p/sell-like-crazy-how-to-get-as-many)).
  Both are **secondary** sources; Suby's own godfather page returned HTTP 503 on two attempts.

**Note on the "would they be stupid to say no" test.** The brief names it; the strongest attributed

### 6.2 Hormozi's value equation, scored honestly for this buyer

### 6.3 Suby's test applied to the three candidate offers

*"Would they be stupid to say no?"* — asked honestly of a sub filing four projects a week.

- **Angle A (Friday Machine) alone:** No. He can buy the same thing for $49 from a competitor with a
  no-card trial. He would not be stupid to say no; he would be shopping.
- **Angle C (Audit Shield) alone:** No. Fear without a mechanism. He has survived so far.
- **Angle B (The Rate Is Right) + A as proof + a free first WH-347:** **Closer to yes.** The offer
  becomes: *see the rate for your county before you sign up; pin the revision your contract locked;
  file this Friday's form free; when that determination is revised we name the classification that
  moved.* He is asked to pay only after a real deadline has passed successfully.
  **Post-verification caveat (§3.6):** the free lookup is no longer "the number he cannot otherwise
  get" — he can get it in two other places for nothing. What he cannot get anywhere else is the
  revision pinned to his contract and reproducible during the three-year retention window. **The
  "stupid to say no" weight now sits on durability, not on access.**

The honest verdict: **this is a strong offer, not an irresistible one**, and the missing ingredient is
not copy — it is the guarantee (`OFFER.md` §5) and the proof that the rate is right (`LANDING_SPEC.md`
§ visuals). Recording that judgement here so the wave-1b reviewer can attack it.
