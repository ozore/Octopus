## 01 Mandate & demand — verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED

# C4 — "Filing Period" — Deep Validation, 2026-08-20

## Headline

The candidate's factual base is the cleanest I have re-verified in this run: **every single number in the card reproduced exactly** from an independent fetch. But the card's *urgency thesis* and one of its two differentiators-vs-ERP are both wrong or expiring, and the miner missed a Bill introduced three months ago that reshapes the regime. **SURVIVES_RESHAPED.**

## What re-verified exactly

I downloaded the DBT register myself (`https://check-payment-practices.service.gov.uk/export/csv/`, HTTP 200, `content-type: text/csv`, `content-disposition: filename="2026-08-20-0414-prompt-payments.csv"`, 102,349,420 bytes) and parsed it cold. Reproduced to the digit: **52 columns; 114,624 reports; 10,235 distinct company numbers; 10,762 reports and 6,340 distinct companies filed 1 Jan–20 Aug 2026; 3,762 filing 2+; regime split 6,236/3,794/732; monthly counts 3,348/608/715/1,319/462/388/3,444/478; 2,925 (27.2%) filed >30 days after period end; 2,309 of 10,044 (23.0%) average-time-to-pay >45 days; 802 (8.0%) >60.** Max filing date 2026-08-19 — the corpus is alive to yesterday. The three worked examples verify row-for-row (ABCAM 186 days, MABEY HIRE 123, BOYS & BODEN 94).

I also **strengthened** the late-filing claim the card hedged as "upper bound": 10,456 of 10,762 periods are standard six-month periods, so the adjusted-period caveat is negligible; **1,917 of 6,340 distinct 2026 filers (30.2%)** filed at least one late report; 860 were >180 days late. Reg 2's 30-day filing period has no extension mechanism, so this is genuine breach, not artefact.

Primary text verified verbatim, not from commentary: reg 8(1) ("the qualifying company **and every person who was a director** ... commits an offence"), reg 8(2) reasonable-steps defence, reg 8(3) fine / level 5; reg 2 "filing period" = 30 days. SI 2024/444 reg 2(2) substitutes **"6th April 2031"** for "6th April 2024" and reg 2(3) moves review to 2029; reg 2(4) inserts para 9(c) sum totals and 10A/10B. SI 2025/1152 (Companies (Directors' Report) (Payment Reporting) Regulations 2025) exists, in force 1 Jan 2026, effect for FYs beginning on/after 1 Jan 2026. Thresholds £54m/£27m/250 from 6 April 2025 confirmed on the guidance page (updated 2 September 2025).

## What breaks

**1. The criminal-liability urgency is theatre. Nine years, zero prosecutions.** The card's one-liner sells "the 30-day statutory filing period whose breach is a criminal offence for the company and every director." True on the page — but a minister told Parliament (Jan 2025 debate on the No. 2 Regulations) "**There has not been a prosecution as yet**," and DBT's practice per its own guidance is to write to non-compliers, not prosecute. Meanwhile **DBT's own official statistics (published 14 July 2026) say 14,000–16,000 businesses were required to report in 2024 — implying 28,000–32,000 expected reports — against 11,178 valid reports in 2025.** So roughly **60% of required reports are simply never filed, and nothing happens.** A mandate that ~5,000–9,000 companies openly ignore with zero consequence is a weak willingness-to-pay signal, not a strong one. The card never tested enforcement at all.

**2. The Commercial Payments Bill [HL], introduced 19 May 2026 — entirely missed.** GOV.UK's own factsheet confirms it: large companies keep half-yearly reporting; **new secondary legislation will require them to disclose "the amount of interest they have paid and the interest they owe"**; boards/audit committees of persistent late payers must publish commentary; and **the Small Business Commissioner gains power to "enforce compliance with statutory reporting requirements."** Secondary reporting (Macfarlanes, 2 July 2026) puts the penalty at **up to 1% of annual UK turnover**. Two-edged: it converts an unenforced criminal offence into a real civil regulator with real money (tailwind, ~2027–28), and it guarantees another spec revision (tailwind for a maintained generator). But —

**3. The construction retention block has a legislated death sentence.** The Bill **prohibits retention deductions in construction contracts**; retention clauses, new and pre-existing, **become void after a two-year transition**. The card leans on retention as a key differentiator ("neither ERP documents any construction retention field from SI 2025/75"). That differentiator is already thin — only **1,460 of 10,762 2026 reports flag qualifying construction contracts and only 818 carry a retention ratio (7.6%)** — and it is scheduled to disappear.

**4. Premise misstatements (kill pattern 4, partial hit).** The card says the retention block is "mandatory from 1 Mar 2025." SI 2025/75 reg 1(2) does come into force 1 March 2025, but **reg 1(4): "The amendments made by regulations 2 to 7 apply in relation to each financial year of a company beginning on or after 1st April 2025."** DBT's guidance says the same. Separately, "DBT's 52-field published spec" is a conflation: 52 is the column count of the *register export*, and five of those columns (Report Id, Policy Regime, Company, Company number, URL) are registry metadata no filer enters. There is no DBT-published "52-field spec."

**5. GTM hole created by finding 1.** The card's acquisition plan is register-driven — benchmark pages and dated outbound to the 10,235 companies in the corpus. But per DBT's statistics the acute non-compliers are the ~40–60% who **never appear in the register at all**. The card's list reaches only the people already doing the job.

## What held under attack

**Pattern 1 (free substitute): holds, with a named forward risk.** I re-fetched the publish service and `/decide/` (both HTTP 200) — eligibility checker and publication form only, no calculator, template, or upload. DBT's own engineering blog (18 March 2025) describes the service rebuild (relaunched 6 Dec 2024) and mentions **no bulk upload, CSV import, calculator or API** — but DBT now owns a modern, extensible codebase and the Bill forces another round of changes. Import is one sprint away from being their problem to solve.

**Pattern 2 (micro-SaaS): holds.** Four independent hunts (dedicated tool, SaaS pricing, directory, outsourced service) surfaced no dedicated product and no published price. Forvis Mazars' service line is live and verbatim as quoted — though its page still says "BEIS," an abolished department, so it may be years stale.

**Pattern 3 (bundling): holds, and I widened the test.** I checked a third ERP the miner missed — **Dynamics 365 Business Central**, the mid-market product that actually serves this buyer. Its Payment Practices report is a **generic global feature** (Sweden/UK/AU/NZ) producing only average agreed payment period, average actual payment term and % paid within agreed period — **no UK speed bands, no value statistics, no retention fields**, and the doc is dated 2024-06-21 (updated 2025-05-07). Bundling risk is therefore *narrower* than feared on the count-based side. Untested and worrying: SI 2025/1152 puts payment data in the **directors' report**, which the company's auditor/accountant already prepares — artifact (3) is plausibly inside a fee the buyer already pays.

**Patterns 5, 6, 7: clean.** One jurisdiction, one GET, no scraper fleet. Rows live to 2026-08-19. All outputs are arithmetic over customer rows against published formulas; the director signs.

### 2. Input side: one AP ledger CSV is NOT sufficient — the sharpest break

Three failures, verified against statute:

- **Retention Sum B is receivables-side data.** SI 2025/75 Sch 2 para 10 defines sum B as "monies deducted or retained pursuant to retention clauses in qualifying construction contracts between the qualifying company and **its clients**". That is retention withheld *against* the company by its customers — it is not in any purchase ledger. Live scale: 1,460 of 6,236 Regime-3 2026 reports flag construction contracts; 818 populate the retention percentage. So ~13% of filers need a second, non-AP input the card does not name.
- **"Payment made" is not the AP payment date.** SI 2024/444 reg 2(4)(d) substitutes Schedule para 14: a payment is made "when it is **received by the supplier**", or, under supply-chain finance, when received by the finance provider. An AP ledger records the run/clearing date. This is a systematic bias, not an edge case.
- **Directors' report block needs consolidation, not one entity's CSV.** SI 2025/1152 Sch 7 Part 9 para 30 exempts a subsidiary included in a parent's group directors' report, and makes group figures cover "the company and its subsidiary undertakings included in the consolidation". Full-year, group-level, from multiple ledgers.

The receipt-date rule itself verifies: guidance states receipt "is not the date on which the invoice is entered on to the business's software system, unless this process happens on the same day". Microsoft's own doc concedes the default: D365's `Receive document date` defaults to invoice date. Exclusions verify (financial services; residential occupier under s.106(2) HGCRA 1996; intra-entity; fully credit-noted). These are legitimately one-time customer settings — but Sum B is data, not a setting.

### 3. Reference data and what A4 refreshes

Effectively nothing external is required for the arithmetic — this is a **pure calculator over the customer's own rows against a published formula**, which is the safest possible engine-never-arbiter posture and the reason reg 9 discipline is achievable. The refresh loop is therefore thin: one nightly GET of a single CSV (for benchmarking only) plus weekly diffs of four SIs and two guidance pages. Staleness risk is spec amendment, and it is real and frequent: thresholds moved 6 Apr 2025, guidance updated 5 Mar 2025 and 2 Sep 2025, value stats from 2024/444, retention from 2025/75, directors' report from 2025/1152. Clean A4 pass on kill pattern 5 — one jurisdiction, one regulator, no scraper fleet. The flip side: a thin refresh loop is a thin moat.

### 4. Buyer enumeration — the card's acquisition claim is overstated

DBT's own official statistics (published 14 Jul 2026) say **11,178 valid reports in 2025** against **14,000–16,000 businesses required to report in 2024**, i.e. ~28,000–32,000 expected reports a year. The register therefore names roughly a third to a half of the legally obliged population. **"Every buyer is named in a free OGL CSV" is false for the majority of the market.** Worse, the enumerable half is shrinking: distinct filers by year from my own parse — 7,538 (2019), 6,969, 6,712, 6,462, 6,307, 6,676, 6,615 (2025), 6,340 (2026 YTD); first-time filers only 408 in 2025 and 297 in 2026 YTD. The April-2025 threshold uplift (36m to 54m) shrinks it further. Realistic addressable unit volume is ~13,000 reports/yr across ~6,600 entities — a gross ceiling near £3.2M/yr at £249/report even at 100% penetration.

Finding the must-file-but-haven't cohort is computable but not cheap: Companies House **Accounts Data Product** is live and free (`Accounts_Bulk_Data-2026-08-19.zip`, 200, `application/zip`, 100,520,150 B, last-modified 2026-08-19), giving iXBRL instance docs from which turnover / balance sheet / employees can be tagged. That is a real, buildable prospecting asset — and it is the single highest-leverage thing the card missed.

The defensible triggers are the fixed calendar, the 6.9% of Regime-2/3 filers leaving value fields blank, and the 8% filing more than 180 days late.

DBT's own **Final Stage Impact Assessment (Feb 2024, DBT024(F)-23-BG)** prices this job per business per year, central estimates, Option 3 (the enacted option):

| | Non-construction | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare reports twice yearly | £759 | £1,051 |
| Collate, approve, submit twice yearly | £408 | £534 |
| Maintain systems | £118 | £118 |
| **Total ongoing** | **£1,285** | **£1,702** |

The addressable slice (prepare + collate) is **£1,167/yr** non-construction. The card's **£99/month = £1,188/yr** is ~102% of the entire government-estimated burden — priced above the pain it removes. The **£249 one-off per entity-report (£498/yr)** is ~43% and defensible. Same IA: **"A number of respondents claimed there would be no ongoing costs associated with preparing the reports once systems had been established"** — the honest deflator on DIY pain. It also fixes in-scope population at **10,300 businesses**.

## What breaks

**Enforcement is theatre, so far.** DBT's Statutory Review (5 Apr 2022): "Government has initiated the formal enforcement process against several businesses … **the initiation of the process alone has been sufficient to lead to full and swift compliance in all cases**", and it concedes "concerns around a perceived lack of enforcement". I found no prosecution, no fine, ever, across 2017–2026. The criminal-liability urgency in the card is legally accurate and commercially soft.

**The card's own data refutes one of its pain signals.** The "696 Regime-2/3 reports with a blank value field" — I re-derived it: 658 have *all four* value fields blank, and **all 658 are nil returns** (`Qualifying contracts in reporting period = False`, `Payments made = False`). Zero reports that made payments omitted the new value stats. Field completion is essentially 100%; the retention block is likewise complete (1,460 Regime-3 2026 reports with construction contracts, 819 with retention clauses, **1** blank ratio). There is no data-quality distress visible in the register.

**Late-filing, refined.** Of the 27.2%: 6.9pp is 1-3 days late (mostly a 466-strong cohort filing 31 July for a 30 June period-end), 9.2pp is >90 days (backfills). **1,917 of 6,340 companies (30.2%)** filed at least one late report in 2026 — still a real signal, differently shaped.

**Premise slip.** SI 2025/75 comes into force 1 March 2025, but reg 1(4): "The amendments made by regulations 2 to 7 apply in relation to each financial year of a company **beginning on or after 1st April 2025**." The card's "mandatory from 1 Mar 2025" is wrong.

**A genuine spec ambiguity, in the product's favour.** Sch 2 para 10(1) requires a percentage "of the **difference between**" sum A and sum B, but para 10(2) renders the formula as `sum A / sum B × 100`. Words and formula disagree; DBT guidance resolves to the ratio. This is exactly the encoded-spec work ERPs decline.

**New: the Commercial Payments Bill** (GOV.UK factsheet, 19 May 2026, introduced to Parliament May 2026). Tailwind: adds duties to "report interest paid and owed" via secondary legislation, plus board commentary for persistent late payers. Headwind: "prohibits deduction and withholding of retention payments" — which eventually deletes the construction-retention differentiator — and a 60-day statutory maximum.

**TAM.** Like-for-like Jan1–Aug20: 2025 = 10,489 reports/6,338 companies; 2026 = 10,762/6,340. **Flat**, and flat since 2020 (6,300–6,700 filers/yr). But the April-2025 threshold uplift moves ~6,000 companies economy-wide from large to medium; the two-balance-sheet test means drop-out lands from 2027.

## 04 Kill-thesis — verdict: REFUTED

## C4 — Filing Period: deep validation, 2026-08-20

**Verdict: REFUTED at the proposed shape.** Every factual claim on the card checks out — I re-derived them from the raw corpus rather than trusting it. What kills it is economics the miner did not measure: the regulator's own Impact Assessment prices the *entire annual obligation* below the product's entry subscription, and the compulsion that would force a budget has never once been exercised in nine years, on ministerial record.

### What survived re-verification (all fetched today)

I downloaded the DBT register myself (HTTP 200, 102,349,420 bytes, `content-disposition: 2026-08-20-0434-prompt-payments.csv`). Independently reproduced: **114,624 reports, 10,235 distinct company numbers, 52 columns**; 2026 filings 10,762 across 6,340 companies; regime split 6,236/3,794/732; **2,925 of 10,762 (27.2%) filed >30 days after period end**; 2,309 of 10,044 (23.0%) average-days >45; 802 (8.0%) >60. Every number matches the card exactly. Rows are current (478 filings in Aug 2026) — no dead data. Reg 8 verified verbatim from legislation.gov.uk: offence by "the qualifying company and every person who was a director… immediately before the end of the filing period". DBT guidance (updated 2 Sept 2025) carries the receipt-date rule, the bands and both retention formulas and offers **no calculator, template or spreadsheet**; the publish service offers no CSV/bulk upload ("Once you've prepared all your report data, you must publish your report online"). Kill patterns 1, 5 and 6 genuinely pass. No micro-SaaS, no directory listing, **no published price anywhere** — I hunted vendors, G2/Capterra, Product Hunt, GitHub, trade bodies and accountancy fee pages. Pattern 2 "passes" only in the sense that nobody is here at all.

### Kill 1 — the price ceiling is published, and it sits below the price (fatal)

DBT's **Final Stage Impact Assessment (Feb 2024)**, fetched and text-extracted today, monetises the ongoing per-business burden: *Maintain systems and processes* £106/£118/£130; **Prepare reports twice yearly £630/£700/£770**; **Collate, approve and submit reports twice yearly £339/£377/£414**; **Total £1,076 / £1,195 / £1,315 per business per year** (construction variants £1,285 and £1,702).

Filing Period's £99/month Single Entity = **£1,188/yr ≈ 99.4% of the government's central estimate of the whole annual cost of the obligation** — including the director's approval time, which the product explicitly does not do. The £249 one-off per entity-report is 71% of the estimated *annual* cost of preparing both reports. No controller buys a tool priced at the full cost of the problem it removes. Worse, the IA states: "A number of respondents claimed there would be **no ongoing costs** associated with preparing the reports once systems had been established" and "Over 50% of respondents… felt there would be **no cost** associated with maintaining systems and processes". The £1,195 is a *median from 36 telephone interviews in 2016*, inflated forward. The only recorded figure for actual current spend is footnote 20: "**Less than 5 responses, ranging between £3,000 and £15,000 per annum**" — the entire observable willingness-to-pay evidence for this category, n<5, spanning 5x.

### Kill 2 — enforcement is nil and admitted on the record (fatal to the forced budget)

Hansard API, fetched today. **Gareth Thomas MP (Minister, DBT), Delegated Legislation Committee, 15 January 2025**: "regulations on reporting did come into force back in 2017. To be blunt and to be candid in this forum, **there has not been much enforcement of those regulations**. We are seeking to put that right, and have written to over 500 firms… **There has not been a prosecution as yet**." **Lord Leong, 15 October 2025**: "we are currently writing to companies… Out of the more than 500 companies contacted so far, over half have already returned to compliance."

Set against DBT's own **Official Statistics of 14 July 2026**: "DBT analysis estimates that **between 14,000 and 16,000 businesses were required to report in 2024**. If each business submitted 2 reports annually, this would equate to between 28,000 and 32,000 expected reports." Actual: **11,178 valid reports in 2025** (11,954 in 2024). That is roughly **40% compliance with zero prosecutions in nine years**, enforced by ~500 letters. The card's 27.2% late figure therefore does not describe "a quarter of the market struggling" — it describes lateness *within the already-compliant minority*, inside a population where ~60% of required reports are simply never filed and nothing happens. The criminal liability is real in law and inert in practice.

### Kill 3 — the group-pricing rationale misreads the card's own corpus

The card justifies per-entity-per-period pricing with "3,762 of the 6,340 companies published 2 or more reports in 2026 — group structures where each qualifying subsidiary files separately." I recomputed: of those 3,762, **3,492 filed exactly two**, and 3,722 had all-distinct reporting periods. That is the *normal twice-yearly cadence of one entity*, not group structure — subsidiaries carry their own company numbers and are already inside the 6,340. Only ~270 companies filed 3+. The tier that survives the price-ceiling arithmetic (£349 Group) rests on a statistic that does not say what the card says.

### Kill 4 — attention is bimodal, not year-round

**73.1% of 2026-filed reports cover periods ending in June or December**; **63.1% of 2026 filings landed in January + July alone**, 75.4% adding April + October. A monthly subscription is being charged for two ~2-week bursts of use; renewal is billed ten times a year for zero usage. Per-filing pricing is the only shape the data supports — and per-filing pricing collides head-on with the £350-per-report regulator estimate.

### Kill 5 — a live Bill the card missed, aimed at its differentiator

**Commercial Payments Bill [HL]** (Bill 4128; bills-api shows **Lords Report stage, last updated 2026-08-14**), introduced 19 May 2026. GOV.UK's overview factsheet: it will "prohibit the deduction and withholding of **retention payments** under the terms of a construction contract"; add reporting of "the amount of interest they have paid and the interest they owe"; require audit-committee commentary from persistent late payers; and give the Small Business Commissioner power to "enforce compliance with statutory reporting requirements" including financial penalties. Two consequences: the **construction retention block — the card's single strongest differentiator over Sage X3 and D365 — is legislatively targeted for abolition**, and the spec is about to move again. (The SBC penalty power is the one fact that could *create* the forced budget, but on unstated lead-in time and not yet law.)

### Kill 6 — ERP bundling is closer than stated

D365 F&O doc (ms.date 2026-05-12, updated 2026-05-13) confirms count-based statistics only and ends "use the report data to prepare the final report" — the card is right. But it also already exports "details per vendor account… details of each payment made during a reporting period" (that *is* a derivation workbook) and already exposes the receipt-date choice as a parameter (**Invoice accounting date / Invoice date / Invoice receive date**) — the card sells both as differentiators. Business Central additionally ships a generic global "Payment Practices" report (UK/Sweden/Australia/NZ) with drill-through to "Payment Practice Data List" calculations. Microsoft has the ledger, the workbook and the lineage; it is missing only the value sums and the artifact assembly.

### The narrow reshape that is not yet disproved

Per-filing pricing (£150–£350 per entity-report), sold *through* accountancy service lines (Forvis Mazars-type) and group shared-service centres, timed to the January and July spikes, aimed at the £3k–£15k/yr self-reported tail. TAM: 12,680 filings/yr against a flat-to-shrinking base (7,538 distinct filers in 2019 → 6,340 in 2026 YTD; only 450 genuinely new filers in 2026; DBT attributes the 2025 −6.5% drop to the threshold uplift). That reshape has zero price evidence behind it and must be priced before it is built.
