## 01 Mandate & demand

## B8 — PortfolioWatch UK: deep validation (fetched 2026-08-19)

### The pain is real, statutory, and well documented

Every load-bearing element of the pain narrative verified against primary GOV.UK text today.

**Penalty schedule** (gov.uk/government/publications/late-filing-penalties, page last updated 16 January 2026): private company/LLP penalties are £150 (≤1 month late), £375 (1–3 months), £750 (3–6 months), £1,500 (>6 months); public companies £750/£1,500/£3,000/£7,500. The doubling rule is live and quoted verbatim: *"The late filing penalty will be doubled if accounts are filed late in 2 successive financial years (beginning on or after 6 April 2008)."* Director liability is explicit on the same page: *"Not filing your accounts or confirmation statements is a criminal offence"* and *"Directors or LLP designated members could be personally fined for this in the criminal courts."*

**Confirmation statement** (gov.uk/guidance/confirmation-statement-guidance, last updated 21 April 2026): *"You can be fined up to £5,000 and your company may be struck off if you do not file your confirmation statement."* Filing window is 14 days after the review period ends.

**Volume** (independent adjudicators' report to Companies House 2024–25, GOV.UK): *"In 2024 to 2025, Companies House issued a total of 317,985 penalties for the late filing of accounts"*, of which *"47,673 penalties were appealed to the first stage, which is around one in seven."* Register size 5.43m at 31 March 2025; 726,813 dissolved companies in the year (CH annual report 2024–25).

**But the counter-number is in the same primary source.** The CH annual report gives *"Rate of annual accounts filed by companies as at 31 March 2025: 98.5%"* and *"Rate of confirmation statements filed... 97.1%."* The failure mode this product sells against affects 1.5–2.9% of the register, and disproportionately the unadvised owner-director — not the client book of a practice that already has a compliance process.

**ECCTA tailwind is genuine but slower than assumed.** Identity verification became a legal requirement 18 November 2025 (changestoukcompanylaw.campaign.gov.uk/identity-verification), with real teeth: *"If you do not comply with identity verification requirements on time, you'll be committing an offence"* and *"You will not be able to make any filings for your company or start a new company."* The at-a-glance timeline confirms 1 February 2026 fee changes. Critically, the accounts shake-up the candidate would ride is dated **1 April 2028**, not 2026: software-only iXBRL filing, abridged accounts abolished, mandatory P&L for small/micro. The campaign site does **not** state the 18 November 2026 deadline for existing directors, nor the confirmation-statement-linked verification trigger — those appear only in law-firm commentary I did not accept as primary.

### The fatal problem: the buyer already has this, free, from the source

The candidate's own risk register names "a free follow-a-company alert" as unverified. It is verified, and it is worse than feared — there are **two** free Companies House services that between them cover nearly the whole promise:

1. **Free email reminders** (gov.uk/guidance/register-for-email-reminders-from-companies-house, 200, 77,343 bytes): free, alerts when *"your company's accounts and confirmation statements are due"*, **up to 4 email addresses per company**. Paper reminders have been discontinued. This is the deadline half — the product's core — given away by the registrar, and an accountant who already files for a client has the authentication to register their own address.
2. **Follow** (verified live: the company page at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/00445790 contains a "Follow this company" link to follow.company-information.service.gov.uk, which 302s to signin). Per gov.uk/guidance/searching-the-companies-house-register (last updated 9 June 2026): *"Follow is a free service... The alert tells you instantly what information has been filed as soon as we've accepted it."* No documented company limit. This is the officer/PSC/status/charges half.

Follow is event-driven and cannot detect the *absence* of a filing — that residual (forecast a deadline, roll a portfolio up) is the only defensible slice, and free email reminders already occupy most of it.

### The market is not empty — it is crowded, and priced 3–10× below the hypothesis

Four live competitors doing precisely this, fetched today:

- **CHWatch** (chwatch.co.uk, 200): *"Built for accountants, RevOps teams, and analysts."* Deadline alerts at 30/14/7/1 days, officer/PSC/status/charge alerts, CSV import, Slack/webhook, calendar export, daily 08:30 digest. **Free (3 companies), £12/20, £30/100, £70/1,000 per month.** This is PortfolioWatch UK, shipped, with a free tier.
- **ComplyTrack** (complytrack.co.uk, 200): free for 2 companies; *"Built for the firm managing 200 companies, not 2"*; *"Join the UK accountants and advisors who let ComplyTrack watch Companies House so they don't have to."*
- **CompanyChangeAlerts** (companychangealerts.com, 200, Last-Modified 29 Apr 2026): £15/10, £35/25, £100/100 companies.
- **CompanyMinder** (companyminder.co.uk, 200): £9 first company + £5 each additional, deadline reminders at 30/14/7/1 days plus IDV tracking — aimed at directors, not accountants.

The observed market-clearing price for "monitor N UK companies and alert me" is **£0–£100/month**, against a $49–199/mo hypothesis. And the incumbent bundles are worse news: **Inform Direct** (informdirect.co.uk pricing, fetched today) is £10/mo first company, £0.69 each for 2–50, £0.52 for 51–250 — a 250-company practice pays **£147.81/mo** and gets *"Email reminders of key deadlines"* **plus unlimited confirmation statement filing, incorporations, share transactions and dormant/micro accounts filing.** Monitoring-only at a comparable price is strictly dominated.

### The comparator that justified the idea does not survive

The whole pricing thesis rests on undercutting Red Flag Alert's "£200/mo". That number is from a **Capterra UK listing** (still showing £200.00 today) — a secondary aggregator, with no attribution to the vendor. Red Flag Alert publishes **no** pricing page: redflagalert.com/pricing returns 404 and its sitemap.xml (200) contains no pricing/plans URL. Red Flag Alert is also a credit-risk/insolvency-scoring product, not a filing-deadline tracker — wrong category, unverifiable anchor.

### Engine-never-arbiter

The base product is clean: a JSON diff of two nightly API snapshots rendered into a fixed template, with nulls surfaced as "not yet reported by Companies House". No model produces a customer-facing number. **The moat strains it.** The proposed "per-company lateness baseline that compounds" is a derived risk signal, and CHWatch already ships the same concept as a *"Filing Risk Score"*. The moment that baseline is expressed as a score a credit team acts on, the engine becomes the arbiter of a solvency judgement on a named third party — with defamation and FCA-adjacent exposure. Any build must keep it to observed historical facts ("filed 3 days late in 2023, 5 in 2024"), never a score.

### Technical feasibility (only thing that holds up cleanly)

api.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/00445790 returns HTTP 401 `{"error":"Empty Authorization header","type":"ch:service"}` — live, real, free-key-gated. The rate-limiting guide at developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/guides/rateLimiting returns 200. Two-to-three week build is plausible. That is the problem: it is plausible for everyone, and four teams have already done it.

### Verdict

Pain verified, tailwind verified, buyer verified — and the wedge is gone. The registrar gives the core away free to the exact buyer, four funded-or-not competitors occupy the niche at £0–£100/mo, and the £200/mo price umbrella that made the economics work is a Capterra artifact for a different product category. Recommend **drop**, or radically re-scope onto the one thing no free service does: portfolio-level *director identity-verification* status tracking ahead of the ECCTA transition — which I could not price or verify demand for today.

## B8 — PortfolioWatch UK: deep validation (all checks run 2026-08-19)

### The data layer is real, free, and better than the card claims

Every technical assertion in the card holds, and two of them improve on it.

**REST API.** `GET https://api.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/00000006` returned **HTTP 401, `application/json`, 58 bytes**, body `{"error":"Empty Authorization header","type":"ch:service"}` with header `ch-authentication-error: Empty Authorization header`. Live, free-key-gated. **Rate limit confirmed verbatim** from Companies House's own guide: "You can make up to 600 requests within a five-minute period… you will receive a 429… your rate limit will reset back to its maximum value of 600 requests," plus "contact us" for an increase. That is 172,800 req/day per key; a 1,000-company portfolio polling profile + officers + PSC is 3,000 calls, ~25 minutes at cap. **Polling is not the constraint.**

**Fields.** The `companyProfile` resource documents `confirmation_statement.{last_made_up_to, next_due, next_made_up_to, overdue}` and `company_status` (string) as the card says. But **`accounts.next_due` is explicitly marked "Deprecated. Please use `accounts.next_accounts.due_on`"**, as are `accounts.next_made_up_to` and `accounts.overdue`. The card names the deprecated field. Build against `accounts.next_accounts.due_on` / `.overdue`. Endpoints `/company/{n}/officers`, `/company/{n}/persons-with-significant-control`, `/filing-history`, `/insolvency`, `/charges`, `/registers`, `/uk-establishments` all documented.

**Streaming API: exists, and it changes the product.** The developer-specs index lists seven products including "**Streaming API** — A streaming API giving access to real time data changes." The reference documents **nine streams**: `/companies`, `/filings`, `/insolvency-cases`, `/charges`, `/officers`, `/persons-with-significant-control`, `/disqualified-officers`, `/company-exemptions`, `/persons-with-significant-control-statements`, on `https://stream.companieshouse.gov.uk`. Each event arrives wrapped as `{event:{fields_changed[], published_at, timepoint, type}, resource_id, resource_kind, resource_uri, data:{}}` — **`fields_changed` is a server-side diff, i.e. the core of the product is handed to you free**. Constraints verified: **max two concurrent connections per account** (a third kills the oldest); blank-line heartbeats; resume by `timepoint`; `416` if the timepoint is too old or you consume too slowly; `429` → wait 60s or "the client IP address will be blocked." Keys are a **separate client type** ("stream key"), not interchangeable with REST keys, self-issued from the developer hub (three client types: API key, stream key, OAuth web client) — no approval call. Caveat: streams are the **whole-UK firehose**, not portfolio-filtered, so you consume everything and filter locally, and the 2-connection ceiling is a real HA constraint.

**Free bulk products, cadence verified against live files.** Free Company Data Product: monthly, "updated within 5 working days of the previous month end"; `BasicCompanyDataAsOneFile-2026-08-01.zip` returned **200, `application/zip`, content-length 493,049,031, Last-Modified Fri 07 Aug 2026 08:10:19 GMT**. PSC snapshot: the download page says "**All files are overwritten daily**… updated every morning before 10am GMT" — `persons-with-significant-control-snapshot-2026-08-19.zip` returned **200, 2,196,596,976 bytes, Last-Modified Wed 19 Aug 2026 07:08:47 GMT**, i.e. today. (Note: gov.uk's data-products guidance calls PSC "monthly"; the download page is primary and says daily.) Accounts data: daily Tue–Sat, each file retained 60 days.

### The free substitutes are stronger than the card assumed

Companies House ships **two** free services, not one, and the card only anticipated the weaker.

1. **Follow** — live: the company page renders `<a id="follow-this-company" href="https://follow.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/00000006/confirm-follow">Follow this company</a>` (signed out it bounces to the identity service). gov.uk: "Follow is a free service from Companies House which sends you email alerts of company transactions. The alert tells you instantly what information has been filed as soon as we've accepted it." **Filing-accepted only. No deadline reminders.** The card's read here was right.

2. **Email reminders** — the card missed this entirely, and it is the dangerous one. gov.uk: the service "tells you when your company's accounts and confirmation statements are due," it "is free," and you can "choose **up to 4 people to receive a reminder (including an agent)**" — with **bulk registration**: email a password-protected spreadsheet of ≥15 company numbers plus authentication codes to `BulkERems@companieshouse.gov.uk`. **Companies House already gives an accountancy practice free, bulk-enrollable, agent-addressed deadline reminders for its whole client book.** The deadline half of PortfolioWatch is a free government product with a bulk onboarding path. What survives is the half CH does *not* do: portfolio dashboard, PSC/officer/status **diffing**, history, and non-client companies (credit/supplier watch, where you have no authentication code).

### The one live, time-boxed wedge nobody in the card saw

The `officerList` resource now carries **`identity_verification_details`** with **`appointment_verification_statement_due_on`** — "The date by which an identity verification statement must be supplied for the appointment" — plus `appointment_verification_start_on`, `appointment_verification_end_on`, `identity_verified_on`, `authorised_corporate_service_provider_name`, and `anti_money_laundering_supervisory_bodies[]`. Under ECCTA, identity verification became a legal requirement on **18 November 2025** with a **12-month transition**, and directors must confirm verification at their **next confirmation statement**. A machine-readable per-officer verification deadline, on the free API, against a hard national clock expiring ~November 2026 — that is a sharper product than "accounts are overdue," and CH's own Follow and email-reminder services alert on neither. CompanyChange Alerts already gates "ECCTA-aligned monitoring" behind its £100/mo tier.

### Engine-never-arbiter

Two strains. (1) The card's moat — "this company is usually 3 days late, this time it's 90" — is a **behavioural inference**, not a published fact; sold to a credit team it becomes a de-facto risk score. Keep it as displayed history (dates, deltas), never a rating. (2) Competitors normalise the failure mode: ComplyTrack's homepage renders "**Penalty Exposure live £4,870 at risk right now**" and sells "Risk scoring." If PortfolioWatch shows a penalty figure it must be a deterministic lookup against Companies House's published late-filing penalty bands, computed in code with the band cited, never generated. The card's `why_simple` ("the paid deliverable is a JSON diff… no generative model is required") is correct and should be enforced as an architectural rule.

## 03 Competition & pricing

## B8 — PortfolioWatch UK: deep validation (fetched 2026-08-19)

### Verdict in one line
The data layer is real, free and confirmed live. The **buyer is not there**: every element of the promise is already delivered either free by Companies House, bundled by the practice-management suite the accountant already pays for, or sold by a live UK incumbent at **£15/month** — one-tenth of the candidate's assumed price floor. The core pricing premise ("undercut Red Flag Alert's £200/mo") is built on a secondary source that does not survive contact with the vendor's own site.

### 1. The data layer checks out (and is the least defensible part)
Everything the card claims about Companies House is true today. `GET /company/00000006` returns **HTTP 401 "Empty Authorization header"** (58 bytes, `application/json`) — real endpoint, free key. The rate-limit guide confirms verbatim: *"You can make up to 600 requests within a five-minute period."*

But two free facilities the card treats as unverified or absent are live and materially worse for the idea than assumed:

- **The Streaming API.** Not polling — a real-time firehose across **nine streams** including filing history, officers, PSC, insolvency, charges and company profile: *"gives you access to realtime data changes of the information held at Companies House."* Free, API-key only, capped at *"two concurrent connections per-account."* This removes the nightly-diff engine as a technical moat: change detection is a supplied primitive, not a built asset.
- **The Free Company Data Product** — full register snapshot as ZIP/CSV, *"provided free of charge,"* refreshed *"within 5 working days of the previous month end."*

### 2. The free "Follow" service is confirmed, and it is unlimited
The card listed this as "not yet verified live." It is verified. GOV.UK guidance (updated **9 June 2026**): *"Follow is a free service from Companies House which sends you email alerts of company transactions… The alert tells you instantly what information has been filed as soon as we've accepted it."* I fetched a live company page (HTTP 200, 35,199 bytes) and confirmed the `Follow this company` control and the live `follow.company-information.service.gov.uk` host. **The guidance states no cap on the number of companies followed** — the card's mitigation ("the wedge has to be portfolio scale") is therefore not protected by a documented limit. Follow is push, instant, and per-filing; PortfolioWatch's nightly diff is *slower* than the free option for the filing-event half of its promise.

### 3. The £200 anchor does not survive vendor contact
Red Flag Alert has **no public pricing page**: `/pricing/` and `/plans` both returned **HTTP 404** and the homepage (HTTP 200, 171,551 bytes, last-modified 17 Aug 2026) contains **no href matching pric|plan|package|cost|subscri**. Capterra UK today shows "**£200.00**" with model "Usage Based (Free Trial)" — **no period attached, no vendor attribution**. £200.00 usage-based may be a minimum spend, an annual figure, or a per-report block. The candidate's entire price-positioning claim rests on this. Same for Creditsafe: the GB packages page lists six packages, all "Get Quote," **zero prices**, with "UK & Ireland company monitoring" bundled in.

### 4. The practice-management suites already do it (vendor-confirmed)
This was the lens's central question. Answer: **yes, and cheaply.**

- **BrightManager (ex-AccountancyManager)** — vendor's own words: *"Companies House (UK) – Client company data and filing deadlines are kept up to date within BrightManager."* Bright's own pricing article (published 1 May 2026, updated 23 July 2026): **£42/user/month** rolling, **£409.25/user/year (£34/mo)**, market range *"£30 and £60 per user, per month."* Deadline tracking is included, not an add-on.
- **IRIS Elements** — Practice Management at **£13/user/month**; Company Secretarial does *"Automatic Companies House form generation including confirmation statements."*
- **Capium** — **£140/mo** (≤100 clients), **£280** (≤300), **£420** (≤500), unlimited users; Company Secretarial module **£50/£125/£167**, Practice Management **£40/£80/£120**.
- **Karbon** — **$59–$89/user/mo** annual; notably **no UK-specific Companies House functionality surfaced** on its pricing page.
- **TaxCalc** — Engager "Track upcoming deadlines," but Companies House features sit in *separate* products (Company Incorporator, Companies House Forms); no automatic deadline sync evidenced, no price published.

**Inform Direct is worse for the candidate than the card claims.** Confirmed rates: **£10.00** first company, then **£0.69** (2–50), **£0.52** (51–250), **£0.34** (251–500), **£0.17** (501–1000), **£0.08** (1001+). At 50 companies that is **£43.81/mo** — for a full company-secretarial platform *including filing*. PortfolioWatch's proposed **$49/mo for 50** is not an undercut; it is roughly parity for strictly less. Worse: Inform Direct's **free** tier explicitly lists *"Email reminders of key deadlines"* and *"Manage one or up to 10 companies"* and *"Reconciliation with Companies House."* The deadline-reminder feature the candidate wants to charge for is a free feature used as an acquisition hook by an incumbent.

### 5. The residual gap is real but already occupied at £15/month
Change monitoring (PSC/officer/status, not just deadlines) genuinely is *not* clearly covered by BrightManager or IRIS. But **CompanyChangeAlerts.com** (HTTP 200, 298,396 bytes, last-modified 29 Apr 2026) is a live UK product doing precisely PortfolioWatch's promise — *"Director/PSC resignations, appointments and changes," "Insolvency/status changes," "New filings and accounts," "Registered office address changes"* — aimed at *"UK law firms, compliance teams and governance professionals"* (the exact non-accountant buyer this lens was sent to find). Prices: **£15/mo for 10, £35/mo for 25, £100/mo for 100, +£50/mo per extra 100.**

Head-to-head at the candidate's own tiers: 50 companies → **£100 (CCA) vs $49**; 250 → **£200 vs ~$99**; 1,000 → **£550 vs $199**. PortfolioWatch only wins at 250+, i.e. exactly where a buyer demands references, security review and a sales motion — killing the A1 self-serve autonomy case. And a commodity floor exists below all of it: an Apify actor at **$0.40 per company report** with PSC/officer/status change detection and no monthly fee.

### 6. Engine-never-arbiter strain
Two flags. (a) The stated moat — *"this company is usually 3 days late, this time it's 90"* — and the framing of PSC change as *"an early distress or change-of-control signal"* edge toward a risk assertion. Deterministic arithmetic is fine; a *lateness baseline* presented to a credit team functions as a credit judgment the customer acts on. It must render observed history, never a score or a "distress" label. (b) The nearest competitor uses Gemini to write summaries; PortfolioWatch must not follow — the card's own `why_simple` (template rendering of a JSON diff) is the correct and defensible line.

### 7. One genuine tailwind, wrongly unexploited
GOV.UK (published 5 Aug 2025) confirms identity verification mandatory for new directors from **18 November 2025**, with existing directors confirming at their next confirmation statement in a **12-month transition**, and **6 to 7 million individuals** needing verification by **mid-November 2026** — i.e. a hard deadline **three months from today**. CompanyChangeAlerts already markets an *"ECCTA-aligned monitoring framework."* PortfolioWatch's card does not mention ECCTA at all. This is the only defensible wedge found, and the incumbent has already taken it.

## 04 Kill-thesis

## B8 — PortfolioWatch UK: kill report

**Verdict: killed on price and prior art.** Two live UK products already do the exact job at £7.50–£35/month, the government does the alerting half for free and faster, and the claimed moat is reconstructable from a public endpoint on day one.

### (a) The free substitute is real, and it is instant

Companies House **Follow** is confirmed live today: *"Follow is a free service from Companies House which sends you email alerts of company transactions… The alert tells you instantly what information has been filed as soon as we've accepted it"* (gov.uk news story, published 19 Jun 2019, **last updated 9 June 2026**; repeated verbatim in the register-search guidance). No stated cap on companies followed. The candidate's own risk note flagged this as "not yet verified live" — it is now verified, and it is worse for the candidate than assumed: Follow fires on filing acceptance, while PortfolioWatch's differentiator is a **nightly** cron. The paid product is strictly *slower* than the free one on the change-detection axis (officer/PSC/status changes all arrive as filings).

What Follow does *not* do is forward-looking deadline aggregation across a book of clients. That is the only surviving gap — and it is already occupied (below).

### (b) Prior art at 1/10th the proposed price — this is the fatal one

- **UK Companies House On The Go** (companiesonthego.uk/pricing/, HTTP 200): Free tier tracks **10 companies**; Standard **£7.50/month incl. VAT for up to 250 companies**; Unlimited **£15.00/month**. Its own marketing page (published 5 Jan 2026) claims it "monitors confirmation statements, annual accounts, officer changes, and PSC updates simultaneously across your entire client portfolio" — the candidate's one-liner, shipped.
- **ComplyTrack** (complytrack.co.uk, HTTP 200, Hostinger-hosted): Free for 2 companies; **£12/mo for 15; £35/mo for 75; £99/mo unlimited**, with tiered email reminders at 30/14/7/1 days, penalty-risk ranking in £, plus ECCTA identity-verification status tracking and Charity Commission data.

PortfolioWatch's pricing hypothesis is **$49–199/mo for 50/250/1,000 companies**. At 250 companies it proposes roughly 10–13× what On The Go charges for the same count, and its top tier costs 1.5–2× ComplyTrack's *unlimited* tier. The market has already priced this feature as pocket change, which is the correct price for a JSON diff over a free API.

Adjacent bundlers compress it further: **PracticeFlow** sells full UK practice management — CH sync, auto-calculated deadlines, task generation — at **£29/mo for 1 user / 250 clients**, £79/mo unlimited clients. **Inform Direct** charges **£10/mo first company + £0.69 each for companies 2–50** (£0.52 for 51–250) and includes "email reminders of key deadlines" — and actually *files*. **Endole** monitoring starts **£25/month**. Nobody is left in the middle: below £15 is a commodity, above £29 you get a whole practice suite.

### (c) The build has no barrier and one landlord

The free CH REST API is live (unauthenticated `GET /company/00000006` → **HTTP 401**, `application/json`, 58 bytes — key required, key is free) at **600 requests / 5 minutes**, with "contact us" for higher. The **Streaming API** exposes eight realtime firehoses (company, filing history, officers, PSC, PSC statements, charges, insolvency, disqualified officers) free with the same key, 2 concurrent connections. So the entire product is a weekend for anyone, and the *better* architecture (stream, not nightly poll) is also free.

Single-source risk is total: one API, one jurisdiction, one landlord who reserves the right to *"ban without notice applications that regularly exceed or attempt to bypass the rate limits."* I found **no** published CH roadmap item for native portfolio alerts, but the 2026-27 business plan blog (20 May 2026) commits to *"enabling users to search the register in more depth"* and *"expanding the use of digital channels to keep in touch with customers"* — a plausible vector for extending Follow into a followed-companies dashboard, which would zero the product. Separately, ECCTA's software-only accounts filing mandate (targeted 1 April 2027, publicly reported as **paused/under review**) pushes every practice into a full filing suite that bundles deadline tracking as table stakes.

### (d) The moat is cosmetic

The claim is a proprietary per-company lateness baseline that "compounds the longer a customer's portfolio is monitored." It does not compound: `/company/{number}/filing-history` (live, HTTP 401 unauthenticated → real endpoint) returns dated historical filings, and the **Free Company Data Product** ships a monthly full-register CSV snapshot (`BasicCompanyDataAsOneFile-2026-08-01.zip`, 470MB, last updated 01/08/2026, free). A competitor backfills ten years of lateness history for every company on the register before launch. There is no time-series a new entrant cannot buy for the cost of bandwidth.

Worse, CH already computes the judgment: the company profile resource exposes `confirmation_statement.overdue` and `accounts.next_accounts.overdue` as booleans. The "diff engine" is mostly reading a flag. Note also a factual error in the card: it cites `accounts.next_due`, which the live spec marks **deprecated** in favour of `accounts.next_accounts.due_on`.

### (e) Rule 2 — the promise cannot be signed

"Never miss a deadline" is an outcome promise underwriting real money: late filing penalties are **£150 / £375 / £750 / £1,500** by lateness band and **double on two successive late years** (gov.uk, updated 16 Jan 2026). An unattended product cannot warrant an outcome that depends on CH data currency, email deliverability, and the customer acting. Defensible mechanism-form: *"Every night we re-read each watched company's Companies House record and email you every field that changed since the last read, with the date Companies House stamped it."* That is checkable and never asserts a due date the source did not state. Anything that infers risk, scores distress, or ranks penalty exposure (as ComplyTrack does in £) crosses into engine-as-arbiter and must stay out.

### Market sizing

The buyer pool is thin at any price. FRC *Key Facts and Trends 2025* (15 Oct 2025): **3,760 registered statutory audit firms in 2024**, down from 5,007 in 2020 (−25% in five years — a consolidating, shrinking buyer base), against 408,000 UK/ROI members. The register itself is **5,479,045 companies** at end-March 2026 with 204,612 incorporations and 190,836 dissolutions that quarter. Even assuming ~10–20k UK practices with a client book worth monitoring, a market that clears at £7.50–£35/month yields low single-digit £m TAM, contested by two incumbents, a free government service, and every practice-management suite.

**Recommendation: do not build.** The only non-duplicative angle left is the ECCTA identity-verification compliance state per officer/PSC across a portfolio — and ComplyTrack already ships that too.

### Fatal risks

- PRICE FLOOR ALREADY SET BY LIVE INCUMBENTS AT 1/10th THE HYPOTHESIS. companiesonthego.uk sells 250 monitored companies for £7.50/mo incl VAT and unlimited for £15/mo (fetched 2026-08-19); ComplyTrack sells unlimited for £99/mo and 75 companies for £35/mo. The card proposes $49-199/mo for 50/250/1,000. There is no path to £30-100/mo for monitoring-only when two shipping products undercut it by 5-25x and both offer free tiers.
- THE FREE GOVERNMENT SUBSTITUTE IS FASTER THAN THE PRODUCT. Companies House Follow emails 'instantly what information has been filed as soon as we've accepted it', free, with no stated company limit (gov.uk, updated 9 Jun 2026). The candidate's core mechanism is a NIGHTLY diff — strictly slower on every change event (officer, PSC, status, accounts) since all of them arrive as filings. The product is a paid, delayed version of a free service.
- THE MOAT IS COSMETIC. The 'per-company lateness baseline that compounds' is reconstructable on day one: /company/{n}/filing-history returns dated historical filings (live, HTTP 401 unauthenticated, curl 2026-08-19) and the full register ships as a free monthly CSV (BasicCompanyDataAsOneFile-2026-08-01.zip, 470MB). No time-series advantage accrues that a competitor cannot backfill before launch. Worse, Companies House already computes the core judgment: confirmation_statement.overdue and accounts.next_accounts.overdue are returned as booleans, so the 'diff engine' is largely reading a flag.
- BUILD COST IS NEAR-ZERO FOR EVERYONE. The free Streaming API exposes eight realtime firehoses (filings, officers, PSC, charges, insolvency, disqualified officers) with a free key. Any competitor, including a practice-management suite, adds this feature in days. There is no technical barrier of any kind.
- THE PROMISE IS AN OUTCOME AN UNATTENDED PRODUCT CANNOT SIGN (rule 2 / engine-never-arbiter). 'Never miss a deadline' underwrites £150/£375/£750/£1,500 penalties that double on two successive late years (gov.uk, updated 16 Jan 2026). Nothing in the design lets the product warrant that outcome. ComplyTrack's £-denominated 'penalty risk ranking' is exactly the arbiter behaviour to avoid. Only a mechanism promise ('we re-read each record nightly and email every changed field with the date Companies House stamped it') is signable.
- TOTAL SINGLE-SOURCE, SINGLE-JURISDICTION DEPENDENCY WITH A LANDLORD WHO CAN EVICT. One free API, one government, and published guidelines reserving the right to 'ban without notice' applications that breach rate limits. The 2026-27 business plan commits to deeper register search and 'expanding the use of digital channels to keep in touch with customers' — a credible route to extending Follow into a followed-companies dashboard that would zero the product.
- BUYER POOL IS SHRINKING AND ALREADY BUNDLED. Registered audit firms fell 25% in five years to 3,760 (FRC, Oct 2025). Practices are being pushed toward full filing software by ECCTA's software-only accounts mandate, and full suites already bundle CH deadline sync at £29/mo (PracticeFlow, 250 clients) — a monitoring-only point tool is squeezed between free and cheap-full-suite with no room left.
- THE PRICE ANCHOR IN THE CARD IS UNSOUND. Red Flag Alert's '£200/mo' comes only from a Capterra directory listing; the vendor's own pricing URL returns HTTP 404 (curl 2026-08-19). It is also the wrong comparator — RFA sells credit scoring and risk data, not deadline diffs, so 'undercutting' it does not establish willingness to pay for monitoring alone.
