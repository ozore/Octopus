## 01 Mandate & demand

## B2 — ExclusionAuto: Deep Validation

**Verdict: the data layer is real and cheap; the *mandate* the pitch rests on does not exist as stated, and the pricing wedge is inverted against the incumbent the card itself names.**

### 1. The mandate is a recommendation, not a requirement — verbatim

I pulled the OIG 2013 Special Advisory Bulletin PDF directly (`oig.hhs.gov/documents/special-advisory-bulletins/881/sab-05092013.pdf`, 20pp, 473,757 bytes) and extracted the governing paragraph:

> "Providers are **not required by statute or regulation** to check the LEIE… Because there is no statutory or regulatory requirement to check the LEIE, providers may decide how frequently to check the LEIE. OIG updates the LEIE monthly, so screening employees and contractors each month **best minimizes** potential overpayment and CMP liability. Additionally, in January 2009, CMS issued a State Medicaid Director Letter (SMDL) recommending that States require providers to screen all employees and contractors monthly. In 2011, CMS issued final regulations mandating **States** to screen all enrolled providers monthly."

The card's problem statement — "HHS-OIG **requires** providers to screen every employee and vendor" — is refuted by OIG's own text. The binding monthly duty runs to **state Medicaid agencies**, not providers: 42 CFR 455.436 (fetched via eCFR versioner API, 2026-08-01 snapshot) requires the State agency to "check the LEIE and EPLS no less frequently than monthly." Providers inherit a monthly duty only where a state pushes it down contractually — which does happen: MassHealth All Provider Bulletin 196 requires providers to screen all employees and contractors "both upon initial hiring or contracting and **on an ongoing monthly basis**." That is a per-state, contract-derived obligation, not a federal one. Marketing copy asserting a federal monthly mandate is factually wrong and is the kind of claim that gets a compliance vendor sued.

What *is* real is the exposure. 42 CFR 1003.200(b)(4) makes the trigger "arranges or contracts (by employment or otherwise) with an individual… that the person **knows, or should know**, is excluded." 42 CFR 1003.210(b)(2) allows an assessment of 3× "the total costs (including salary, benefits, taxes…)" for **non-separately-billable** work — i.e. a home-health aide who never bills a line item still creates 3×-comp exposure. And the per-item penalty is now $25,595, verified primary in the Federal Register annual adjustment published 2026-01-28 (doc 2026-01688, raw text line 475): "42 CFR 1003.210(a)(4) … Penalty for employing or contracting with an excluded individual … 24,947 → **25,595**", multiplier 1.02598, effective on publication. Cross-checked against the 45 CFR 102.3 table via eCFR API.

**The correct pitch is therefore not "you must screen monthly." It is: monthly screening evidence is what rebuts "should have known."** That reframes the product from a screening utility to an evidence generator — and OIG asks for exactly that: "providers should maintain documentation of the initial name search performed (such as a print screen…)."

### 2. Demand is proven with named, dated money

OIG's own enforcement listing (`/fraud/enforcement/?type=cmp-and-affirmative-exclusions`, 200, 75KB) carries two settlements in the target segment for precisely this: **Center at Lowry — $292,000** and **Center at Northridge — $227,000**, both "for Allegedly Violating the Civil Monetary Penalties Law by Employing an Excluded Individual" (Dec 2025 cluster). Real, primary, and in the segment.

The SAB also hands over a wedge the card missed entirely (footnote 21): a hospital using agency nurses "may reduce or eliminate its CMP liability if [it] can demonstrate that it **reasonably relied on the staffing agency** to perform a check of the LEIE… agreed by contract… and the hospital exercised due diligence in ensuring that the staffing agency was meeting its contractual obligation." That is a two-sided document flow — staffing agencies must *produce* proof-of-screening to hospital clients, hospitals must *collect and audit* it. Far more defensible than a cheaper lookup.

### 3. The pricing wedge is inverted

Fetched `exclusionscreening.com/pricing/` directly (curl 200, 149KB; WebFetch was 403). Title: "Pricing | Exclusion Screening Plans from $30/mo". Tiers: **$30/month** — "Up to 100 screens per month", OIG LEIE + GSA SAM + state Medicaid exclusion list, "Monthly monitoring & alerts"; **$40/month** — plural "state Medicaid exclusion lists"; above that, "Need More Than 100 Screens? … Contact Us for Custom Pricing."

So the incumbent is **$30–40**, the candidate proposes **$49** — more expensive — and the incumbent already ships state Medicaid lists, which the candidate does not. The card's moat sentence ("many competitors emphasize LEIE alone") is refuted by the named incumbent's own page fetched today. The only genuine gap is the 100-screen cap, and a cap is a config value, not a moat.

ProviderTrust is confirmed quote-walled (`/get-started/` 301→200, no prices, "one of our team members will reach out… and a personalized demo") — so the enterprise-quote thesis holds at the top. But the bottom is already occupied at $30.

Coverage is the killer: 44 states + DC maintain separate Medicaid exclusion lists, each with its own format and cadence. ProviderTrust's own sources page (200) has legend entries for "States with No Available List" and — decisively — **"States with Email Requests Only."** A state list obtainable only by emailing the agency is an A6 breaker: recurring human minutes per state per month, forever. Federal-only avoids that but makes the product a strict subset of the $30 incumbent.

### 4. Data layer: verified, but the "deterministic" claim is false

LEIE downloaded live: **HTTP 200, text/csv, 15,578,603 bytes, Last-Modified Mon 10 Aug 2026 13:18:45 GMT** — exactly matching OIG's "by the 10th" cadence. 83,841 records (80,396 individuals, 3,446 businesses), 18 columns as claimed.

But I measured the identifiers: **only 10.3% of individual records carry a real NPI** (the field is `0000000000` placeholder for the rest). 98.9% carry DOB. 690 SMITHs, 591 JOHNSONs; 3,232 first+last name pairs collide *within LEIE itself* (12 MARY JOHNSONs). So the card's `why_simple` — "pure deterministic string/ID matching" — is wrong: fulfilment is overwhelmingly fuzzy name matching disambiguated by **date of birth**, which means ingesting employee DOB, i.e. HR PII with state breach-notification consequences the card never mentions.

Worse, OIG closes the loop against the product: "The Downloadable Data File does not contain SSNs or EINs. Therefore, **verification of specific individuals or entities through the use of the SSN or EIN must be done via the Online Searchable Database.**" The product can never close a match. Every hit is handed back to the customer. That is *good* for engine-never-arbiter but hollows out "unlimited screening" as a value proposition — unlimited *initial name searches* is the honest promise.

SAM.gov: `api.sam.gov/entity-information/v4/download-exclusions` returns **404 with no key** (reachable, gated). GSA docs (200, Last-Modified 2026-05-20) confirm an API key is required and publish rate limits — **10 requests/day for a non-federal personal key with no role**, 1,000/day with a role. The docs contain **no refresh-cadence statement**, so the card's "documented monthly-full/daily-incremental cadence" is unverified. Note also OIG explicitly ranks SAM *below* LEIE: "We recommend that providers use the LEIE as the primary source… SAM includes OIG's exclusions but also includes debarment actions taken by Federal agencies." The "two federal feeds" moat is the regulator's own second choice.

### 5. Engine-never-arbiter

Structurally safe, and forcibly so — no model can determine identity when the authoritative identifier (SSN/EIN) is absent from the file. Two strains to fix: (a) a numeric "match score" surfaced to the customer is a model-produced number they will act on — show DOB/name/address field agreement instead; (b) any "CLEARED / no match" stamp is an assertion of a negative the data cannot support. Emit "searched [roster] against LEIE dated 2026-08-10; N candidate rows returned; verification via OIG Online Searchable Database required" — timestamped evidence, never adjudication.

### Fatal risks

- THE MANDATE DOES NOT EXIST AS PITCHED. The card's problem statement says 'HHS-OIG requires providers to screen every employee and vendor'. OIG's own Bulletin says the opposite, verbatim: 'Providers are not required by statute or regulation to check the LEIE... providers may decide how frequently to check.' A compliance product whose headline claim misstates the regulation is both a conversion risk (informed buyers know) and a liability risk. The pitch must be rebuilt on the 'knows or should know' standard of 42 CFR 1003.200(b)(4) and on state contract terms (MassHealth Bulletin 196), not on a phantom federal monthly mandate.
- PRICED ABOVE THE INCUMBENT IT CLAIMS TO UNDERCUT. Candidate proposes $49/mo flat; ExclusionScreening.com's published price fetched today is $30/mo (100 screens) and $40/mo — and both tiers already include state Medicaid exclusion lists that the federal-only candidate does not. The 'structural pricing wedge' is inverted: the candidate is more expensive for less coverage. The 100-screen cap is the only real gap, and a cap is a config change the incumbent can make in an afternoon.
- THE STATED MOAT IS REFUTED BY THE NAMED INCUMBENT'S OWN PAGE. The card's moat is 'combining both federal feeds (many competitors emphasize LEIE alone)'. ExclusionScreening's $30 tier lists OIG LEIE + GSA SAM + state Medicaid. Additionally OIG itself says SAM is the inferior source for OIG exclusions and its incremental content is non-healthcare procurement debarments. There is no moat here at all — not data access, not coverage, not price.
- STATE-LIST COVERAGE IS EITHER MANDATORY OR THE PRODUCT IS A SUBSET — AND IT BREAKS A6. Buyers billing Medicaid in states with separate lists (reportedly 44 + DC) need those lists; MassHealth-style provider contracts make it a live obligation. But ProviderTrust's own sources page has a legend category 'States with Email Requests Only' — meaning some state lists require emailing a state agency to obtain. That is unbounded recurring human labour, directly contradicting A6 (zero human minutes). The product must choose between being uncompetitive (federal-only, subset of a $30 incumbent) or non-autonomous (state coverage with email loops).
- 'DETERMINISTIC MATCHING' IS FALSE AND DRAGS IN EMPLOYEE PII. I measured the file: only 10.3% of individual LEIE records carry a real NPI. The functional disambiguator is date of birth (98.9% populated). So fulfilment is fuzzy name matching plus DOB, which means the product must ingest and store employee DOB — HR PII, state breach-notification scope, and a data-handling burden the card never acknowledges while claiming 'no generative or judgment component in the fulfilment path'.
- THE PRODUCT CANNOT COMPLETE THE JOB IT SELLS. OIG: the downloadable file has no SSN/EIN, so 'verification... must be done via the Online Searchable Database.' Every candidate match is handed back to the customer for manual verification. For a turnover-heavy 200+ roster screened monthly against a file with 690 SMITHs, the volume of hand-backs may exceed the manual work the product claims to remove — the 'unlimited screening' promise delivers unlimited initial name searches, not unlimited resolved screens. This is the number-one churn risk and must be measured (false-positive rate per 100 roster names) before any build commitment.

## 02 Corpus & moat

## B2 — ExclusionAuto: deep validation (all fetches 2026-08-19)

### 1. The OIG LEIE half is real and better than the card claims

`UPDATED.csv` is live and unauthenticated: **HTTP 200, `content-type: text/csv`, `content-length: 15,578,603`, `last-modified: Mon, 10 Aug 2026 13:18:45 GMT`**, served with `content-disposition: attachment`. Header row confirmed by range-GET: `LASTNAME,FIRSTNAME,MIDNAME,BUSNAME,GENERAL,SPECIALTY,UPIN,NPI,DOB,ADDRESS,CITY,STATE,ZIP,EXCLTYPE,EXCLDATE,REINDATE,WAIVERDATE,WVRSTATE` — 18 columns, including **NPI and DOB**, the two fields that make deterministic matching defensible.

The supplement machinery the card gestures at is real and I enumerated it: the downloads page lists paired `YYMMexcl.csv`/`YYMMrein.csv` back 12 months (2026: `2601`–`2607`; 2025: `2501`–`2512`). `2607excl.csv` = 200, 47,558 B, LM 2026-08-10 13:15:19; `2607rein.csv` = 200, 14,497 B, LM 2026-08-10 13:15:56. `2608excl.csv` = **404** (not yet posted). The page states verbatim: *"OIG only archives monthly supplement files for the previous 12 months."* — so an archive built from launch day forward is a genuine, non-replicable asset (same moat logic as the PECOS expansion).

**Cadence correction.** The card says "updated by the 10th of every month." OIG's own FAQ says: *"Both versions of the LEIE are generally updated by the middle of each month. The updates include all actions taken during the prior month."* The empirical evidence matches "middle," not "10th": the file posted 2026-08-10 is labelled **07-2026** on OIG's page, and the newest supplement is `2607`. A4's staleness detector must key on `Last-Modified` + supplement-file existence, not a hardcoded day-10 expectation. Practically: an exclusion action can be up to ~6 weeks old before it reaches the CSV.

### 2. The SAM.gov half is the actual problem

Three things in the card are wrong.

**(a) The endpoint does not exist.** `https://api.sam.gov/entity-information/v4/download-exclusions?fileType=CSV` → **HTTP 404, content-length 0**. So does `/v3/download-exclusions`, `/v4/exclusions` with `DEMO_KEY`, `/data-services/v1/extracts`, and `https://api.sam.gov/` itself — all 404, `server: istio-envoy`. The card read "404 rather than DNS failure" as evidence of a live endpoint; it is not. The real endpoint per GSA docs is `https://api.sam.gov/entity-information/v4/exclusions?api_key=<value>`, and v1/v2/v3 were **retired 2024-09-13**.

**(b) There is no no-key public download.** GSA's extract docs state access requires *"a non-Federal/Federal Individual (Personal) account and the respective API Key, a non-Federal/Federal System Account with the 'Read Public' permission and the respective API Key in SAM.gov."* The only key-free route I found — data.gov's `samexclusionspublicextract-gsa-1626.csv` — 302s to a signed S3 URL for a file whose dataset metadata says **last updated December 18, 2018**. Dead.

**(c) The rate limits break A6.** GSA's published table: *non-federal user with no role = **10 requests/day***; with a role, or a system account, 1,000/day. A system account requires being *"associated with an entity"* in SAM.gov. So the SAM leg requires a human-completed, identity-proofed SAM.gov registration, and unlimited-roster screening is throttled unless the founder holds a system account. This is a real A1/A6 puncture, not a formality.

What *is* good: the V2 extract layout (PDF, 200, 171,367 B, LM 2026-04-20, "Version 1.5, April 16, 2026") has **31 columns** including `Classification` (Firm / Individual / Special Entity Designation / Vessel), `Unique Entity ID`, `Exclusion Type`, `Excluding Agency`, `Cross-Reference` (aliases), `CAGE`, and **`NPI`** (col 30). Kindly check after 7 AM Eastern time."* But **no DOB and no EIN/TIN** — so SAM individual matching is name+address only, materially weaker than LEIE.

### 4. The build-scope verdict: federal-only is not sellable at $49

The nearest competitor's live pricing page today settles it. **Basic $30/mo — "Up to 100 screens per month", OIG LEIE + GSA SAM + "1 state Medicaid exclusion list". Premium $40/mo — same 100-screen cap, LEIE + SAM + "All 46 state Medicaid exclusion lists".** State coverage is a **$10/month delta**. A federal-only product priced at $49 is *more expensive* than a 46-state incumbent at $40. The wedge as written inverts.

Does the buyer legally need the states? **No — and that cuts against the product, not for it.** OIG's 2013 Special Advisory Bulletin (fetched, 20 pp): *"Providers are not required by statute or regulation to check the LEIE."* The monthly-screening norm is a CMP-minimisation recommendation: *"OIG updates the LEIE monthly, so screening employees and contractors each month best minimizes potential overpayment and CMP liability."* 42 CFR 455.436 (verified verbatim on eCFR) puts the monthly LEIE+EPLS duty on **the State Medicaid agency**, not the provider; SAB footnote 27 confirms CMS *"does not mandate States to require their Medicaid providers to screen the providers' employees and contractors against the LEIE each month."* Exposure is 42 CFR 1003.210: **$20,000 per item or service** for conduct after 2018-02-09, plus treble assessment.

So state lists are demanded by **state Medicaid provider agreements and MCO contracts**, not federal rule — which is exactly why incumbents bundle all 46 and why buyers treat the bundle as table stakes. A4 (no 40+ brittle scrapers) and market viability are in direct conflict here. That is the finding.

### 5. Engine-never-arbiter

The card's A3 is directionally right but under-specified, and OIG's own bulletin explains why it must be: *"The Downloadable Data File does not contain SSNs or EINs. Therefore, verification of specific individuals or entities through the use of the SSN or EIN must be done via the Online Searchable Database."* (`exclusions.oig.hhs.gov` → 302, an interactive site with no documented API.) The CSV **structurally cannot** complete OIG's recommended verification step. The honest deliverable is therefore "N candidate rows requiring your SSN/EIN verification at exclusions.oig.hhs.gov," never "your roster is clean." Any "0 matches — compliant" badge, any confidence score presented as a decision, and any auto-generated attestation PDF that a compliance officer signs would violate engine-never-arbiter. The evidence PDF must be a dated *record of what the files said*, with the verification gap printed on it.

### Fatal risks

- PRICING INVERSION — the wedge is upside down. Verified today at https://exclusionscreening.com/pricing/: Basic $30/mo = LEIE + SAM + 1 state list; Premium $40/mo = LEIE + SAM + all 46 state lists. State coverage costs the incumbent's customer $10/month. The card proposes $49/mo for federal-only, i.e. 22% MORE than a 46-state competitor. The differentiator has to be the uncapped roster alone, and that is a thin story against a $9/mo price disadvantage on coverage.
- A4 vs. MARKET FIT ARE IN DIRECT CONFLICT — this is the build-scope answer. Federal-only (LEIE + SAM) is clean, cheap and A4-safe. But every incumbent bundles the states because state Medicaid provider agreements and MCO contracts, not federal rule, are what buyers are answering to. Adding 40+ states means 40+ bespoke, individually-brittle ingestions: my 5-state sample produced 5 different patterns (CA rotating-filename CSV, TX ASP.NET __doPostBack with no GET URL, NY dynamic tab-delimited, OH date-stamped XLSX behind a WebSphere portal, FL unverifiable). That scraper fleet is precisely what A4 exists to forbid. There is no version of this product that is both A4-compliant and competitively complete.
- SAM.gov IS NOT A FREE, KEY-FREE, AUTONOMOUS FEED — the card's factual basis for the SAM half is wrong. The claimed endpoint (/v4/download-exclusions) returns HTTP 404 and does not exist. No no-key public download survives (data.gov's copy is from 2018-12-18). GSA requires an API key for all access, obtained through an identity-proofed SAM.gov account, and a system account requires entity association. Rate limit for a non-federal user with no role is 10 requests/day. A human registration step and a throttle sit inside the fulfilment path, puncturing A1/A6.
- THE PRODUCT CANNOT PRODUCE THE ARTIFACT ITS BUYER WANTS. OIG's own Special Advisory Bulletin: 'The Downloadable Data File does not contain SSNs or EINs. Therefore, verification of specific individuals or entities through the use of the SSN or EIN must be done via the Online Searchable Database.' The CSV structurally cannot complete OIG's recommended verification; exclusions.oig.hhs.gov has no documented API. So the honest deliverable is a candidate list plus homework, not a clean compliance attestation — and the moment the product renders '0 matches, compliant' or an attestation PDF a compliance officer signs, it becomes the arbiter of a regulatory decision. Engine-never-arbiter forbids the artifact the market is actually paying for.
- NO LEGAL MANDATE ANCHORS THE PURCHASE. OIG states verbatim: 'Providers are not required by statute or regulation to check the LEIE.' 42 CFR 455.436's monthly duty falls on the State Medicaid agency; SAB footnote 27 confirms CMS declined to mandate that states require provider-level monthly screening. Demand is CMP-fear-driven and contract-driven, which makes it soft, price-sensitive, and defensible only by whoever bundles the most lists — the exact axis where a federal-only entrant is weakest.
- MATCHING QUALITY DEGRADES EXACTLY WHERE THE MONEY IS. The SAM public extract carries no DOB and no EIN/TIN; CA's 23,894-row list has no NPI column at all; NY collapses name and AKA/DBA into one free-text field. Only LEIE offers NPI+DOB. On a turnover-heavy 200+ name staffing roster of common surnames, the false-positive volume against these fields is the product's core cost, and by A3 every one of them must be handed back to the customer unresolved — which is a support burden the flat $49 does not fund.

### 2. Does the unlimited-roster wedge survive? Mostly no.

The candidate's core claim — that per-screen caps and custom-quote walls leave a flat-fee, unlimited-roster slot open — **is already occupied**. HealthProviders DB sells **Continuous Exclusion Monitoring at $89/month, no contract, no stated roster cap**, self-serve, covering **OIG LEIE + SAM + State Medicaid + CMS/NPI/FDA**, with automatic alerts, a dashboard, saved evidence and audit-ready reports with primary-source links per match. That page was published 2026-03-23 and carries a `modified` date of **2026-08-19 — today**. It is the proposed product, shipped, at 1.8× the hypothesised $49.

Worse for the pricing thesis: at small rosters the incumbents are **cheaper than $49**. A 100-name roster costs $25/mo at HealthProviders DB (pay-per-screen) or $30/mo at ExclusionScreening. ExclusionAuto's $49 flat only wins above ~200 names versus metered, and always versus $89 — a real but narrow band, not a category-defining wedge.

Two parts of the thesis **do** survive contact:

1. **The custom-quote wall is real and starts at 100.** ExclusionScreening's own page: *"Need More Than 100 Screens? We offer custom pricing…"* — and even the published $30/$40 tiers are **demo-gated**: I found zero checkout CTAs on the pricing page, only "Schedule a Demo" (×4) and "Book Your Free Consultation". ProviderTrust, Streamline Verify, Verisys, MedTrainer and 1stHCC publish **no price at all**. Card-in, no-call purchase above 100 names is genuinely scarce — HealthProviders DB is the only one doing it.
2. **Per-employee pricing punishes the target segment brutally.** Compliancy Group's $10–12/employee/month means a 200-worker home-health agency pays >$2,200/mo for a suite whose exclusion screening is one checkbox. Flat-fee versus per-head is a legitimate framing against the bundlers.

### 3. Two factual corrections to the card

- **The moat claim is wrong.** The card says "many competitors emphasize LEIE alone" and treats LEIE+SAM as differentiation. Live pages contradict this: **$40/mo buys LEIE + GSA SAM + all 46 state Medicaid lists**; $89/mo buys those plus CMS/NPI/FDA. Shipping LEIE+SAM only makes ExclusionAuto **feature-inferior to the $30 tier** at a $49 price. The 46 state lists — heterogeneous, many HTML/PDF, no uniform API — are the actual moat, and they sit on the wrong side.
- **"Screens" ≈ roster, not queries.** ExclusionScreening's client-portal FAQ: *"You will have access to your employee list at all times and may update it anytime at no additional charge"* and *"There is no additional cost for performing new-hire searches."* So the 100-screen cap is a **100-person roster cap**, not a metering trap. The "unlimited re-screens" half of the pitch is selling something the incumbent already gives away; only the **roster-size** half is real.

### 4. Ramanujam framing

- **Value metric:** roster size is the honest metric (it tracks CMP exposure — one excluded hire ≈ one penalty exposure) but the market has already picked it, priced in 100-name blocks. A flat fee is a *packaging* choice on top of a roster metric, and it inverts badly: it overcharges the 20-name dental practice (which is most of the TAM) to undercharge the 500-name staffing agency. **Recommended: keep the roster metric, but price in far wider blocks than 100** (e.g. $19 ≤50, $49 ≤500, $99 ≤2,500) so the "no cap you'll ever hit" feeling is preserved without giving away the enterprise band.
- **Packaging:** the fenced feature everywhere is **state Medicaid lists**. Basic vs Premium at ExclusionScreening is literally 1 state vs 46. Any tier ladder here must fence on list coverage, not on volume.
- **Free tier:** competing against OIG's free 5-at-a-time search — a free tier must be sized where the 5-name limit hurts. **25 names, monthly, LEIE-only, no evidence archive** is the right shape; the archive (dated evidence per cycle, saved verification notes) is the paid hook, and it is what HealthProviders DB charges for too.

### 5. Engine-never-arbiter

The card's A3 is well-specified and holds. Two live-verified reinforcements: OIG's own instructions require the final step to be **SSN/EIN verification on OIG's site**, and *"The Privacy Act prohibits the distribution of SSNs so they cannot be included in the Downloadable Database."* The product therefore **structurally cannot** complete a screen — the identity determination is always a human step at exclusions.oig.hhs.gov. This is a gift for engine-never-arbiter compliance but a marketing constraint: never say "verified", only "name-search record + candidate rows + primary-source link". HealthProviders DB already words it this defensively (*"Do not treat a screening result as a final determination based solely on a name match"*), which is the bar to match. The one strain: the card's "dated evidence PDFs per screen cycle" reads as an audit artifact a customer relies on — it must document what was searched and what was returned, never assert a status.

### 6. Feed mechanics (verified)

LEIE CSV: **HTTP 200, text/csv, 15,578,603 bytes, Last-Modified Mon 10 Aug 2026 13:18:45 GMT** — exactly matching OIG's *"All exclusion information is updated by the 10th of every month."* (Note OIG's FAQ hedges to *"generally updated by the middle of each month"*.) SAM.gov `v4/download-exclusions` returned **404 with content-length 0** without a key — reachable, gated; GSA docs state rate limits from **10 requests/day** (non-federal, no role) to 10,000/day, and the extract API *"can return only the first 1,000,000 records."* A daily pull fits inside 10/day, but obtaining the key/role is a manual GSA provisioning step that sits outside A1.

**Anchor for ROI copy:** 45 CFR 102.3 lists 42 CFR 1003.210(a)(4), *"Penalty for employing or contracting with an excluded individual"*, at **$25,595** in the current column (and $48,833 for an MA organization, $62,829 for an HMO).

## 04 Kill-thesis

## B2 — ExclusionAuto: deep validation (adversarial), 2026-08-19

### The data is real; the *identifiers* are not what the card assumes

I pulled the LEIE full file live today: `https://oig.hhs.gov/exclusions/downloadables/UPDATED.csv` → HTTP 200, `text/csv`, 15,578,603 bytes, `Last-Modified: Mon, 10 Aug 2026 13:18:45 GMT`. Parsed: **83,842 rows**, 18 columns (LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME, MIDNAME, BUSNAME, GENERAL, SPECIALTY, UPIN, NPI, DOB, ADDRESS, CITY, STATE, ZIP, EXCLTYPE, EXCLDATE, REINDATE, WAIVERDATE, WVRSTATE). 80,396 individuals, 3,446 businesses.

The card's fulfilment claim is "deterministic name/**NPI** matching." **NPI is a decoration, not a key**: only 8,841 rows (10.54%) carry a real NPI — the rest are the literal placeholder `0000000000`. For individuals it is 8,257/80,396 = **10.27%**. OIG says so itself: "Many individuals and entities that are excluded by OIG do not have NPIs to include in the LEIE." So ~90% of the file is matchable only on name.

Name-only matching is measurably bad: **7,431 individuals (9.24%) share an exact first+last name with at least one other LEIE record** (MARY JOHNSON ×12, JAMES BROWN ×11). Against a 200-person turnover-heavy roster of common surnames, "possible match" rows are a weekly event, not an edge case.

**The one thing that rescues the degraded mode, and the card never mentions it: DOB.** 79,535 of 80,396 individuals (98.93%) carry a full DOB (e.g. `19710329`); only 861 individuals (1.07%) have neither NPI nor DOB. Exact-normalized name **+ DOB equality** is a defensible deterministic rule that collapses most false positives — but it forces the buyer to upload dates of birth, which turns a toy CSV app into a PII processor with real security obligations (and probably a BAA conversation), and it does nothing for false *negatives*.

### The false-negative problem is structural, and it is exactly the artifact being sold

The 2013 Special Advisory Bulletin (fetched today) states the online database contains "the name of the excluded person **at the time of the exclusion**," and the downloadable file has **no alias/AKA column** (verified: 18 columns, none). A nurse excluded under a maiden name is invisible to an unattended name+DOB matcher. This is a monetised gap, not a theoretical one: Exclugo sells "**Alias Exclusion Monitoring**" as a separate product line.

Worse, OIG's own guidance forecloses the unattended path to certainty. Quick Tips: "**Search online for up to 5 individuals or entities at a time. You'll be able to verify an identity by entering a SSN or EIN**" and "The Privacy Act prohibits the distribution of SSNs so you'll need to use the Online Search to verify specific individuals and entities." The SAB repeats it. So the honest evidence PDF can only ever say: *"No row in the 2026-08-10 LEIE file matched these name+DOB fields under rule X"* — never "this person is not excluded." A product that markets itself as protection while shipping that sentence is selling a receipt, and OIG has already assigned the loss: "**because it is the provider's responsibility to determine whether employees are excluded, the providers will retain the potential CMP liability if they employ or contract with an excluded person**" (SAB, p.11). The customer pays. Verified exposure: 42 CFR 1003.210(a)(4) permits up to $20,000 per item/service post-2018; the 45 CFR 102.3 inflation table (eCFR, 2026-08-18) lists "Penalty for employing or contracting with an excluded individual" at **$24,947 (2024) / $25,595 (2025)** — note the widely-repeated "$24,947 for 2026" is the *2024* column.

**Engine-never-arbiter check:** deterministic exact matching is fine; the danger is the card's "match score." A score is a number the customer acts on. The rule must be published, binary, and reproducible (normalized name equality + DOB equality), with every candidate row shown raw. Any fuzzy/model-assisted threshold makes the engine the arbiter of identity. Also strike the word "protection" — the promise line ("the per-screen caps … deleted") pushes toward asserting clearance the product cannot assert.

### The demand is softer than the card assumes

SAB, verbatim: "**Because there is no statutory or regulatory requirement to check the LEIE, providers may decide how frequently to check the LEIE.**" The monthly mandate in 42 CFR 455.436 (eCFR, 2026-08-18) binds "**The State Medicaid agency**," not providers — "Check the LEIE and EPLS no less frequently than monthly." Provider-side monthly duty is contractual/state-by-state, i.e. exactly the heterogeneity the product proposes to skip.

### Channel and price are both already occupied

- **Incumbent undercuts the hypothesis.** exclusionscreening.com/pricing (HTTP 200 today): Basic **$30/mo**, up to 100 screens, LEIE + GSA SAM + **1** state list; Premium **$40/mo**, up to 100 screens, **all 46 state Medicaid lists**; all plans include "**Identity Verification** … to reduce false positives" and human support; custom pricing above 100 screens. The proposed **$49/mo flat** is *more expensive* than the incumbent's top published tier while omitting the two things that tier includes. The real wedge is narrower than claimed: it is self-serve signup (every incumbent CTA is "Schedule a Demo") plus uncapped volume — not price, coverage, or accuracy.
- **Bundling has already eaten the SMB.** Accountable: "Federal Exclusion Screening is included at no additional cost with every Accountable subscription," automatic monthly LEIE screening of employees/contractors/vendors, inside $199–$799/mo HIPAA plans. Viventium — healthcare-specialist payroll/HR, "over 9,000 providers" — ships exclusion monitoring "built into your HR platform," covering "OIG, SAM, OFAC, state lists." A home-health agency's HR stack is where this gets bought. (Gusto/ADP/Paychex specifically: unverified.)
- **Free tier that serves the low end**: OIG's online DB is free, 5 names at a time, *and* is the only place identity can be verified. A 15-person practice needs nothing else.

### Feed reality

SAM.gov is **not** an open CSV. `https://api.sam.gov/entity-information/v4/download-exclusions` returned **HTTP 404, 0 bytes, no content-type** without a key; GSA docs require `api_key` obtained via a registered SAM.gov account with a requested role, rate-classed at **10 requests/day** for a non-federal user with no role (1,000/day with a role), and `download-exclusions` additionally needs `&token=`. Fine for one daily pull, but it is an account dependency, not a public file.

State lists are mostly machine-readable but churn: CA's S&I list is CSV on data.ca.gov (resource last_modified 2026-08-12, filename `suspended-ineligible-list-july-2026_v3.csv` — month and `_v3` in the URL, so a hardcoded fetch silently rots); NY OMIG publishes formatted/tab-delimited/Excel/30-day-short variants. 46 of these, monthly, is the actual recurring labour — and it is precisely what $40/mo already buys.

**Cadence trap for A4:** OIG's downloads page today labels the current full file "**07-2026** Updated LEIE Database" while the file I fetched is stamped 2026-08-10, and OIG states "LEIE files include all actions taken **during the prior month**." Staleness must key on `Last-Modified`, and the evidence PDF must say "exclusions through end of prior month" — a ≤40-day blind window that has to be printed on the artifact, not buried.

### Verdict

Not fatal on data access; fatal-adjacent on *product truthfulness × price × channel*. The buildable honest version is "name+DOB deterministic diff with raw candidate rows and a dated, caveated PDF" — which is worth roughly what Accountable already gives away and less than the $40 tier that adds identity verification and 46 states. Survivable only if repositioned as an unbundled, API-first, uncapped-volume screening *runner* for rosters of 500+ where per-screen caps bite, priced above $49, with the "no match found" language legally hardened and DOB required at upload.
