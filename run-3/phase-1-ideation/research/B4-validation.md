## B4 — MarkDocket: deep validation (fetched 2026-08-19)

### The statutory rules, from primary text

**§8/§9/§71 windows (verified).** 15 U.S.C. §1058(a) (uscode.house.gov, "current as of August 18, 2026") sets the affidavit windows verbatim: "Within the 1-year period immediately preceding the expiration of 6 years following the date of registration" and "Within the 1-year period immediately preceding the expiration of 10 years following the date of registration, and each successive 10-year period," with a "6-month grace period immediately following the expiration." USPTO's own maintenance page (last updated Sep 9, 2025) mirrors this and adds the §71 Madrid track. The card's rule table is correct.

**Statement of Use (verified).** 15 U.S.C. §1051(d): SOU "[w]ithin six months after the date on which the notice of allowance … is issued"; one mandatory 6-month extension on written request; further extensions on good cause "for periods aggregating not more than 24 months." Correct.

**Office Action window — and the finding that matters.** 37 CFR §2.62(a), pulled verbatim from the live eCFR renderer today, reads: **"Each Office action shall set forth the deadline for response. (1) Response periods. *Unless the applicant is notified otherwise in an Office action*, the response periods … (i) Three months … for … section 1 and/or section 44 … (ii) Six months … for … section 66(a)"**, extendable "by three months up to a maximum of six months from the Office action issue date, upon timely request and payment of the fee set forth in §2.6(a)(28)." USPTO's response-time-period page adds: "Because the length of the response period may vary, please confirm the specific time period in your office action," and "Madrid applicants must respond within six months … with no option to extend."

This breaks the card's core mechanism claim ("computed by fixed rule from TSDR prosecution history"). The regulation makes the office action document itself the authority; a computed date is a *derived guess* that is wrong whenever USPTO sets a different period. Round-4 kill pattern (mandate misstated) fires here in the subtle form: the premise isn't wrong about the numbers, it's wrong about *who computes them*. The only defensible product form is quoting the deadline USPTO stated, from the document — which requires parsing the office action PDF, not the status JSON.

### Kill pattern 1 — the free first-party substitute is total

USPTO does the headline job itself, for free, in three separate places:

1. **Courtesy email reminders** (uspto.gov, updated Feb 5, 2024): "We send courtesy email reminders on the first day of the statutory filing period … to all email addresses of record in the Correspondence and Current Owner(s) Information fields," containing "the maintenance filing currently due …, along with the anniversary filing deadline, **the grace period deadline**, and the filing fee per class." That is MarkDocket's §8/§9/§71 tier, delivered by the agency.
2. **TSDR Maintenance tab** (uspto.gov check-status page, updated **Aug 6, 2026**): "go to TSDR, enter your registration number, and select the **Maintenance** button … You'll see **the next filing deadline** and maintenance document type you need to file." USPTO publishes the computed next deadline for free, per registration.
3. **MyUSPTO trademark dockets** (uspto.gov, updated Dec 18, 2023): "MyUSPTO trademark dockets may be configured to send additional alerts to a designated email address **any time the status of an application or registration changes** or there is a change to certain prosecution history entries, such as change of owner, correspondence, attorney, or domestic representative." USPTO's "Ten things" page (Oct 10, 2024) instructs owners to "Set up an 'alert' in your MyUSPTO account, which will notify you when there are changes or something is filed."

And the office action and Notice of Allowance are themselves emailed to the address of record with the deadline in the heading. For a 1–10-mark owner — the exact target — the paid product's incremental value over free is an escalation ladder and a portfolio view, not the deadline.

### Kill pattern 2 — live incumbents, on both sides of the price

- **DeadlineDocket** (deadlinedocket.com/pricing, fetched today) is MarkDocket, already shipped: "imports trademark data from the USPTO's TSDR database, calculates deadlines automatically," covering office action responses **and extensions**, SOU deadlines and extensions, §8, §9, and opposition monitoring — at **"$89/mo"** or "$890/yr", **"Unlimited trademarks"**, 14-day trial, self-serve. Unlimited-marks pricing structurally kills the "flat cheap tier for 1–10 marks" wedge: the incumbent already has no per-matter meter.
- **Alt Legal** (fetched today) confirms $60/$100/$195/$295 per month for 50/100/200/400 matters, plus "+$30 to +$95/month" trademark-protection add-on — as the card said. But it is the *ceiling*, not the floor.
- **The watch tier is priced backwards.** Markify (fetched today): **US Federal Full "$39/year"**, single registers "From $39/year", Global "$129/year", reports delivered **weekly**, all classes, 190 registers available. MarkCollide's hypothesis is $9–15/mo *per mark* = $108–180/yr — **3–5× more expensive than a live incumbent that already covers more registers and more classes**. This is the round-1 pattern in its purest form.

### Kill pattern 3 — bundled in the filing platform

USPTO's own online-tools page (updated **Aug 18, 2026**): "**Trademark Center** … file a new trademark application, pay application-related fees, and **use the docketing feature to track the status of applications**." Docketing now ships inside the free filing system the buyer already uses. A live docket UI exists at trademarkcenter.uspto.gov/docket/all.

### A4 / data-access risk

- TSDR API is live and gated: `https://tsdrapi.uspto.gov/ts/cd/casestatus/sn88888888/info.json` → **HTTP 401**, Content-Length 278, today 22:22 UTC. Key required (USPTO bulk-data page: "You must register for an API key in order to use the TSDR APIs").

The replacement, `https://api.uspto.gov/api/v1/datasets/products/TRTDXFAP`, returns **HTTP 401 `{"message":"Unauthorized"}`**. `https://data.uspto.gov/bulkdata/datasets/TRTDXFAP` returns HTTP 200 but is an Angular SPA shell (20,666 bytes, last-modified 2026-08-17), not a file index. Daily-XML access is now key-gated and undocumented at the fetch level — build risk the card treats as settled.

### Engine-never-arbiter

The promise — "Every statutory trademark deadline on your marks, computed" — is a deterministic outcome claim with liability attached, the round-1 pattern. Docketing is the highest-liability clerical function in IP: aggregated industry reporting puts missed deadlines at the top of malpractice causes. §2.62(a) means a computed date can lawfully differ from the real one; a $15/mo unattended product cannot sign that number. The sellable form is mechanism-plus-record: "USPTO's office action dated X states a response deadline of Y — here is the document," not "your deadline is Y." The collision tier is clean (ranks, never opines) but is the tier being outpriced 3–5×.

### Verdict

Refuted. The maintenance half is free from USPTO three ways; the prosecution half is authored by USPTO in the document itself and emailed; the price wedge under Alt Legal is already occupied by an unlimited-marks incumbent at $89/mo; and the watch tier is priced above a $39/yr incumbent. No reshape I could construct survives all four simultaneously: an evidence-archive reframe (dated TSDR document + prosecution-snapshot archive, quoting the deadline USPTO stated) is the only A3-clean form, and a 3-mark owner's willingness to pay for it is unevidenced.

**TSDR already computes and publishes the §8/§9 deadlines, free and unauthenticated.** `https://tsdr.uspto.gov/maintenanceview/rn7000001` returned, today, in 2,155 bytes: "§8 (6 year) Declaration Due Dates: Earliest date §8 can be filed: Mar. 14, 2028 / Latest date §8 can be filed without paying additional fee: Mar. 14, 2029 / Latest date §8 can be filed by paying an additional fee: Sep. 14, 2029." What USPTO does *not* give away: `maintenanceview` **503s for pending applications** (confirmed on sn79286868, a live §66(a) app) — there is no free USPTO-computed OA-response or SOU/extension-ladder date, and no portfolio-level aggregation.

## 3. Where a WRONG DATE arises from data alone

Deterministic from the record: §8/§71 6-year and §9 10-year windows (15 U.S.C. 1058(a), 1141k(a), 1059(a) — 1-year window + 6-month grace, arithmetic on registration date); basis branch (TSDR exposes `Filed 66A / Currently 66A` flags, so 3-month vs 6-month is derivable per 37 CFR 2.62(a)(1)).

Not deterministic:
- **2.62(a) opens with "Unless the applicant is notified otherwise in an Office action."** The authoritative period lives in the PDF, not the event date. Suspension notices, examiner's amendments and priority actions carry different periods.
- **§66(a) has no extension** — 2.62(a)(2) extends only paragraph (a)(1)(i) (§1/§44). Getting the basis wrong flips a 6-month hard stop into a phantom 9-month one.
- **Anchor-date drift:** §1058(a)(1) runs from registration *or* "the date of the publication under section 1062(c)"; §1141k runs from "issuance of the certificate of extension of protection," not registration.
- **Extension limbo** (2.89(b),(c): four subsequent 6-month requests, "may not aggregate more than 24 months") — between request and the grant event, the live deadline is genuinely ambiguous.
- **2.196 rollover** for Saturday/Sunday/"Federal holiday within the District of Columbia" — needs a maintained holiday calendar.
- **2.66 petitions to revive** reset state after abandonment.

### (a) Real clearing price for docketing, 1–10 marks: **$0**

The USPTO already does the headline job for this exact buyer, for free, from its own system of record.

- **Post-registration maintenance.** USPTO sends courtesy email reminders for §8, §71 and §9 "on the first day of the statutory filing period for the maintenance filing," to "all email addresses of record in the Correspondence and Current Owner(s) Information fields," containing "registered mark, serial and registration numbers, registration date, owner(s) of record, maintenance filing due with deadlines and fees" (uspto.gov, page last updated Feb 5 2024). That is the §8/§9/§71 half of MarkDocket, itemised, gratis.
- **Prosecution-stage status.** MyUSPTO trademark dockets "may be configured to send additional alerts to a designated email address any time the status of an application or registration changes or there is a change to certain prosecution history entries" (uspto.gov, updated Dec 18 2023). The online-tools page states plainly: "You can even sign up to get email updates when your application status changes."
- **Filing-platform bundling — and it's the government's own.** Trademark Center is described by USPTO as the place to "file a new trademark application, pay application-related fees, and use the docketing feature to track the status of applications filed through Trademark Center."
- The triggering events themselves arrive by email anyway: office actions and Notices of Allowance are emailed with the response deadline in the notice heading.

USPTO attaches the standard disclaimer ("not obligated… failure to receive a reminder does not excuse you"). That disclaimer is real, but it is not a wedge — it is exactly the liability the *paid* product also cannot absorb (see engine-never-arbiter, below). A $15–19/mo product whose differentiator is "the free federal reminder might land in spam" has no defensible price.

Above the free line, the paid floor is attorney-grade and already occupied: **Alt Legal $60/mo (≤50 matters) → $100 (≤100) → $195 (≤200) → $295 (≤400)**, unlimited users, no setup fee — the card's $60–295 figure is confirmed verbatim. **DocketTrak $125/mo flat for 5 users.** **Docket Alarm $99/mo per professional.** There is no observed paying cohort between $0 and $60.

### (b) Real clearing price for watch, per mark: **$39/yr automated — or $0**

- **Markify US Federal Full: $39/year**, weekly reports by email plus online workspace, all classes included, keyword G&S filter; EU Full $59/yr, Europe $75/yr, LatAm $65/yr, Global (190 registers) $129/yr; single registers from $39/yr. Confirmed on Markify's own page today.
- **Trademarkia: free.** Its monitoring page states weekly alerts on "new confusingly similar trademark filings" at no charge, monetised by a $379 cease-and-desist / letter-of-protest upsell.

MarkCollide's hypothesis is $9–15/mo/mark = **$108–180/yr**, i.e. **2.8×–4.6× Markify**, against a competitor that is already fully automated and weekly. The 5-mark bundle at $29–49/mo ($348–588/yr) versus Markify's $195/yr for the same five marks is 1.8×–3× worse. The card's stated moat ("no human analyst in the loop → structural cost advantage over Markify") mis-describes Markify, which is itself the automated low-cost player; the human-reviewed tier is elsewhere and priced accordingly: **Trademark Factory TM Watch Silver $19/TM/country/month ($228/yr) automated, Gold $47/mo ($564/yr) with expert review; LegalZoom $249/yr; Trademark Engine $175/quarter ($700/yr).** Corsearch publishes no price at all (demo request only) — enterprise, irrelevant to this buyer.

### Kill patterns

1. **Free government substitute — fired, hard, on both tiers.** USPTO courtesy reminders + MyUSPTO docket alerts + Trademark Center docketing cover docketing; Trademarkia's free weekly watch covers collision.
2. **Live micro-SaaS below the hypothesised price — fired.** **DeadlineDocket** ($89/mo, $890/yr annual, unlimited trademarks, 14-day trial) is not merely adjacent: its published description is MarkDocket's build spec — paste serial numbers, it "imports the full prosecution history from USPTO TSDR, then generates all applicable deadlines automatically," plus a TSDR-every-24h filing-verification loop the card never conceived. The card lists no incumbent below Alt Legal, which means the miner did not look.
3. **Bundled inside software already paid for — fired.** Alt Legal sells watch as a **+$30/+$40/+$60/+$95 per month** add-on riding the docketing subscription; LegalZoom and Trademark Engine bundle monitoring with the registration they filed; USPTO bundles docketing with its own filing tool. Every channel to the 1–10-mark owner is already carrying this feature.
4. **Mandate check.** The card flagged the office-action window as unverified; it is now verified and *is* more branched than the card assumed. USPTO: "we **must** receive a response to your office action within three months," one three-month extension available for a fee ($125 via TEAS), **except** Madrid §66(a) applicants, who "must respond within six months from issue date, with no option to extend." Post-registration office actions run on a separate schedule. Grace periods (§8/§9 six-month grace, with surcharge) are not in the card's rule sketch. This is buildable but it is a real rule table with jurisdictional forks — and every fork is a place where a wrong date is a dead registration.
5. **A4 / data access.** TSDR is fine: `tsdrapi.uspto.gov/ts/cd/casestatus/sn88888888/info.json` → **HTTP 401, text/plain, 278 bytes**, i.e. live and key-gated; TSDR FAQ states verbatim "All requests are rate limited at 60 requests per API key per minute. PDF and ZIP downloads are rate limited at 4 requests per API key per minute." Sixty per minute is ample for a 10-mark portfolio. Keys are free, so this is friction rather than refutation — but the daily-XML path is *unproven live* as of today.

### Engine-never-arbiter
The ballot promise — "Every statutory trademark deadline on your marks… every confusingly-close new filing, flagged" — is precisely the round-1 pattern. A computed §8 window is a **deterministic output asserting a legal fact with terminal liability**: miss it and the registration is cancelled, and there is no revival. "Never miss a deadline" is an outcome an unattended product cannot sign — which is exactly why USPTO itself disclaims its own reminders. The sellable form states mechanism and shows the record: *"TSDR shows event NOA mailed 2026-03-04; USPTO's own §1(d) rule sets a 6-month window from that mailing; here is the raw TSDR row."* Likewise "every confusingly-close new filing" is a completeness claim over a similarity function. The card's internal A3 text is disciplined (ranks candidates, never opines on likelihood of confusion); the ballot promise is not, and the promise is what a customer would sign.

A reshape toward the *attorney* buyer ($60–125/mo band) abandons the card's entire thesis, which was the price wedge *below* Alt Legal — and that band is already served by DeadlineDocket at $89/mo with a feature (filing-receipt verification) MarkDocket does not have.

## B4 — MarkDocket: deep validation (adversarial lens), 2026-08-19

### 1. The free first-party substitute covers the headline job for the named buyer

MarkDocket's buyer is "solo filers, small brands… with 1–10 marks." For exactly that buyer, USPTO already emails the three things the product sells:

- **Maintenance half:** "We send courtesy email reminders on the first day of the statutory filing period for the maintenance filing… to all email addresses of record in the Correspondence and Current Owner(s) Information fields," containing "the maintenance filing currently due… along with the anniversary filing deadline, **the grace period deadline**, and the filing fee per class" (uspto.gov, page last updated Feb 5, 2024). That is the §8/§9/§71 tier, free, computed by the agency that owns the record.
- **Status half:** MyUSPTO — "sign up to get email updates when your application status changes" (uspto.gov *Online trademark tools*, last updated **Aug 18, 2026** — i.e. current as of yesterday). Trademark Center adds a free "docketing feature to track the status of applications."

The residual failure mode MarkDocket would catch is "owner ignored / never received the USPTO email." A paid product emailing the same inbox fails identically. This is round-1 kill pattern #1, at full strength.

### 4. Liability: the promise is an outcome claim, and the rule set is not as fixed as the card says

The card's own words: "Every statutory trademark deadline on your marks." Missing one is terminal — "If you don't file before the end of the grace period, your registration will be canceled or will expire" (uspto.gov, updated Sep 9, 2025), and remediation costs §8 $325/class, §9 $325/class, $100/class grace surcharge (USPTO fee schedule, effective Jan 19, 2025, revised Aug 14, 2026).

The deadline rules branch more than "fixed rule table" implies: pre-registration office actions are **three months** with "an optional three-month extension… for a fee" ($125, fee code 7016), but §66(a) Madrid cases get **six months with "no extension option"** (uspto.gov *Responding to office actions*, updated Aug 4, 2026); §71 replaces §8 for Madrid registrations; suspensions, petitions to revive, and extension requests each move the date. Every branch is a wrong-date vector aimed at a customer who by construction has **no counsel** — that is the whole segment thesis. Meanwhile USPTO disclaims its own free version: "The USPTO is not obligated to send reminders… failure to receive a reminder does not excuse you." An agency can disclaim a courtesy; a $15/mo product whose entire value proposition *is* the guarantee cannot simultaneously disclaim it. Strip the outcome claim down to the engine-never-arbiter-safe form — "here is USPTO's record and the date printed on USPTO's document" — and you have re-built TSDR/MyUSPTO, free.

### 5. The watch half also strains engine-never-arbiter, and the base rate is brutal

A ranked similarity score, however deterministic, is read by an uncounselled buyer as a verdict on likelihood of confusion, and it drives a decision with a hard clock (publication/opposition window) and real money. Worse is the silence: a text-only edit-distance engine cannot see phonetic equivalents, design marks, or foreign-equivalent conflicts, yet a customer paying for "collision watch" reads no-alert as safety — a false-negative liability the product can never discharge. Base rate from USPTO's own TTAB stats (FY2025, page updated Jul 8, 2026): **7,650 oppositions** and **19,130 extensions of time to oppose** against **more than 824,000 new classes filed in FY2025** (USPTO, Dec 2, 2025). Under ~1% of filings are ever opposed; ≥99% of alerts are non-actionable noise the buyer pays to triage.

### 6. Sizing the "reachable middle"

The middle MarkDocket needs is: enough marks to care, no counsel, and unwilling to rely on free USPTO email. Pro-se share of filings is commonly cited at ~30% but I could not verify it from a USPTO primary (the FY2024 TPAC annual report, which I extracted in full, contains no pro-se statistic). Whatever its size, it is reached through the same paid-search channel where filing services hand out watch alerts free to acquire a $200–$600 filing customer — a CAC contest an unattended $15/mo subscription loses. And this cohort's marks are the low-value ones where losing a registration is cheapest to fix.

**Verdict: REFUTED.** Free first-party coverage of the headline job, an incumbent price floor at $0–$39/yr the card mis-read as a ceiling, bundling by both the firm-side suite and the filing services, and an unsignable outcome claim at the center of the promise. No reshape I tested (attorney second-check mirror; raw data feed) clears both the liability and the price floor.

### Fatal risks

- Free first-party substitute for the headline job: USPTO's own courtesy email reminders deliver the §8/§9/§71 deadline, grace deadline and fee to the owner's email on day one of the window, and MyUSPTO emails status changes free — for the exact 1–10-mark buyer MarkDocket names. (Hunted: uspto.gov maintain/, apply/, online-tools, login, dashboard, timelines pages, all fetched today.)
- Price floor inverted: the card claims to undercut Markify but $9–15/mo/mark = $108–180/yr vs Markify's live $39/yr US Federal Full watch (all classes, weekly reports). Below that sits PageCrawl's $0 Free Forever tier and Trademarkia's free weekly alerts. There is no margin left for an unattended product.
- Bundled inside what the paying buyer already buys: Alt Legal at $60/mo for 50 matters (≈$1.20/matter/mo, unlimited users, 180+ offices, TEAS integration, calendar sync) makes the '50-mark solo practitioner' tier unsellable; Alt Legal's §2(d) Watch beats similarity scoring by using the examiner's own citation.
- Unsignable outcome claim at the center of the promise: 'every statutory trademark deadline' on a right that dies (registration 'canceled or will expire') sold to a customer with no counsel, over a rule set that branches on §1/§44 vs §66(a), extensions, suspensions and petitions. USPTO refuses to guarantee even its own free reminders. Reduce the claim to the engine-never-arbiter-safe form ('here is USPTO's record and the date on USPTO's document') and the product becomes free TSDR/MyUSPTO.
- Watch half arbitrates in effect: a ranked similarity score read by an uncounselled buyer is a de facto likelihood-of-confusion verdict driving an oppose/don't decision on a hard clock; and text-only matching's false negatives (phonetic, design, foreign equivalents) sell silence as safety. Base rate makes it worse: 7,650 oppositions + 19,130 extensions of time to oppose in FY2025 against 824,000+ classes filed — ≥99% of alerts are non-actionable.
