## D1 — Lookback: deep validation (live, 2026-08-20/21)

### 1. Statutory premise: PROVEN from primaries, and stronger than the card claimed

I extracted Florida DCF's own ESS Policy Manual PDF (ch. 1600/1640, MSSI — the program covering ICP nursing-home Medicaid). §1640.0608: "You must consider any transfer that occurred within the transfer look-back period prior to the date of application… The look-back period for non-trust transfers is 60 months." §1640.0618: "Uncompensated value divided by the average private nursing home rate equals the number of months of ineligibility," with fractional penalties post-11/1/07 and — a trap the card never names — "The current average private nursing home rate is used for all transfers, regardless of when the transfer occurred."

The miner's best evidence was secondhand; the manual is better. §1640.0609.01 lists the caseworker's "most common clues to potential transfers of assets," beginning "1. unidentified withdrawals from bank accounts… 4. unidentified deposits on financial statements." That is the product spec, written by the adjudicator.

PA DHS confirms the same mechanics ("transferred, sold, or given away within the past 60 months… penalty period is figured by dividing the UV by the average daily private pay rate"). NY GIS 25-02 (fetched raw) gives seven regional rates, $12,842–$15,127 for 2025, and requires "the regional rate in effect as of the date of application… for the region in which the facility is located" — a per-case rule a generalist gets wrong.

Timing pressure: PROVEN. FL ESS §0640.0401 (MSSI): "The verification/information due date is 10 calendar days… Leave the case pending until the 30th day after the date of application."

### 2. Where the card is wrong — and why it argues *for* the company

### 4. UPL

Florida (183 So. 3d 276, 2015): nonlawyers may "assist a Medicaid applicant with their application as permitted by federal law," but may not "give legal advice concerning how to apply Florida law to obtain Medicaid benefits, including advising on legal strategies for spending down and restructuring assets." Ohio UPL 11-01: nonlawyers may "review documents, prepare and file Medicaid applications, and attend state hearings."

Selling to **law firms is clean** — the lawyer is the professional and the deliverable is factual assembly. Selling to nursing homes and non-attorney planners is not symmetric: the completeness matrix and raw transfer schedule are permitted fact-assembly, but the **state-specific penalty-exposure estimate** applies state law to facts to predict a benefits outcome, which is what Florida forbids nonlawyers to do. Fix is a feature gate (penalty estimate and exempt-transfer classification available only to verified attorney accounts), not human labor per customer. **Class B, and it re-prices segment 2 rather than killing it.**

## 02 Corpus & technical premise — verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED

# D1 — Lookback: deep validation (technical lens)

**Verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED.** No Class A finding. The premise is real, the corpus is obtainable, the cost model holds under adversarial recomputation, and the card's honest-degradation design is the right shape. But I broke one of its own numbers against a primary source, and I found a resolution ceiling and a dual-use hazard the miner missed.

## 1. Can VLMs actually read degraded scanned statements?

The best live evidence is **RealDocBench** (Extend AI, arXiv 2606.07401, fetched today) — 1,356 field-level questions over 581 *real regulated* documents with handwriting, stamps, checkbox grids, scanned variants; 18 systems under one scorer. Results: **Claude Opus 4.8 (non-thinking) 93.0% per-field / 87.1% per-question**, second of eighteen, beating Azure DI (89.1), AWS Textract (70.7) and every open-source system. Gemini 3.5 Flash 89.3/82.2. On the **finance** domain specifically: best system 92.7%, Gemini 3.5 Flash 83.8%. Cost/page from the same table: Gemini 3.5 Flash $0.0113, Azure DI $0.010, Extend v2 $0.040.

Corroboration and counterweight:
- ACL 2026 industry paper on **long scanned financial documents in KYC** (arXiv 2604.26462): ~3,000 scanned pages with "skew, low resolution, compression noise, stamps"; best OCR+VLM config 87.27% overall, **83.95% on financial statements**. Open-weight models only.
- **Ocrolus FAQ (fetched today)**: "OCR struggles to get much above 85% accuracy across a representative mix of documents" — their 99%+ claim is explicitly *human-in-the-loop*. The market leader in bank-statement extraction still buys the last 14 points with people.

**Reading:** the technical premise is proven at the ~90% per-field level and *not* proven at the 99% level. That is fatal for a product that asserts conclusions and fine for one that produces a reviewed exhibit — which is what the card designs. Two hardening requirements follow. (a) The completeness grid must not depend on per-page extraction accuracy: statement period and account number repeat on every page, "page 3 of 8" is a cross-check, and **closing balance must chain to the next period's opening balance**. Balance-continuity reconciliation converts a 93%-accurate reader into a near-deterministic gap detector, and it is the single most important design addition. (b) The transfer-schedule metric must be published as *recall of uncompensated transfers*, not field accuracy — the expensive error is the miss, and per-field averages "overstate end-to-end reliability" (RealDocBench §5.1).

**New finding — resolution ceiling (Class B).** Claude's vision doc (fetched today) gives cost as `⌈w/28⌉×⌈h/28⌉` visual tokens with a hard cap: **standard tier (all pre-4.7 models, i.e. Haiku 4.5) = 1,568 visual tokens, 1,568 px long edge**; high-resolution tier (Claude 4.7+, so Sonnet 5) = 4,784 tokens / 2,576 px. A letter page downscaled to the standard cap is ~975×1,261 px ≈ **115 DPI** — well under the 300 DPI norm for OCR of faxes. The card's "~1,800 vision tokens/page" is not achievable on Haiku 4.5; the page is silently downsampled instead. Fix is tiling (2–4 crops/page), which multiplies tokens but not much money.

## 2. Cost model — re-verified and recomputed

## 3. Fine-tune on synthetic statements — precedented, but aimed wrong

Precedent exists and is strong. **SynthDoG/Donut** (ECCV 2022) established synthetic document generation as pre-training data for OCR-free document understanding. More directly, **RealDocBench itself** builds part of its gold set by "populat[ing] real, blank form templates with fictitious-persona data, preserving the authentic layout while fabricating only the field contents… the persona values serve as gold by construction" — the card's plan (a), published by a commercial document-AI vendor in 2026.

The counter-evidence is about *where* the fine-tune sits. RealDocBench: Nemotron Nano V2 VL, a generalist 12B VLM, scores **41.6% per-field** with "characteristic OCR misreadings ('L0S5 H1ST0RY')", and the paper concludes "a general VLM is no substitute for an OCR-specialist model"; the whole open-source field trails commercial agentic systems by 17+ points per-question. **A fine-tuned 7–8B pixel-level extractor is not a realistic replacement for the frontier read.** The card's *classifier* tier, however, runs over already-extracted structured lines (payee string, amount, recurrence, counterparty match) — that is a text classification problem where a small fine-tune is entirely credible and near-free. Scope the fine-tune there; keep extraction on frontier models permanently. The card already hedges this ("until the eval harness says yes, we ship on frontier models"), so this is a scoping correction, not a break.

**New risk the miner missed (Class B):** building "~200 US bank/brokerage statement layouts reproduced as synthetic generators with scan/fax degradation" is, functionally, a bank-statement forgery engine. Live 2026 reporting describes AI-generated document fraud growing ~4x from June 2025 to May 2026 and banks adding rotating transaction IDs and export fingerprints in response. This needs governance from day one — generator never leaves the training VM, no logos rendered at fidelity, and an explicit ToS/AUP read before any cloud GPU touches it. Flip side: the same corpus makes a *doctored-statement detector* a natural paid feature.

## 04 Kill-thesis — verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED

> # D1 — Lookback: deep validation (live re-verification 2026-08-21)
>
> **Verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED.** No Class A finding stands. Two Class B findings are severe enough to change the product's shape before build.
>
> ## (a) Liability — Class B, but the design has a hole the card does not see
>
> The buyer is the responsible professional and the artifact is attorney work product, not a filing made by the vendor. Nothing here structurally requires human work per customer, so A1–A6 hold. The card's A3 design is above average: page-image crop per flagged line, model confidence, the state rule *and its effective date* printed on the output, a separate "unresolved" schedule, abort-and-refund when coverage is too poor, and deterministic (non-model) arithmetic for the completeness grid and the penalty division.
>
> But every one of those controls governs **false positives**. The malpractice event is a **missed transfer** — a false negative — which by construction produces no crop, no confidence score, and no unresolved-schedule entry. It is invisible in the UI. The card acknowledges the risk only in its moat section ("recall of uncompensated transfers (the expensive miss)") and never surfaces it in-product. **Required reshape:** deterministic per-statement reconciliation — extracted debits + credits + beginning balance must equal the printed ending balance; any page that fails to reconcile is stamped *incomplete extraction* on the exhibit. That converts recall from a model promise into arithmetic. CounselPro already ships something adjacent ("self-healing reconciliation," gated to its $500/mo Premier tier and its $499/matter plan, pricing page fetched today), which is evidence the check is feasible and commercially valued.
>

### Unproven

- Whether the Wayback Machine is a workable A4 fallback for Akamai-blocked state sources. The availability API returned a valid 200 snapshot for the Texas I-5100 page dated 2026-01-13, but web.archive.org content retrieval is blocked by this sandbox's egress policy and by WebFetch, so I could not confirm the archived page actually carries the divisor text or that the snapshot cadence is frequent enough to catch an annual change.
- National annual volume of long-term-care Medicaid applications. No CMS, KFF or state source publishes application or eligibility-determination counts; only stock figures (1.38M nursing home residents, ~63% Medicaid-covered). The $199/case TAM therefore rests on one firm-level datapoint ('hundreds annually') plus a NAELA membership estimate the card sourced by search, not by fetch.
- Whether elder-law firms will upload a dying client's complete financial records to a third-party SaaS on a card swipe with no negotiated vendor agreement. The card names this as its biggest unknown; I found no evidence either way and it remains the single largest A1 risk.
- Whether the synthetic-statement fine-tune generalizes to real faxed statements. Untestable pre-build; the card's own fallback (ship on frontier models, moat is the rule corpus) is the correct hedge but leaves AP3 resting entirely on a corpus I have just shown to be error-prone.
- Whether SNF/assisted-living business offices buy per-application services at all. The card admits no fetched evidence; the Florida UPL holding makes selling them a penalty-exposure estimate additionally hazardous, so this segment should be treated as absent rather than secondary.
- Whether balance reconciliation actually catches missed transaction lines at useful rates on degraded fax scans, where beginning/ending balances themselves may be unreadable. The mechanism is sound in principle; its yield on the real input distribution is unmeasured.

### Findings (with class)

- **[B]** CORPUS INTEGRITY (most serious). The card's own cited divisor for Texas is live-wrong: $242.13/day vs the HHSC handbook's $262.37/day effective 9/1/2025, and the aggregator it relies on repeats the stale figure under a 'Last updated July 09, 2026' stamp. A wrong divisor flows straight into the arithmetic the card markets as carrying 'no model uncertainty at all.' Not Class A because the data is obtainable and 51 effective-dated numbers is a curated table, not a corpus — but the card's stated mechanism ('re-crawl state eligibility manuals') must be replaced with a versioned table plus a loud cron diff alarm before build.
- **[B]** CORPUS ACCESS. Probed live: hhs.texas.gov 403 (Akamai), mass.gov 403, PA services.dpw.state.pa.us TLS reset; only Florida DCF's PDF and codes.ohio.gov fetched clean (2 of 5). The A4 crawler as designed does not run from a datacenter. Mitigable with headed-browser fetching, per-state PDF transmittals, and archive fallback, but it is engineering cost the card has not budgeted and a standing staleness risk.
- **[B]** RECALL BLIND SPOT IN THE A3 DESIGN. Every honest-degradation control in the card (crops, confidence, unresolved schedule, abort-and-refund) governs false positives. The malpractice event — a missed transfer — produces no artifact at all and is invisible in the UI. Fix: deterministic per-statement balance reconciliation (extracted debits + credits + opening balance must equal the printed closing balance), with non-reconciling pages stamped 'incomplete extraction' on the exhibit. Without this the product's core safety claim is one-sided.
- **[B]** UPL SCOPING. The Florida Bar re Advisory Opinion, 183 So. 3d 276 (Fla. 2015) makes nonlawyer Medicaid planning — including advice on applying state law to obtain benefits — unlicensed practice and a third-degree felony. The card's 'secondary and larger' segments (non-attorney planners, SNF business offices) cannot safely receive a state-specific penalty-exposure estimate. Sell to law firms; gate the penalty number behind attorney attestation; give non-attorneys only the deterministic completeness matrix.
- **[B]** PRICE FLOOR AND CHANNEL PRESSURE. CounselPro's credit math (1 credit = 1 page; 12,000 credits/yr on $500/mo Premier) implies ~$0.50/page against Lookback's $0.27/page — a 2x undercut, not a moat, and CounselPro already ships reconciliation. Separately, brevy.com already occupies the free per-state divisor SEO surface the card designates as distribution channel #1. Both are constraints to price and flank around, not kills.
- **[B]** INPUT DEPENDENCY. Plaid Statements caps at two years of history against a 60-month requirement, so no API closes the acquisition gap; the product only begins after the client's hardest step. Partly offset by the completeness matrix, which is genuinely the artifact that shortens that step, but the wedge must be sold as 'know what is missing before the state tells you,' never as 'we get your statements.'
