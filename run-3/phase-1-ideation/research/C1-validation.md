# C1 — ChargeFile: Deep Validation, 2026-08-20

## Verdict in one line
The **regulatory premise is clean and the corpus is alive** — but the two kill patterns the miner declared survivable (2: live incumbents; 3: bundling) **both land**, and the pricing anchor collapses. Survives only if reshaped around two assets the miner never found.

## 1. Primary text: re-verified, premise holds (Pattern 4 SURVIVES)

Fetched 45 CFR Part 180 whole from eCFR today (44,298 bytes, HTTP 200). Every load-bearing claim checks out **verbatim**:

- **180.50(a)(3)(iii)–(iv)**: "Beginning January 1, 2026, attest in its machine-readable file the following: To the best of its knowledge and belief…" plus "encode the name of the hospital chief executive officer, president, or senior official designated…". Confirmed.
- **180.50(b)(2)(i)(A)**: "beginning January 1, 2026, Type 2 (organizational) National Provider Identifier(s) (NPI)". Confirmed.
- **180.50(b)(2)(ii)(C)(2)**: "Beginning January 1, 2026, calculate and encode the tenth (10th) percentile allowed amount, the median allowed amount, and the ninetieth (90th) percentile allowed amount… Hospitals must also calculate and encode the total number of allowed amount remittances". Confirmed.
- **180.50(c)(2)** CMS-template conformance from 1 July 2024; **180.50(e)** "at least once annually"; **180.50(d)(5)** naming convention. All confirmed.
- **180.90(c)(2)**: $300 ≤30 beds; beds×$10 for 31–550; $5,500 >550; CPI-adjusted. Confirmed. Amendment trail ends at **90 FR 54087, Nov. 25, 2025** (the CY2026 rule).
- CY2026 fact sheet confirms the *method* claims verbatim: lookback "no less than 12 months and no longer than 15 months", "EDI 835 electronic remittance advice (ERA) or an alternative, equivalent source", and "use the next highest observed value".

**But the miner overstated scope.** Parsing the v3.0.0 schema's `payers_information.allOf`: `median_amount`/`10th_percentile`/`90th_percentile` are required **only if** `standard_charge_percentage` or `standard_charge_algorithm` is present **and** `count != "0"`. If `count == "0"`, only `additional_payer_notes` is required. A hospital that encodes dollar amounts throughout — which CMS's own May-2025 guidance pushes it toward — **owes no percentiles at all**. The 835 WASM parser, the card's hardest differentiator and its stated moat layer, is **optional**, and CMS's FAQ (fetched today, last-modified 2026-06-29) explicitly blesses "revenue cycle systems, EHRs, payer portals, clearinghouses, or third-party data aggregators" as substitutes for 835s.

## 2. The hosting question — answered, and it cuts both ways

**180.50(d)(1)**: "A hospital must select a **publicly available website**." No own-domain requirement. Vendor hosting is legal. **But 180.50(d)(6)(i)** puts the `cms-hpt.txt` "in the root folder" of that website, and CMS's TXT FAQ (last-modified 2024-08-29) is explicit: "you must place the .txt file on the root of the domain of the public website your hospital has selected to host its machine-readable file… without regard to page structure." One .txt may carry multiple entries.

So the hosting tier is legal via per-tenant subdomains. **And it is where incumbents are failing.** I tested the vendor domains actually serving warned CAHs today: `apps.para-hcfs.com/cms-hpt.txt` → **404**; `sthpiprd.blob.core.windows.net/cms-hpt.txt` → **400**; `search.hospitalpriceindex.com/cms-hpt.txt` and `cdmpricing.com/cms-hpt.txt` → HTTP 200 but serving the Angular SPA shell, i.e. soft-404. **Not one vendor-hosting domain serves a real root .txt.** That is precisely the violation CMS penalized.

## 3. The enforcement story is weaker than the card claims

I found CMS's **primary machine-readable enforcement dataset** (13,355 rows, modified 2026-08-14) — the miner missed it and used an AP secondary instead.

| | |
|---|---|
| Warning Notices, 2026 (to 7/31) | **953** |
| CAP Requests, 2026 | **426** |
| **CMP Notices, 2026** | **2 — both the same hospital (Pinnacle)** |
| Unique hospitals ever warned | **2,973** |
| Unique hospitals ever penalized | **28** |

A warned hospital's chance of ever paying a CMP is **~0.9%**. "Met Requirements" (3,620) and "Closure Notice" (3,869) dominate outcomes: hospitals fix it and the case closes.

**The 519 figure does not reconcile as warning letters.** CMS's own rows for 2026-04-01 → 2026-06-02 give 322 Warning Notices. Warnings **+ CAP Requests** = 540 rows / 530 hospitals, state distribution TX 42, CA 39, IN 35, LA 28 — matching the AP's "TX 42, CA 38, IN 34, LA 27" almost exactly. So 519 is a **mixed count of two different pipeline stages**, and no CMS primary page publishes it.

**Worse for the pitch: the $51,615 is cited for the wrong section.** I extracted both Pinnacle PDFs. The June 3 2026 notice (UCN 2026HPT002) confirms "A CMP of $51,615.00 was imposed" — but the material violation itemized is **one item under § 180.60**, consumer-friendly shoppable services, which ChargeFile does not produce. The Feb 4 notice (UCN 2026HPT001) cites seven MRF violations, of which **six are the .txt file and the footer link** and the seventh is "no online machine-readable file was found." CMS is penalizing *absence and plumbing*, not schema conformance, percentiles, attestation or NPI. The daily rate is now **$342** (CPI-adjusted from $300).

## 4. Kill patterns 2 and 3: REFUTED

The miner hunted Turquoise, Rivet, Panacea, PMMC, Payerset — all analytics/enterprise players — and concluded the artifact "is not currently bundled." I sampled the vendors actually serving **warned critical access hospitals today**, from their live `cms-hpt.txt` files:

- **Benson Hospital** (CAH, AZ, warned 7/23/2026) → **Hospital Price Index**, a **Health Catalyst** (NASDAQ: HCAT) product, MRF hosted on HPI's Azure blob. Its page states: *"HPI Staging tool creates and publishes MRFs directly, reducing file production time from roughly 8 hours per facility to between 20 minutes and 1 hour"* and *"HPI calculates the required median, 10th percentile, and 90th percentile allowed amounts using ERA/835 claims data"*, plus updated attestation, CEO/designee, and Type 2 NPI encoding.
- **North Valley Health Center** (CAH, MN) → **PARA HealthCare Financial Services** (HFRI), which publishes a "CAH Revenue Integrity Program," hosting the MRF on `apps.para-hcfs.com`.
- **North Valley Hospital** (CAH, WA, warned 7/29/2026) → **cdmpricing.com**.
- **GoRev**: *"released a new reporting feature that allows facilities to easily create their machine-readable files and post them directly to their public websites."*
- **Sliced Health**: *"CMS-compliant MRFs… including the new 2026 requirements for median and percentile allowed amounts drawn from actual ERA data."*
- **Stroudwater**, advising this exact buyer: *"Many vendors are already rolling out modules for automated EDI 835 pulls, percentile analytics, and MRF generation—often with built-in attestation workflows,"* and tells rural hospitals to go to their **EMR vendor and clearinghouse**.

The card's designated "top risk," Craneware's free Trisus, is confirmed *not* an MRF generator — the miner aimed at the wrong competitor while the real ones went unseen.

## 5. The pricing hypothesis is dead as stated

Health Catalyst's own marketing puts manual MRF production at **~8 hours per facility**. At a loaded CAH analyst rate that is roughly $400–600 of labour for an annual artifact. The card's "0.2–0.4 FTE" anchor is contradicted by the incumbent's own number by an order of magnitude. **$3,900/yr is not defensible**; the CMP anchor is a ~1% event cited against the wrong CFR section.

## 6. What genuinely survives, and what to build instead

Patterns **1, 5, 6 survive cleanly** — a rare, real strength. CMS ships only a validator, filename wizard and TXT generator (all three re-fetched today, all formatting helpers). There is no prose corpus, no scraper fleet, no jurisdictional fan-out: one federal rule, one JSON Schema, one CC0 validator at **2.6.0 published 2026-08-18**. The Hospital General Information CSV is exactly as claimed: **5,419 rows** — 3,107 Acute Care, 1,382 CAH, 624 Psychiatric, 43 REH.

Two assets the miner missed are worth more than the ones it claimed:

1. **The enforcement dataset is a named, dated, monthly-refreshed lead list.** Cross-referencing it to hospital type: of 953 hospitals warned in 2026, 557 matched, of which **146 are CAHs and 3 REHs**. Since 2026-04-01, **159 distinct CAH/REH** received a Warning Notice or CAP Request — ~450/yr in the exact target segment, each with name, address, city, state, action and date. That is the go-to-market, and it is free and public.
2. **The vendor-hosting `.txt` defect is real, empirically demonstrated, and is the single violation CMS actually penalizes.** A product that hosts on a per-tenant subdomain *and* serves a correct root `cms-hpt.txt` fixes the top enforced deficiency. The footer link (180.50(d)(6)(ii)) remains the hospital's own job and cannot be delivered.

**Reshape**: drop the $3,900 tier and the 835 engine as the core. Sell the **$99–$299 one-time validator-report-plus-gap-list to hospitals CMS has just named publicly**, and a low-hundreds/yr hosting-and-`.txt` tier. Engine-never-arbiter holds — the arithmetic is spec-defined and the customer's CEO signs the attestation — provided the product never says "compliant," only "passed validator vN against schema vM on date D."

### Where the card is wrong or narrower than stated

**1. Cite error.** Annual update is 45 CFR **180.50(e)** ("Frequency of updates"), not 180.50(b)(3) as the card's artifact field says.

**2. Percentiles are conditional, not universal.** Both the CFR — 180.50(b)(2)(ii)(C) opens "If the standard charge is based on a percentage or algorithm" — and CMS's own 41-slide webinar deck confirm the four allowed-amount elements fire *only* on percentage/algorithm entries. CMS slide 21 is explicit: "If the hospital can 'do the math' and use the percentage of a known fee schedule to calculate a 'Payer-Specific Negotiated Charge: Dollar,' the hospital must do so" — and then "the hospital would not be required to encode the allowed amounts." **The RVU corpus is therefore the spine, not a side-corpus: dollarising %-of-Medicare is how you *avoid* the 835 entirely.** The 835 path narrows to %-of-billed-charges (CMS's worked example: 87% of billed charges), per diem, case rate and algorithms — which CMS confirms are expressible as free-text `standard_charge_algorithm` (answering lens 5) but still drag the percentiles with them.

**3. The `count: "0"` escape hatch.** The schema's `allOf` makes median/10th/90th required *unless* `count` is `"0"`, in which case only `additional_payer_notes` is required. CMS's slide 22 blesses this: encode "0" plus the reason. Counts 1–10 must be the literal string `"1 through 10"`. A CAH can ship a validator-clean v3.0 file with zero remittance analytics. That is the competitive floor for the whole 835 half of the product.

**4. "Alternative, equivalent source" materially loosens the 835 requirement.** Verbatim, twice today: CY2026 fact sheet — "EDI 835 electronic remittance advice (ERA) **or an alternative, equivalent source of remittance data**"; CMS webinar — "...or an alternative, equivalent source of remittance data that includes the same information as EDI 835 ERA transaction data would include." A posted-payments extract from the patient-accounting system qualifies. The in-browser X12/WASM parser is a nice-to-have, not a moat, and it is the highest-friction input in the funnel.

**5. CMS never defines how to derive an allowed amount from an 835.** 180.20 says only "total allowed amounts the hospital has historically received." Neither the CFR, the fact sheet, nor the webinar names a segment. The commonly repeated CLP04+CLP05 rule is commentary. CLP is claim-level; the MRF is per code — clean for MS-DRG rows (CMS's own examples), messy for mixed outpatient claims needing SVC-line allocation. **This is the real engine-never-arbiter pressure point**: the card's pattern-7 answer assumes "allowed amount" is a field to sort. It is a rule to choose. The derivation must be a customer-selected, worksheet-printed setting, never a product default presented as arithmetic.

That is kill pattern 3 materialising, not hypothetical. And Stroudwater's own 2026 analysis states flatly: "Many vendors are already rolling out modules for automated EDI 835 pulls, percentile analytics, and MRF generation—often with built-in attestation workflows."

## Second and third incumbents the card missed

The card asserts Turquoise has "no MRF creation offering." Turquoise publishes an entire MRF-generation knowledge base (`llms.txt` index, HTTP 200, 2,626 b). Its `claims-data` page (updated 2026-01-16) states: *"The Turquoise process for reporting the Percentile Allowed Amounts involves calculating the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentile reimbursement amounts (by service and payer) **directly from 835 remittance files**."* SlicedHealth — explicitly aimed at community hospitals with *"lean compliance and IT teams"* — advertises *"CMS-compliant MRFs… including the new 2026 requirements for median and percentile allowed amounts drawn from actual ERA data,"* using *"actual ERA/835 remittance data going back 12–15 months."* Both are quote-gated, so the price umbrella is not proven closed from below by *price*; it is closed from below by *zero*.

## Premise drift vs primary text (kill pattern 4, partial)

From eCFR today (45 CFR 180, HTTP 200, 44,298 b): §180.50(b)(2)(ii)(C) requires the 10th/median/90th/count **only** where "the standard charge is based on a percentage or algorithm." Flat DRG rates, per diems, case rates and even straight percent-of-charges resolve to dollars and need no percentiles. The card's framing ("four harder fields" replacing one) is right in kind but materially narrower in scope than presented. Separately, the codified text nowhere says "835" or "electronic remittance advice" — it says "the total number of allowed amount **remittances**." The EDI-835 sourcing is preamble/guidance, not regulation, and the card cites it as if it were §180.50 text.

## The HIPAA claim is asserted, not established (lens b)

45 CFR 164.514(b)(2), fetched today (HTTP 200, 19,546 b), requires removal of *"All elements of dates (except year) for dates directly related to an individual"* plus catch-all (R) *"Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code."* The card's transmitted tuple carries a "service date bucket" — anything finer than year breaks safe harbor outright. Fixable (compute the window client-side, ship no date), but the card states a legal conclusion it has not earned. The deeper problem is commercial, not legal: HHS's cloud FAQ says a BAA is required with any provider *"creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting"* ePHI on the entity's behalf — and Turquoise and SlicedHealth already sign those and already take the 835s. The BAA is therefore not the moat the card claims; it is a solved cost of doing business for incumbents, while ChargeFile still faces the same security review with a novel "trust our WASM" story a 25-bed CAH's privacy officer has never seen.

## The demand spike points elsewhere (lens d)

CMS does not publish a warning-letter list; its enforcement register (last updated **June 11, 2026**) lists **25** CMP notices in ~4 years, not the 28 the card claims. Matching those names against CMS's own hospital file: only **Bucktail Medical Center (PA)**, **Fulton County (CAH)** and **Falls Community Hospital and Clinic (TX, Rural Emergency Hospital)** sit in the small/rural bucket; the rest are acute care, psychiatric or proprietary. And the 519-letter wave, per Advisory Board's June 15 2026 briefing, went to *"acute-care hospitals, behavioral health hospitals, surgical hospitals, and inpatient rehabilitation facilities"* — **CAHs and REHs are not named**. The urgency is real; it is not aimed at the buyer.

## What survives

CMS still ships no generator (validator, filename wizard, cms-hpt.txt generator only) — kill pattern 1 holds against the *agency*. No state generator and no open-source generator surfaced. The corpus is genuinely alive and genuinely single-jurisdiction (patterns 5 and 6 hold). Pattern 7 discipline in the card is exemplary. None of that matters when the artifact is free from Craneware.

**Verdict: REFUTED.** Not on data, not on regulation, not on liability — on the one risk the card itself flagged and then talked itself out of with a single-page grep.
