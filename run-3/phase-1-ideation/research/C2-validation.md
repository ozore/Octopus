## 4. State fragmentation — better AND worse than the card said

**Better:** California CIWQS eSMR's published example file has a **byte-identical header to NetDMR's 31 columns** (`permitted_feature_id,limit_set_txt,mped_txt,…,freq_analysis_cd,sample_type_cd`). CA's 1,237 permits need no second emitter. Nevada runs a state-hosted NetDMR clone (same format).

**Worse — and this is the finding that reshapes the idea.** **Ohio EPA's e-DMR does the calculation for you, free:** *"eDMR only transfers your raw data to our system, not the statistics. **Our internal system calculates the needed statistics on our end.**"* and *"Your permit limits are not documented in e-DMR and are not involved in the e-DMR process."* Ohio = **4,307** effective NPDES individual permits where the regulator is the free substitute. Ohio's import is XML, a third format. **Pennsylvania** (**12,061** permits) publishes **no bulk import at all** — per-permit web template entry. **North Carolina** (1,648) has CSV import but a *different* positional Excel-template spec.

## 03 Competition & pricing — verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED

# C2 — Outfall: Deep Validation

**Verdict: SURVIVES_RESHAPED.** The mandate and the corpus are the strongest I have tested in this run — genuinely live, genuinely machine-readable, genuinely complete down to a school district's outfall. But the miner's kill-pattern-1 clearance is **false**, and two of the three load-bearing moat legs are already free.

## What held up

**The corpus is real, and it works for the hard case.** I re-fetched the ECHO effluent chart for IA0067156 (Adair-Casey Community School District, `CWPMajorMinorStatusFlag='N'`) today: HTTP 200, 411,094 bytes, **327 limit/DMR rows across 8 parameters, 283 carrying a numeric limit**. Limits effective `01-MAR-24 → 28-FEB-29`; monitoring periods through `31-MAY-26`; newest `ValueReceivedDate` `16-APR-26`; `DMRDueDate`, `NODICode`, `LimitValueQualifierCode` and `StatisticalBaseDesc` all populated. This is not a metadata ghost — kill pattern 6 survives cleanly on the minor permits that are the actual buyer.

**The pain is stated by the regulator, verbatim.** I extracted the Oregon DEQ guide (40pp) myself: *"NetDMR is only set up to accept summary statistics, not raw data collected more frequently than monthly. As a result, permittees need to perform calculations on raw data to enter into the eDMR."* The guide then teaches geometric mean, 7-day moving average and mass loading by hand.

**The lab does not do this job.** I chased the sharpest unexplored angle — does the contract lab deliver a DMR-ready report? No. SPL (2,000+ clients/month): *"Accurate DMR preparation starts with accurate data. SPL's results are delivered in formats that directly support your DMR workflow."* Phoslab: data *"help clients comply with the requirements of discharge monitoring reports."* Both supply the input; neither computes the artifact. **This risk is retired.**

**Price anchors re-verified.** Effluent Labs still publishes $1,500 / $3,500 / $5,500+ per month verbatim. Hach WIMS: **$27,140 one-time + $4,885.20/yr**; Operator10: **$14,165 + $1,970/yr** — both from the same municipal packet, `last-modified 2022-10-11`.

## What broke

**1. Kill pattern 1 fails — a free first-party generator exists.** Pennsylvania DEP ships a free downloadable Excel *Daily Effluent Monitoring* spreadsheet: *"Enter daily results and the statistical values will be computed for entry into DMR reports"* — 15 parameters per discharge point, expanded versions to 30 parameters/1 outfall and **90 parameters/3 outfalls**. That is precisely Outfall's computational core, shipped free by the permitting authority, and sized past Outfall's entire $89 tier (12 parameters). MPCA ships free calculation worksheets; TCEQ ships a free geometric-mean DMR calculator. The card's claim — *"a free validator/transmitter exists; a free generator does not"* — is refuted.

**2. The moat's front door is already free.** Georgia EPD's NetDMR FAQ: *"the information in NetDMR that describes your permit, effluent limits, etc., is pre-programmed into NetDMR by EPA."* Moat layer (1) — "permit number in, complete limit set out" — is what the free mandated channel already does when the permittee logs in. Outfall's ECHO call is convenience, not a structural gap.

**3. The buyer count does not reproduce.** The miner's exact cited call returns an **error** today, not a number: `"Rows Returned would be 100352. Queryset Limit would be exceeded - please make search parameters more selective."` So the national figure is ~100,352, not 92,846, and the cited endpoint yields no `QueryRows` at all. The Iowa slice *does* reproduce exactly (`QueryRows 1835`, `TotalPenalties $212,560`), so the method is sound — but the headline number is miscited.

**4. Fragmentation is larger than "not yet proven."** Florida, primary: *"Effective Dec. 21, 2016, all NPDES facilities are required to submit DMRs electronically using EzDMR"* — no NetDMR. Pennsylvania, primary: *"DMRs may be submitted on paper to DEP regional offices or electronically through DEP's eDMR system."* California runs CIWQS eSMR (CSV upload, own format). A secondary source (Klir) lists 11 eDMR-only states — NV, KS, OK, MO, OH, WV, PA, VA, NC, SC, NJ. With CA and FL that is **~13 states carved out**, including four of the five largest. NetDMR-only v1 loses a material share of ~100k permits.

**5. The arithmetic-mean premise is thinner than stated.** The very permit the miner used as proof returns `StatisticalBaseDesc` values `GEO MEAN` and `7 DA AVG` — the CFR's "arithmetic mean" default is not operative for E. coli or 7-day averages. ICIS's `StatisticalBaseCode` resolves most of this (a genuine strength), but Oregon defers below-detection-limit handling to *"DEQ's Quality Assurance Guidance for Self-Monitoring Laboratories (NPDES and WPCF), January 2019"* and carries an E. coli resample-demonstration rule that lives in the permit. That is **per-state prose** — a real A4 exposure the card underrates.

**6. Locus is a closer competitor than admitted.** *"automating the data assembly, calculations, and formatting of Discharge Monitoring Reports"*, *"New formats, such as Florida DEP ezDMR, are regularly being added"*, users *"saved over $2,000,000 on DMR reporting."* Demo-gated, no price, mid-to-large. Separately, Mapistry publishes **$298/yr** entry (Capterra) — an environmental-compliance SaaS floor under Outfall's $1,068/yr, though DMR generation is unconfirmed at that tier.

## The reshaping

The residual defensible value is narrower than the card claims: **lab-EDD column mapping + the NetDMR import file + the ICIS limit cross-check + the audit worksheet.** The calculation itself is free in PA/MN/TX; the limit set is free in NetDMR. That is a real but thin wedge against a free spreadsheet, and $89/month must be justified by the mapping and the ledger, not by the arithmetic. Engine-never-arbiter survives — the product computes order statistics on the customer's own numbers and the permittee signs in NetDMR — provided BDL convention and NODI selection stay customer-set and worksheet-printed, which the card already specifies.

## 04 Kill-thesis — verdict: REFUTED

# C2 — Outfall: REFUTED

## (a) The pain is ~10 numbers a month, and 40% of them require no arithmetic

I did not accept the card's framing; I measured it. I pulled ECHO's own effluent-chart JSON for 40 randomly sampled Iowa NPDES individual permits (seed-fixed sample of the 1,835-row state roster ECHO returned today) plus the card's own poster child, ADAIR-CASEY COMMUNITY SCHOOL DISTRICT (IA0067156).

**Median DMR value-cells per monitoring period, across the 19 sampled permits carrying reported data: 10** (mean 11.4, min 3, max 25). Small-town STPs cluster at 6–25. The card's own exemplar carries 8 parameters at one outfall, 15–24 cells/month.

Worse for the premise: across those permits I found **475 concentration parameter-periods carrying both a reported average and a reported maximum, and in 188 of them (40%) the two numbers are identical** — meaning exactly one sample was taken that month, so the "monthly average" is a transcription, not a calculation. IA0067156's January-2025 BOD is `168` for both `30DA AVG` and `DAILY MX`.

So the honest task size is: ~10 cells, ~6 of which need arithmetic, over a handful of lab rows. Oregon DEQ's own worked example (2026-07-20 guide) uses **8 BOD samples in a month**. This is a 20–40 minute monthly task. It does not support $89/month against a free alternative, let alone the card's $1,500/month anchor.

## (b) The buyer — and the anchor — do not survive

The card's entire willingness-to-pay case rests on one URL: Effluent Labs at $1,500/mo. Fetched today, effluentlabs.com has **no team page, no founder names, no physical address, no customer names, no case studies, no testimonials, an empty blog, and a 2026 copyright**. It is a pre-launch landing page, not evidence money is changing hands. It is also evidence that someone else already has this exact idea and is trying to sell it at 17× the hypothesised price without visible traction.

The AllMax $14,165 figure is real but is a plant-wide SCADA/lab data system — not a comparable. Ecesis, Klir and Hach WIMS are all quote-gated. So: **zero verified transacted price for the artifact, at any price point.**

On the operator: contract operations firms already fold DMR preparation into their service (Indigo Water Group states it "completes and files the monthly Discharge Monitoring Report with the State"), and states such as NC require contract operators to register. The economic buyer for a school-district STP is a contract operator whose $/month for the site already includes the DMR — a $89/month per-permit add-on competes with their own labour, not with $1,500.

## (c) Engine-never-arbiter survives formally; the product it leaves is a worksheet

40 CFR 122.41(l)(4)(iii) does prescribe the arithmetic mean, so a deterministic engine is defensible. But 122.41(k)(2), fetched verbatim today, is harsher than the card conveys: a knowingly false statement in a monitoring report is punishable by "a fine of not more than $10,000 per violation, or by imprisonment for not more than 6 months **per violation**." And 122.41(j)(5) reaches anyone who "falsifies, tampers with, or knowingly renders inaccurate any monitoring device **or method**" — $10,000 / 2 years.

The mens rea is "knowingly," so a good-faith signer relying on a buggy tool is not criminally exposed. But the 122.22(d) certification, fetched verbatim, requires the signer to attest that the document was "prepared under my direction or supervision **in accordance with a system designed to assure that qualified personnel properly gather and evaluate the information submitted**." An unattended $89/month SaaS is precisely the substitution of software for the "qualified personnel … evaluate" step the certification describes. And the exposure is symmetric: an over-computed value self-reports a strict-liability CWA §309 effluent violation; an under-computed one is a false report.

Conclusion: **the honest product is a calculation worksheet the operator verifies** — which the card half-concedes. Once conceded, the deliverable is a nicer spreadsheet for ten numbers.

## (d)+(1)/(3) Free first-party substitutes are richer than the card admits

The card's kill-pattern-1 evidence is Oregon's NetDMR User's Guide. **Oregon no longer uses NetDMR.** Its current 81-page DMR guide (6.0 MB, last-modified 2026-07-20) contains **zero occurrences of "NetDMR"** — Oregon moved to Your DEQ Online. And YDO does much of Outfall's job free: it auto-generates a permit-specific `DMRSummaryUploadTemplate.csv` pre-populated with that permit's substances, monitoring locations, required summary statistics and units; it offers a Daily Data tab where raw sample results are entered directly for many permit types; and it copy-paste imports.

Elsewhere: **Minnesota MPCA e-services states "a feature in e-services can be used to calculate the required limit types for parameters on the DMRs"** — a free first-party calculator. Pennsylvania eDMR "generat[es] specifically for their permit" a reporting template. NC DEQ ships a free Excel template that produces the eDMR upload CSV. TCEQ publishes a free geometric-mean calculator XLSX for DMR completion. Pattern 1 does not survive as claimed; pattern 3 (bundled free in the regulator's own portal) is live.

## (e) TAM: the 92,846 is wrong twice over

The identical ECHO query today returns an error reading **"Rows Returned would be 100352"** — not 92,846. More important, **12 of the 52 resolvable Iowa permits I sampled (23%) return zero outfalls, zero parameters and zero DMR rows** — feedlots, farms, DOT garages, a pipeline blanket permit, one city STP. They have no DMR product to buy.

I could not obtain a NetDMR state list: EPA's contact selector sits behind CDX, `netdmr-web/public/home.htm` returns HTTP 500 (`java.lang.NullPointerException`), and the registration app is a 144-byte CDX redirect.
