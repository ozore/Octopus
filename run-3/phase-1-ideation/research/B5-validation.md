**CPT licensing is broader than the card assumes.** The card's mitigation is "build on CMS-published HCPCS and code numbers... must not redistribute CPT descriptor text without a license." AMA's own FAQ (updated Jul 14, 2026) says: "If your organization uses, references or displays CPT content, your organization is expected to obtain the appropriate license"; "Distributor license for health technology organizations... Both CMS delivery paths carry click-wrap: the NCCI PTP full files are served through `cms.gov/license/ama?file=...` ("Any use not authorized herein is prohibited... making any commercial use of CPT"), and the MCD Downloads page gates on a stricter grant — "authorized to use CPT only as contained herein for **your personal use only. Personal use means non-commercial uses**." The MCD article code table ships AMA descriptors verbatim (e.g. 29877 → "ARTHROSCOPY, KNEE, SURGICAL; DEBRIDEMENT/SHAVING OF ARTICULAR CARTILAGE"). Build legality requires an AMA distributor license before line one — which is what the card said, then mitigated away incorrectly.

### The arithmetic hole nobody caught

The $149 entry offer is "upload a code+volume CSV." A PTP edit is a **pair** billed on the same beneficiary on the same date of service; a per-code volume total contains no co-occurrence information, so PTP dollar exposure is not computable from the accepted input. MUE exposure needs units-per-claim distributions, not totals — and CMS states "other MUE values are confidential. Confidential MUE values are not releasable," so MUE coverage is structurally incomplete. Getting the real inputs means 837/835 line-level ingestion, which the card excludes by design to stay out of HIPAA scope. The headline dollar figure is the product, and the cheap input can't produce it.

### Narrow survival path (not enough to change the verdict)

An HCPCS-Level-II-only product (DME, J-codes, supplies) escapes AMA entirely — the quarterly alpha-numeric HCPCS files carry no license gate. But that shrinks the buyer pool to DME/supplier niches that DME MACs already blanket with free listservs, and PolicyChanges.app/PayerPolicy cover those specialties too.

## Dollar exposure and self-serve

Deterministic and feasible: PPRRVU2026_Jul × GPCI2026 × locality (`26LOCCO.xlsx`) × conversion factor. But NCCI/MUE/LCD govern **Medicare FFS only**; MA and commercial payers adopt variants. In fact CMS ships the code layer structured: `article_x_hcpc_code`, `article_x_icd10_covered` (52.7 MB), `article_x_icd10_noncovered`, `article_x_hcpc_modifier`, `article_x_revenue_code`, joined to jurisdiction via `article_x_contractor` → `contractor_jurisdiction` → `state_lookup`. A deterministic, MAC-aware, code-level diff is buildable with no prose extraction and no scraper fleet.

That is the good news, and it is the end of it.

## Kill 1 — the "proprietary longitudinal diff" is published free by CMS, on both halves

The card's stated moat: *"CMS publishes snapshots, not changelogs, so a maintained revision history … is assembly work nobody sells."* Both clauses are false, verified from the primary files today.

*Coverage side:* the LCD export contains `lcd_x_revision_history.csv` (7,977 rows) with human-written explanations — e.g. `R3 | Revision Effective: 10/01/2015 | Revision Explanation: Icd-10 code in range T63.421-T63.464 have been added…` — plus `lcd_x_reason_change.csv`. On top of that CMS runs a free **Local Coverage What's New Report** (pick any week back one year — current week 08/09/2026 — and any contractor) *and* a free Coverage API endpoint that "retrieves the contents of the Local Coverage What's New Report, a list of recently added and updated Local Coverage documents." No API key required since 8 Feb 2024; 10,000 req/s throttle.

*NCCI side:* CMS publishes the quarterly diff itself, **ungated**. ## Kill 3 — the incumbent is cheaper and already bundles all of it

The bad pair is blocked before it leaves the office. "Advance weather" therefore does not prevent the denial — the scrubber already does. It only buys workflow lead time.

**And that lead time is already free and structural:** NCCI Q3 posted 1 June, effective 1 July (~30 days). Final LCDs carry a CMS-mandated "minimum of 45 days" notice period. MLN Connects ships weekly and free (latest edition 8/20/2026). Noridian's listserv is free, twice weekly, topic-filterable. MCD lets you click **Subscribe** on any individual LCD or Article. Specialty societies hand members free PFS impact calculators (ACC, 19 Nov 2025).

## Kill 4 — CPT licensing, and arbiter strain

CMS's own MCD license, fetched today: *"You are authorized to use CPT only as contained herein for your personal use only,"* prohibiting *"creating any modified or derivative work of CPT, or making any commercial use of CPT,"* with *"License to use CPT for any use not authorized herein must be obtained through the AMA."* The full NCCI PTP edit files sit behind a `/license/ama?file=…` interstitial. Every NCCI change file opens with *"CPT codes, descriptions and other data only are copyright 2025 American Medical Association."* CMS's exports do carry full CPT long descriptors (e.g. `ARTHROSCOPY, KNEE, SURGICAL; DEBRIDEMENT/SHAVING OF ARTICULAR CARTILAGE`) — the data is right there and commercially unusable without a licence. The mitigation ("numbers only") ships a product that says "97110 changed" with no descriptor, while the $44/mo incumbent shows the descriptor legally.

Separately, "$41k of exposure over your last 90 days, effective October 1" is a dollar forecast the customer acts on — engine-as-arbiter. The defensible form is "these 3 pairs in your list changed; here is the CMS record and its effective date," which is precisely the free What's New report with a filter.

## What would survive

Only a much smaller thing: a free weekly "what changed, filtered to your saved codes" digest as an SEO/list-building surface, monetised elsewhere. That is not a $99–799/mo business, and the card offers no competitor price and no buyer evidence to argue otherwise.
