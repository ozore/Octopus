- Card-swipe conversion at a hospital remains completely untested and NRHA guidance ('work with a compliance expert or agency') points the other way. The $149 dossier is the cheap experiment; if the first 40 enforcement-triggered sends convert at zero, the thesis is dead and should be killed fast.
- CMP base rate is ~1% (2,973 hospitals ever warned, 28-29 ever penalized; 2 CMP notices in all of 2026, both to one hospital). The urgency must be sold as the named public warning letter and the CEO's own attestation signature, never as penalty avoidance — and that is a materially weaker sales story.
- Any incumbent can close the locator flank in an afternoon. Serving a correct root cms-hpt.txt is a single static text file; Health Catalyst, PARA and cdmpricing could each fix theirs at trivial cost, which would erase the sharpest wedge. The defensible residue is then independence (a vendor cannot audit itself) and the dated ledger, both weaker.
- Publishing per-hospital observation records creates reputational and defamation exposure. Mitigation is absolute discipline: observed facts only (HTTP status, field presence, filename, content-type, validator error codes and line numbers), no compliance verdict, no ranking or grading language, no penalty prediction, a visible correction path, and same-day re-check on request.
- AMA CPT descriptor licensing is untested and unresolved. Public pages and dossiers must carry conformance metadata only and must never republish descriptor text from a hospital's MRF; this constrains how rich the free pages can be.
- Crawl legitimacy and hygiene: the files are ones 45 CFR 180.50(d)(3)-(4) requires to be public, free, login-free and directly downloadable, which is about as clean an obtainability story as exists — but 5,196 weekly fetches of multi-megabyte files needs robots.txt respect, rate limiting, conditional requests and a published crawler identity, or the company becomes the abuse complaint.
- Validator parity is unproven: @cmsgov/hpt-validator 2.6.0 (CC0) is embeddable, but the report could not confirm the hosted CMS Online Validator runs the same version or ruleset. Every output must state 'validator vN against schema vM on date D' precisely, and a divergence from CMS's hosted tool would be a credibility event.
- Spec churn is a live treadmill in both directions: six validator releases in 2026. A missed version bump silently degrades every customer's monitor, so staleness detection must fail loud and mark records stale rather than continuing to imply currency.
- Regulatory reversal risk: AHA is lobbying to sunset parts of the framework. A repeal or a material narrowing of 180.50(d)(6) would remove the locator duty that the entire wedge is built on.
- The pivot away from generation means abandoning every claimed moat in the original card (835/WASM parser, RVU translation table, no-BAA posture, generation ledger). What remains is a thin, fast, cheap product whose only durable asset is the observation ledger — if that ledger turns out not to be something buyers will pay for, there is no second line of defence.

**Why winnable:** Every functional competitor re-verified in the last 48 hours — Ecesis, Klir, Locus, Hach WIMS, AllMax — is demo- or quote-gated with no published price and no self-serve path, which leaves the entire sub-$100/month card-swipe band structurally empty in a market of ~100,352 permits. The corpus that powers both the product and its whole SEO surface is free, national, weekly-refreshed and proven live to the byte (newest received value six days old). And the one function no free state tool performs anywhere examined — diffing what EPA has actually received against what the permit requires, by due date — is precisely the failure mode EPA itself attests is 'the single largest source of SNC nationwide' and 'over half of the facilities in SNC status.' The honest doubt, stated plainly: the arithmetic is genuinely small — median 10 value-cells a month, 40% of average/maximum pairs identical — and the enforcement expectation for a minor permittee is about $815 over a lifetime with only 4.8% ever facing formal action. The whole thesis therefore rests on one untested proposition: that a small permittee or a contract operator will pay ~$470/yr for a deadline-and-completeness watch on a public federal file. Nothing in four validation lenses closes that question, and it should be tested by free-tier-to-paid conversion before meaningful spend, not assumed.

**Residual risks:**

- No verified transacted price exists for this artifact at ANY price point — the $39/$129/$349 ladder is derived from adjacent anchors (Mapistry's published $298/yr floor, AllMax's $1,970/yr support line), not from an observed sale of a DMR generator. Willingness to pay is the single biggest open question and must be tested by free-tier conversion first.
- The measured task is tiny (median 10 cells per period; 40% of concentration average/maximum pairs identical, i.e. one sample and zero arithmetic). If the received-vs-required watch does not carry the price on its own, the calculator cannot, and the company has no price at all.
- Free first-party substitutes are moving in the direction that kills the file product: Ohio computes statistics server-side and does not even hold permit limits in eDMR, and Oregon's Your DEQ Online auto-generates a permit-specific pre-populated CSV. If NetDMR adds a calculator or more states adopt Ohio's raw-data-in model, the emitter dies; only the watch layer survives.
- NetDMR end-to-end acceptance is still untested — no lens submitted a generated import file, and it is unknown whether the format requires NetDMR-internal identifiers only visible inside the permittee's own account. This must be closed with a real CDX/Login.gov account before any paid claim about the file is made.
- Per-authority calculation prose (MAL substitution, BDL reported at the QL, geometric-mean zero-substitution, flow-weighted averaging, the fewer-than-four-samples fallback, calendar-week weekly averages) is a real ~47-jurisdiction build. Shipping into an authority whose convention is not published risks producing a number the permittee certifies under 40 CFR 122.22 — the exposure is symmetric, since an over-computed value self-reports a strict-liability CWA 309 violation and an under-computed one is a false report.
- Effective TAM is materially below 100,352 and unquantified: 23% of sampled permits returned zero outfalls, parameters and DMR rows; ~34.7% sit in state-eDMR jurisdictions for the file product; and the monthly-vs-annual monitoring split remains unknown after four lenses tried to resolve it.
- Contract operators cut both ways and it is unresolved which dominates: the same firms that would buy the portfolio tier already fold DMR preparation into their service fee, so their alternative is their own labour rather than a purchase.
- The D80/D90 enforcement-event email is legally clean (public record, opt-out, factual) but reputationally delicate; a single mis-framed subject line implying a violation would breach engine-never-arbiter in the marketing layer even while the product itself stays clean. Copy must be template-locked and reviewed as a mechanism claim.
- The 526 MB weekly ETL is a hard operational dependency with known silent-failure modes: a terminated permit returns HTTP 200 with an empty PermFeatures array (AL0074373), and 13% of one permit's rows carry a null LimitValueNmbr. Undetected, these emit blank or wrong artifacts.
- Effluent Labs, whatever it is, already occupies this positioning and its search surface; if it is funded rather than pre-revenue, the generic-keyword lane is closed and the strategy depends entirely on the permit-ID long tail ranking.

**Residual risks:**

- WILLINGNESS TO PAY IS THE WHOLE BET AND IS UNTESTED. Not one price, invoice, quote or listing exists for this artifact anywhere; the nearest evidence is IA footnote 20's n<5, 5x-wide £3,000–£15,000/yr range. The £149 figure is derived from the regulator's cost model, not from anyone's observed purchase. Gate: a live checkout on the free calculator must convert before any further build.

The service was rebuilt Jun–Dec 2024 specifically because the original was 'hard to maintain and update', the codebase is now extensible, and the Bill forces another change cycle. No upload route or API today (verified: /api, /report/upload, /account/sign-in all 404), but an import would delete the keying-convenience third of the value. Mitigation is to keep keying convenience under a third of the value; watch the DBT digital blog and the publish service weekly.
- SAGE OR MICROSOFT CLOSES THE VALUE-SUMS GAP IN ONE LOCALISATION RELEASE. Both are actively maintaining (X3 page 28 Jul 2026, D365 12 May 2026). If either adds the sum totals per band and the sum not paid within terms, the residual product is pack assembly, lineage and the directors'-report block. D365 shops are already partly pre-empted (per-vendor/per-payment tabs, receipt-date parameter) and should be the last segment targeted, not the first.
- THE RETENTION DIFFERENTIATOR HAS A LEGISLATED DEATH DATE. The Commercial Payments Bill voids new and pre-existing construction retention clauses after a two-year transition. The £249 construction tier and the SI 2025/75 block have a horizon around 2028–29; the offsetting new interest-paid/interest-owed fields are not yet specified in secondary legislation, so the replacement revenue is promised, not scheduled.
- SPEC AMBIGUITY THE PRODUCT MUST RESOLVE AND DISCLOSE, NOT SILENTLY DECIDE. SI 2025/75 Sch 2 para 10(1) requires a percentage 'of the difference between' sums A and B while para 10(2) gives the formula sum A / sum B x 100 (same conflict in para 11); DBT guidance resolves to the ratio. Similarly, SI 2025/1152 reg 1(4) applies the whole of Sch 7 Part 9 from FYs beginning 1 Jan 2026 while DBT guidance phrases it as elements 4 and 5 only. Every such divergence must be shown on the face of the pack with both readings named — this is reg 9 territory and the exact place an unattended engine could accidentally arbitrate.
- GARBAGE IN ON THE RECEIPT DATE. SI 2024/444 para 14 makes a payment 'made' when received by the supplier, not when the AP run clears, and guidance says invoice receipt is not the software posting date — yet D365 defaults Receive document date to invoice date. The average-days statistic is wrong no matter how good the arithmetic if the field is wrong. The substitute field used must be named on the pack and in the workbook every time, never quietly substituted.
- THE INPUT IS NOT ALWAYS ONE CSV. Retention sum B is receivables-side (company vs its clients) and absent from any purchase ledger for the ~13% of filers with qualifying construction contracts; the SI 2025/1152 block is group-consolidated and full-year. The uploader and the marketing must both stop claiming one-CSV universality, which narrows the frictionless case to the plain-vanilla non-construction single entity.
- SEASONAL CASH AND SEASONAL CHURN. With 63.1% of filings in January and July, revenue arrives in two bursts and there is no monthly relationship to retain a customer between them; the persisted settings and lineage archive are the only retention mechanism and they are unproven as such.
- TAM IS SMALL, FLAT, AND STEPS DOWN. ~6,340 distinct filers in 2026 vs 7,538 in 2019, only ~297–450 genuinely new filers a year, and ~6,000 companies economy-wide reclassify as medium under the April 2025 uplift with drop-out landing from 2027 (two-balance-sheet test). Model the ceiling at ~£1.9m/yr gross before committing build time.
- TWO CARD STATISTICS ARE DEAD AND MUST NOT SURVIVE INTO MARKETING. The '696 blank value fields' signal (all nil returns) and the '3,762 group structures' claim (3,492 filed exactly two, ordinary cadence) are refuted by the corpus itself. The 27.2% figure must be restated as 'filed more than 30 days after period end' (30.2% of companies filed at least one such report), never as a measured non-compliance rate, and the PPN 018 register-gating sentence must be struck entirely — the PPN text does not reference the register.

Any register-derived number used in a public benchmark page must carry a stated derivation and definition, or the SEO surface itself becomes an accuracy liability.

If DBT prefills last period's tick-box and narrative answers, a meaningful slice of the 'persisted answers' value evaporates on day one and cannot be discovered without a real filer's credentials.
