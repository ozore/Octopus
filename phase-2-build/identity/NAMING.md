## 2. Why the shortlist's top three were struck

The brief's stated disqualification rule: *collisions in the Amazon-seller-services niche are disqualifying; a taken exact-match .com is a minus but survivable.* Independent verification found live collisions the source research missed on **all three** of its recommended names. This is the same failure mode that killed "Reinstate" (D2 / R1: reinstate.io has traded since 2019), so it is applied consistently rather than negotiated.

### 2.1 Casewright — STRUCK (was ranked #1)

**Finding: `Casewright Consulting, LLC` is a live US business trading under the exact wordmark.** A Pennsylvania LLC providing litigation support to law firms nationwide — intake, litigation, and trial services, with a published pricing page and a © 2025 notice — operating at **getcasewright.com** ([getcasewright.com](https://www.getcasewright.com/home)).

Why this is fatal rather than merely inconvenient:

1. **It repeats the D2 error in a different lexical field.** Dunford's Step 6 (*Obviously Awesome*, 2019) holds that you cannot own a market category under a name a live competitor already trades under. The dossier killed "Reinstate" for precisely this and made renaming a **BLOCKING** risk (R1). Adopting a name already worn by an active US services firm would reproduce the defect we are spending Day 0 to remove.
2. **It takes the natural domain pattern.** `getcasewright.com` is the exact `get`-prefix convention the category uses (cf. `getappealdesk.com`) and it is held by the collision.
3. **The in-class near-mark family is dense and directly on point.** Beyond the CaseWorthy / Caseware / CaseWORTH / CaseStack cluster already noted in the source research, verification surfaced **CASEWIZARD** and **CASEAWARE** — both registered marks for *"computer software for legal case management"* ([CaseWizard](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/casewizard-com-inc-5085440), [CaseAware](https://trademark.justia.com/786/23/caseaware-78623638.html)). That is Class 9, the class we must file in, for goods described almost exactly as ours.
4. **It aggravates R9 (UPL exposure).** A litigation-support firm is the single worst adjacent business to be confused with, given B11's unauthorized-practice-of-law control.

Not an Amazon-seller-niche collision, so not disqualified by the letter of the rule — but disqualified by the rule's purpose, and by (3), which is an in-class trademark problem on its own.

### 2.2 Standfast — STRUCK (was ranked #2)

**Finding: `getstandfast.com` is a live, in-category AI startup named Standfast.** It is an early-access AI assistant for operators and managers that reads across email, calendar, project trackers and CRMs, then **drafts replies and recommendations for user approval but never sends anything itself**, under the explicit banner *"counsel, not autopilot"* ([getstandfast.com](https://www.getstandfast.com/)).

This is a worse collision than Casewright's, because it collides on **name, class, positioning and era simultaneously**:

- Same wordmark.
- Same class (AI/SaaS software).
- **Same positioning thesis.** "Drafts and surfaces; every send is your tap" is a restatement of our own copilot-not-agent stance (D1, D9) and of Anthropic's workflow/agent distinction in [*Building Effective Agents*](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents). Two products cannot own "human-approved AI drafting" under one name.
- Same launch window (early access, recruiting founding users, 2026).

The source research checked "Standfast" against packaging, whisky, textiles and nitrogen gas springs, and concluded the software class was clear. It was not; the collision simply had not been indexed under the bare word. Plus `standfast.com` is definitively gone (StandFast Group, corrugated packaging, founded 1967, [standfastgroup.com](https://www.standfastgroup.com/)) and `standfast.io` is parked.

### 2.3 Standwell — STRUCK (was the "strong dark horse")

**Finding: `usestandwell.com` is an active Amazon-seller compliance service named Standwell.** It sells compliant review programs to e-commerce brands — *"verified reviews, no incentives, no review pods, and a program built to follow marketplace rules"* — referencing ASINs, selling on **long-term account health**, © 2026, with a named founder ([usestandwell.com](https://usestandwell.com/about/)).

This is a **direct hit inside the Amazon-seller-services niche**, and therefore disqualifying under the brief's own standard without further argument. It is made worse by the fact that "long-term account health" is *our* Shield pitch (§6.2), sold to *our* buyer.

Compounding: `standwell.io` serves a page titled *"Standwell — in active development"*; `getstandwell.com` is a live Shopify storefront; `standwell.com` redirects to a registrar lander; and there is also StandWell bicycle components (Taiwan, est. 2003, [standwell.com.tw](https://standwell.com.tw/)), Standwell Structures (construction), and standwell.org. The word is thoroughly occupied.

## 3. Why Clausewright wins

Clausewright is the last name standing on clearance — but it is not a default. It is the strongest candidate on the two axes the dossier itself identifies as decisive.

### 3.1 It is the only candidate that attacks the binding constraint directly

Per Hormozi's value equation (*$100M Offers*, 2021), scored in IDEA_DOSSIER §6.1, three of four terms already sit at 8–9. **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement scores 3/10 and is the binding constraint**, which is why D7 assigns it the entire offer budget.

Every defense-register name on the shortlist — Standfast, Holdfast, Backguard, Sellshield, Warden — makes a *claim* about likelihood ("we protect you"). Claims are exactly what this category is saturated with: unaudited 85–93% success rates that the dossier refuses to imitate (N10, R11). A claim cannot move a 3/10.

Clausewright names a **demonstration** instead. §7.1 establishes that the differentiator must be visible **before the paywall** or the primary experiment (A4) is confounded; the cited clause is the one piece of proof a panicking seller can verify with their own eyes, for free, before paying anything. Naming the company after the only falsifiable, on-screen, pre-paywall proof we possess is the highest-leverage naming decision available.

It is also the one asset we genuinely hold on day one. §1.1 is explicit that we do not market assets we do not hold — which is why the corpus claim was demoted (§5.5). Citations ship in v1; the corpus does not.

### 3.2 It is Dunford Step 8 compiled into a wordmark

Dunford's Step 8 is to layer a relevant market trend onto positioning. §2.3 identifies that trend as **buyer preference for cited, verifiable AI over black-box completion**, noting that "AI-powered" is worthless because every competitor claims it. Clausewright makes the trend and the brand inseparable — a competitor cannot describe our differentiator without saying our name.

The architectural warrant is Lewis et al. 2020 (NeurIPS), which established that retrieval-augmented generation produces *"more specific, diverse and factual language than a state-of-the-art parametric-only seq2seq baseline"* ([arXiv:2005.11401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)). Factuality is the axis we sell; the name is that axis.

### 3.3 The name is enforced by a test, not by copywriting

B4 is a **code-level invariant**: *the UI renders a policy reference only if it originated in a citation object*, implemented against Anthropic's [Citations API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) (`cited_text` with source locations) and enforced by a CI test on Day 2 (§7.6). R4 restates it: *"Not a prompt instruction."*

This makes Clausewright the rare brand promise that cannot silently rot — the invariant fails the build before it fails a customer. Per Karpathy's [*Software 2.0*](https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35), the artifact that defines desirable behaviour is the thing worth branding; here the brand claim is compiled into the product rather than asserted around it. This is also the Twelve-Factor discipline of making guarantees structural rather than procedural — the promise lives in the codebase and its test suite, not in a runbook or a marketing brief.

### 3.4 "-wright" does the D3 and D9 work

The `-wright` suffix (shipwright, playwright, wheelwright) denotes a **maker working to a craft standard**. It encodes human judgment, which is the axis D3 uses to exit the "AI POA generator" category where AppealDesk already owns cheap-and-fast at $97 (§5.2 — the single most important competitive finding).

It is also *architecturally honest*. Anthropic's [*Building Effective Agents*](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) distinguishes workflows ("predefined code paths") from agents that "dynamically direct their own processes," and counsels finding "the simplest solution possible." D9 commits us to a fixed classify → retrieve → draft → critique workflow with a human escalation tier (B8) — **not** an autonomous agent (N7). Any `-AI`, `-GPT`, `Auto-`, `Agent-` or `-Bot` name would market more autonomy than the architecture has. "Wright" markets a craftsman with a process, which is what we actually built.

Per Nielsen's usability heuristic #2 (match between system and the real world), "clause" is also the buyer-facing word from our own one-liner — *"cites the exact policy clause you were charged under"* (§1.1) — rather than Amazon's internal "POA" jargon.

### 3.5 It supports the premium price

Ramanujam & Tacke (*Monetizing Innovation*, 2016) name **minivation** — undercutting into an undifferentiated position — as the failure mode D4 exists to avoid, and §6.2 is explicit that the entire copilot positioning is void if we read as a cheaper AppealDesk. A coined craft compound reads premium and carries the $149-over-$97 delta; a plain English defense word does not.

Per Apple's Human Interface Guidelines (Liquid Glass) app-naming guidance, the wordmark must survive at icon and label sizes. `clausewright` is a single unbroken token with no punctuation, spacing or case ambiguity — it renders as one unit and truncates predictably.

## 4. The tagline

> **"Every day dark costs you a day's sales. Get back to selling — with the exact policy clause on your side."**

Two clauses, each doing one job:

**Clause 1 — the dream outcome, in cash.** §1.3 is unambiguous: *"Sell the recovered cash flow, not the document."* An AppealDesk tester reported losing **$800/day** while suspended; Hormozi scores Dream Outcome at 9/10 here precisely because *the buyer performs the arithmetic unprompted*. The line therefore states the rate and lets the seller multiply it themselves. Hormozi also warns against *manufactured* urgency — this urgency is real and externally imposed, so it is stated, never invented, and no countdown is fabricated.

"Dark" is the buyer's own word: §1.2 renders the JTBD circumstance as *"my account went dark this morning."* Nielsen heuristic #2 again — speak the user's language.

**Clause 2 — perceived likelihood, the binding constraint.** Per D7, the whole budget goes here, and per §6.1 the top-priority lever is *"show the retrieved policy clause verbatim with its source — no competitor surfaces this today."* The tagline promises exactly what B4 enforces.

**What the tagline deliberately does not say:** no "POA," no "document," no "draft," no "AI," no "instantly," no success rate. Naming the artifact would re-enter AppealDesk's category (D3); a success rate would breach N10/R11 until B9 produces one with a stated *n*.

**Compliance constraint (B11 / R9):** the tagline says **"policy clause,"** never "legal clause." This wording is binding on all downstream copy — see §5.

## 5. Naming invariants — binding on all downstream copy

Derived from B11 (UPL control) and R9 (*"drafting a document that argues a party's case sits near the line"*). These are constraints on the brand, not stylistic preferences, and exist because the chosen name sits closer to a legal register than the alternatives did.

1. **Never pair the name with a professional-advisor title.** Prohibited: *counsel, advocate, attorney, lawyer, legal, litigation, represent, case law, our clients' cases.* Clausewright is a **maker**, never an adviser.
2. **Always "policy clause," never "legal clause."** Amazon and Walmart policy terms are contract terms, not law.
3. **"Not legal advice" stays prominent** on every surface that renders a draft (B11).
4. **Never claim autonomy.** Prohibited: *agent, autopilot, automatic submission, we file for you, we log in.* Contradicts D9, N2, N3, N11.
5. **Never publish a success rate** until B9 yields one with its denominator and methodology (N10, R11).
6. **Never use an `Appeal*` or `Seller*` construction** in product, tier or campaign names — those lexical fields are owned (AppealDesk, AppealDraft, AppealAI, PlatformAppeal, The Appeal Guru, Appeal Wizard; SellerCandy, SellerSonar, Seller Interactive, SellerApp, SellerLabs, SellerAppeal). This is why **Appealwright** was struck (§6.2).
7. **Brand the proof, not the feature.** Present citations as the *visible surface* of the outcome-feedback loop (D10, Process Power), so that when a competitor adds citations (R7) we already own both the word and the deeper claim beneath it.
